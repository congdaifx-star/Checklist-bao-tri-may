Troubleshooting Automated System Diagnostics (ASD) Test Results

This topic includes information about how to troubleshoot the results of automated system diagnostics (ASD) tests.

Automated System Diagnostics (ASD) Tests

See the table below for details/descriptions on each of the available diagnostic tests/service assays:
Test What it Does Performing the Test
Low Volume Reagent Pipetting (ASD) Verifies performance of the reagent pipettors (and pumps) and alignments to pipetting locations (reagent nests, wash towers, reagent carriages). Performing the Low Volume Reagent Pipetting ASD Test
Luminometer Linearity (ASD) The LED inside the luminometer is illuminated to a variety of voltages, and the slope of the voltage versus the sensor response is determined. Performing the Luminometer Linearity ASD Test
Precision Sample Pipetting (ASD) Verifies performance of the precision sample pipettor and alignments to the reaction build carriages, as well as dilution capabilities of the reagent pipettors. Performing the Precision Sample Pipetting ASD Test
Substrate Blank (ASD) Verifies performance of the substrate and luminometer systems, including substrate dispense volume, signal strength (contamination, etc.), and luminometer reading. Performing the Substrate Blank ASD Test
System Carryover (ASD) Verifies proper washing of the reagent probes (sufficient removal of hot sample/reagent from the probes from one sample to the next). Performing the System Carryover ASD Test
Wash Efficiency (ASD) Verifies proper washing of the reaction vessels at the wash wheel; including wash buffer dispense, mixing, and aspiration. Performing the Wash Efficiency ASD Test

General Troubleshooting for All ASD Test Failures

If an ASD test fails, before you re-run the test, be sure to consider the following possible causes for the failure.

Possible Cause Countermeasure
Known bug or behavior Review known bugs and behaviors and common failure troubleshooting topics for the software version. See the Troubleshooting section in the Software chapter for more information.
Bad sample, sample container, or dead volume Use a known good sample and sample container, and use the proper amount of dead volume necessary for the type of tube or cup.
Bad reagent pack(s) Remove all reagent packs associated with the bad result from the instrument and load new reagent packs.
Reflex test configuration Be sure that reflex testing has been configured correctly

After you have eliminated the possible causes listed in the table above, if the ASD test continues to fail:

  1. Perform the Luminometer Linearity ASD Test, and wait until the test passes. See Performing the Luminometer Linearity ASD Test for more information.
  2. Perform the failed test again. If the test fails, troubleshoot the specific test. See Troubleshooting Specific Tests and Failure Modes for more information.
  3. If the test continues to fail, troubleshoot the assay(s). See Troubleshooting Assays for more information.

Diagnostic Test Failure Mode Areas to Focus
Low Volume Reagent Pipetting (ASD) Precision: %CV failure for an individual pipettor

  • RP alignment to RBC (x, y and z)
  • Check fluid lines of RP from front to back (click n seals into RP, manifold, seal at syringes, valves, etc.)
  • Check syringe assembly is tight and properly seated
Accuracy: Median RLU outside threshold
  • Possible syringe pump issue - check syringe assembly is tight and properly seated

  • Contaminated ASD pack if results for all pipettors are failing
Substrate Blanks (ASD) Accuracy: RLU <2500

  • Check for dark count flags, potentially loose wires into lumi
  • Substrate contamination in substrate line/probe (RLU values of initial reps are often elevated and then trend down as more reps are run, but not always). Contamination may be fixed by repeated primes of probe.
  • Air in substrate line if not primed properly
  • Check for leaks in substrate line
  • Not as common but check Wash Wheel (WW) alignment and clean substrate dispense collet. If alignment is off or collet was contaminated by a hot sample it could be contaminating future test results

Accuracy: RLU >6000
  • Substrate contamination
  • Perform the substrate priming dry procedure. See Decontamination and Priming Dry for more information.
  • Load fresh substrate and prime the instrument. See Priming an Empty Substrate System for more information.
Precision: CV >5%
  • Air in substrate lines

See Priming an Empty Substrate System.

Precision Sample Pipetting (ASD) Accuracy: Median RLU outside threshold (6.7 million - 11.2 million RLU)
  • Prime all reagent pipettors 10 cycles.
  • Alignment of precision sample pipettor to build carriage.
  • Precision pipettor mandrel to pump tubing leak/disconnection/kink
  • Mandrel damage/obstruction
  • RLU signal low due to low reagent delivery upstream of Precision Aspiration and Dispense à Reagent Pipetting failure modes of fluidics, ASD pack, alignments, etc
  • RLU signal low due to substrate delivery issues downstream of Precision Aspiration and Dispense à Substrate failure modes for fluidics, substrate bottle, etc.

Precision Sample Pipetting Dilution Sequence

During the Precision Sample Pipetting Automated System Diagnostic (ASD), the sequence of the dilution reagent pipettor involves performing a dilution using the reagent pipettor located "to the left" of the Reaction Build Carriage (RBC) listed in the ASD. Specifically, RBC 1 performs dilution at RP4, RBC 2 performs dilution at RP1, RBC 3 performs dilution at RP2, and RBC 4 performs dilution at RP3. This means that the reagent pipettor to the left of each RBC is utilized for the dilution process.

The two pairs of pipettors utilized together during this dilution process are:
  • RBC 1 with RP4
  • RBC 2 with RP1
  • RBC 3 with RP2
  • RBC 4 with RP3
Imprecision (%CV): CV exceeds 8%
  • Prime all reagent pipettors 10 cycles.
  • Precision sample pipettor mandrel to pump tubing leak/disconnection/kink
  • Mandrel damage/obstruction
  • Precision sample pipettor to RBC alignments
  • Alignment of precision sample pipettor to Build carriage then alignment of Reagent pipettor to build carriage
  • Alignment of precision sample pipettor to Reagent Build Carriage (RBC) is the top cause. Check pipettor of any test result dropouts, if all dropouts are on one RBC then that needs to be realigned. Can also just check PP alignment to RBC in prototype alignment as Z alignment should be within a few steps on all RBC. Do not press down when aligning PP to RBC
  • Check for any fatal pressure flags on test results. Could be a leak in the PP causing bad results. Then troubleshoot PP lines (particular attention to probe tip wear or clear acrylic manifold)
  • RLU signal high CVs due to luminometer issues downstream of Precision Aspiration and Dispense à Luminometer failure modes for high dark counts (spill in luminometer, linearity/drift not passing, etc)
Note: All Pipettors must be enabled in system configuration.

Precision Sample Pipetting Dilution Sequence

During the Precision Sample Pipetting Automated System Diagnostic (ASD), the sequence of the dilution reagent pipettor involves performing a dilution using the reagent pipettor located "to the left" of the Reaction Build Carriage (RBC) listed in the ASD. Specifically, RBC 1 performs dilution at RP4, RBC 2 performs dilution at RP1, RBC 3 performs dilution at RP2, and RBC 4 performs dilution at RP3. This means that the reagent pipettor to the left of each RBC is utilized for the dilution process.

The two pairs of pipettors utilized together during this dilution process are:
  • RBC 1 with RP4
  • RBC 2 with RP1
  • RBC 3 with RP2
  • RBC 4 with RP3
System Carryover (ASD) Accuracy (RLU) Mean Carryover Difference: > 4,500 RLU NOTE: "Mean Carryover Difference" is defined as: [mean of last 5 cold reps] - [mean of first 5 cold reps]
  • RP alignment to wash tower misaligned is most common (RP should not deflect when going down into alignment point, this is common error)
  • Fluid/vacuum lines to RP wash tower obstruction or not connected (check for PM flags)
  • Damaged wash tower
  • Wash tower O-ring not installed properly
  • Bad vacuum pump
  • Prime reagent pipettors 10x
Accuracy (RLU) 1st cold replicate after hot: > 15,000 RLU
  • Wash tower O-ring or alignment of Probe to wash tower
Wash Efficiency (ASD) Accuracy: Mean RLU > 100,000
  • Wash wheel alignments
  • Potentially contaminated spin mix collets
  • Wash dispense/aspirate not functioning properly. Check all the lines (could see through PM flags too)

Known Issue for Low Volume Precision Pipetting ASD Test

There are key differences between the newer Precision Sample Pipetting ASD test and the older Low Volume Precision Pipetting ASD test.
  • The Precision Sample Pipetting ASD test requires system software version 1.17 or newer. The test verifies that the precision sample pipettor is correctly aligned to the reaction build carriages, assesses the dilution capabilities of the reagent pipettors, and tests whether the precision sample pipettor can dispense defined sample volumes with precision. See Performing the Precision Sample Pipetting ASD Test for more information.
  • The Low Volume Precision Pipetting ASD test requires system software version 1.14 through 1.16.2. The test measures the precision and accuracy of reagent pipettors by focusing on their performance and alignments to pipetting locations such as reagent nests, wash towers, and reagent carriages. The test requires having fluid in the sample container, and a known issue in system software version 1.16.2 affects pass/fail results.

Overall, the newer Precision Sample Pipetting ASD test, which is available in system software version 1.17 or newer, provides a more comprehensive assessment of pipetting precision and dilution capabilities compared to the older Low Volume Precision Pipetting ASD test.
