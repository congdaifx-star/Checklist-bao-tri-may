Operating Principles for the Reagent Storage Module (RSM)

This topic includes information about the theory of operation, or operating principles, for the reagent storage module (RSM).

Function

The reagent storage module accepts reagent packs from the user, stores them in a cooled environment, presents them to the reagent pipettors, and discards to the solid waste or returns them to the user. Reagent storage is maintained at a cold ambient temperature between 4-10C, setpoint 6.5C.

Location

Reagent storage is located at the front center of the instrument, beneath the reagent pipettors and substrate module.

1 Reagent storage module (RSM)

Operational Overview

The reagent storage module operation includes reagent pack loading, use, and removal.

Operation Description
Loading Reagent Packs
  1. The user places up to four reagent packs on the load tray and closes the door.
  2. The reagent load tray moves into the RSM, and the barcode reader scans the packs as they move past its field of view.
  3. The reagent gripper moves to the load tray and grasps a pack.
  4. The gripper moves to the first open reagent storage nest and places the pack into the nest.
Using Reagent Packs for Pipetting
  1. The reagent gripper moves to the nest and grasps the reagent pack.
  2. The gripper moves to a reagent pipetting nest and places the pack into the nest.
  3. After pipetting is complete, the gripper returns to the pipetting nest and grasps the pack.
  4. The gripper moves to an open reagent storage nest and places the pack into the nest.
Discarding Expired or Depleted Reagent Packs
  1. The reagent gripper moves to the reagent storage nest and grasps the reagent pack.
  2. The gripper moves to the waste chute and releases the pack into the opened waste chute door to deposit the pack into solid waste.
Unloading Reagent Packs
  1. The reagent gripper moves to the reagent storage nest and grasps the reagent pack.
  2. The gripper moves to the reagent load tray and places the pack onto the tray.
  3. The tray moves out and the door unlocks to make the pack available to the user.

Regent Gripper / Reagent Storage Features and Functions

Feature Function
On Demand Pack Transfers The user may manually command the gripper to perform a pack transfer between any two desired nest locations as part of diagnostics.
Daily Maintenance The gripper jaws are exercised (opened and closed) five times during a daily maintenance routine with position verification to ensure proper operation.
Gripper Error Recovery If a X or Z movement is out of tolerance at the end of a move, the gripper will attempt to put the pack back into its original location. If the second attempt fails, the RSM will be disabled.
Gripper Timing Calibration A timing calibration function captures the total transfer time transferring a pack from a pipettor nest to a storage nest and back to the pipettor nest. This transfer timing is completed for each pipettor nest and to and from all 50 storage nests. The output of this calibration is a data table with real-world transfer times that the software then uses for pack transfer scheduling. The actual transfer timing may differ from the theoretical timing because the gripper open-and-close operation does not use a static delay before checking jaw position sensor states. Instead, it polls the sensors continuously until the expected state is confirmed. The gripper open-and-close times are a function of pneumatic pressure and gripper condition and cannot be accurately predicted by the software.
Interlocks

Both the front door and the load tray have interlock functionality.

At the front door, the RSI/TSI board detects the presence of the front door via a magnetic reed switch. The software can read the door status bit to determine whether the door has been opened. When the door is open, the software ceases to call gripper motor moves. The motor current is maintained to prevent motor slippage. When RSI/TSI power is removed, the RSI/TSI board still monitors the door sensor and logs when it has been opened, in both dwell and sleep modes.

At the load tray, the RSM dMCC detects when the door has been opened or closed via a magnetic reed switch. When the load tray door has been opened, circuitry on the RSI/TSI board disables the load tray motor current. The software can interrogate the RSM dMCC to determine whether the load tray door has been opened. This is considered a workflow interrupt.

Encoder Backup

The RSI/TSI board contains circuitry that allows it to store the encoder values for more than four days. (A sample size of one lasted six days). When the RSI/TSI board detects that power has been removed, it enters a dwell mode.

In dwell mode, it monitors both X and Z encoders for movement every five seconds for up to 30 seconds. If there is no movement between two readings taken five seconds apart, it exits dwell mode and enters into sleep mode. If movement is detected between each reading for 30 seconds, it exits dwell mode with an error, the encoder values are invalidated, and it enters sleep mode.

In sleep mode, it monitors the front door sensor and voltage levels every five minutes until backup power has been depleted. If the front door is opened, encoder values are invalidated.

Magnetic Gripper Sensor Interference The gripper sensors are somewhat prone to magnetic interference from the RSM Z motor. If the gripper sensors are not set correctly from the manufacturer, performance issues may arise. As a result, the gripper sensor nominal positions were changed so that the effects of the magnetic field are minimized. It was measured that this magnetic field's strength when the gripper is near nest 50 is ~2 Gauss. This can result in a shift of the "closed" gripper sensor of .005". The magnetic field diminishes quickly as the gripper moves away from the bottom right corner.
