Replacing the Aspirate Arm

This topic includes information about how to replace the aspirate arm for the wash wheel.

Safety Reminder

Servicing the DxI 9000 instrument involves potential risks for pinch and puncture, electrical shock, and exposure to hazardous materials. You must mitigate these risks by taking appropriate countermeasures. See Safety for more information.

Recommended Tools

The following tools are recommended for replacement of the components listed in this topic:
  • 2.0mm Hex driver
  • 2.5mm Hex driver
  • 3.0mm Hex driver
  • 5.0mm Hex driver

Removing the Aspirate Arm

Perform this procedure to remove the Aspirate Arm.

  1. Remove the D1, D2, and QS Dispense probes to allow room to maneuver.
  2. Gently remove the Substrate Probe.
    1. Disconnect the fluidics (click and seal).
    2. Unscrew the (2) screws at the base of the Substrate Probe that secure it to the Dispense Plate and gently lift up and out.
      Note: Use caution when lifting the Substrate Probe because there is little room to maneuver.
  3. Remove Aspirate Probes A1, A2, and A3
    1. Disconnect the fluidics (click and seal).
    2. Gently pull the probe out.
    3. Repeat for each Aspirate Probe.
  4. Remove the motor to the Dispense Arm (right motor)
    1. Loosen the tension of the motor belt.
      1. Loosen, but don't remove, the (2) 2.5mm hex driver belt tensioner screws located directly beneath the top pulley of the belt. This will loosen the belt so it can be removed.
      2. Push the tensioner down a little to loosen the belt.
      3. Tighten the tensioner screws so the tensioner allows the belt to stay loose enough to remove it from the pulley.
      4. Remove the belt from the bottom pulley of the motor.
    1. Unscrew the (4) 3mm hex driver screws holding the motor to the Arm Support.
    2. Disconnect the electrical connectors to both the Encoder and the Motor.
    3. Pull the motor out.
  5. Disconnect the Aspirate Arm from the Aspirate Rail
    1. Unscrew the (4) 2.5mm hex driver screws from the Aspirate Arm to disconnect it from the Aspirate Linear Rail .
      Be careful not to drop the screws inside the instrument.
      Tip: Put a small piece of PARAFILM on the tip of the wrench to help hold the screw more tightly to the hex wrench.
  6. Remove the Movement Limiter (Arm Spacer)
    1. Unscrew using a wrench.
    2. Remove the Movement Limiter and put it in a safe place.
  7. Remove the Aspirate Counter Balance Spring.
    1. Lift the Aspirate Arm a little bit to provide some slack.
    2. Remove the spring from the lower pin.
    Note: Be careful not to overextend the spring as this will cause it to bend.
    Note: You must remove the Aspirate Counter Balance Spring to get to the Home Sensor.
  8. Lift the Aspirate Arm straight up to remove it.

Installing the Aspirate Arm

Perform this procedure to install the aspirate arm.

  1. Place the Aspirate Arm into position on the Wash Wheel.
    Note: Be sure the pin to attach the Aspirate Counter Balance Spring is in it's proper place in the Arm Support.
  2. Attach the Aspirate Counter Balance Spring to the pin.
  3. Gently lower the Aspirate arm to take up the slack in the spring.
  4. Re-mount the Movement Limiter and use a wrench to screw it in.
    Note: Do not over-tighten the Movement Limiter.
  5. Connect the Aspirate Arm to the Aspirate Rail
    1. Screw the (4) 2.5mm hex driver screws to the Aspirate Arm to connect it from the Aspirate Linear Rail .
      Be careful not to drop the screws inside the instrument.
      Tip: Put a small piece of PARAFILM on the tip of the wrench to help hold the screw more tightly to the hex wrench.
  6. Mount the motor to the Dispense Arm (right motor).
    1. Put the motor into place.
    2. Screw the (4) 3mm hex driver screws to hold the motor on the Arm Support.
    3. Connect the electrical connectors to both the Encoder and the Motor.
    4. Mount the belt to the bottom pulley of the motor.
    5. Tighten the tension of the motor belt.
      1. Loosen, but don't remove, the (2) 2.5mm hex driver belt tensioner screws located directly beneath the top pulley of the belt. This will cause the belt to tighten to the proper amount of tension.
      2. Tighten the tensioner screws so the tensioner allows the belt to stay on the pulley and it is reasonably tight.

Alignments

Perform necessary alignments and/or adjustments for affected components and subsystems.
