Repairing or Rebuilding the KNF Compressor

This topic includes information about how to remove and disassemble the KNF compressor, how to install replacement parts, and how to reassemble and install the KNF compressor.

Safety Reminder

Servicing the DxI 9000 instrument involves potential risks for pinch and puncture, electrical shock, and exposure to hazardous materials. You must mitigate these risks by taking appropriate countermeasures. See Safety for more information.

Required Materials (for repair):
  • Compressor Repair Kit: Gasket, piston seal, o-ring.

Perform this repair procedure after troubleshooting the compressed air system and determining that the compressor does not provide adequate air pressure. Refer to Testing Pneumatics Lines for Leaks and Troubleshooting Pressure Fault Events documents for troubleshooting.

Reference Graphics:

Perform this cleaning procedure when the KNF compressor has failed internally.

Caution: When handling the internal compressor parts, do not touch the surface with your bare skin. Handle the valve plates, intermediate plate, and head only by their edges.
Note:
  • The best solution to acquiring compressed air is to install the House Air Kit.
  • Compressors, no matter what type or brand, can require periodic maintenance or replacement.
  • When changing the seals, check the valve plates for damage (bent tongues, corrosion).
  1. Remove the compressor
    1. Turn off the instrument. See Turning Off the Instrument for more information.
    2. Remove the left rear lower panel to access the compressor.
      1. Open the syringe pump door and remove the lower left screw (3mm driver) securing the panel to the instrument chassis.
      2. Loosen the three screws (3mm driver) securing the tabs at the top of the panel and slide the panel off of the instrument.
    3. Locate the compressor and disconnect pneumatic tubing from the fitting on the compressor head.
    4. Disconnect the compressor power cable from J5 on the Pneumatic Interface printed circuit board (PCB) and remove the cable from its routing through the chassis.
      Note: Make note of the cable routing for installation.
    5. Remove the three screws (3mm driver) securing the compressor to the instrument chassis and remove the compressor out from the instrument.
  2. Disassemble the compressor.
    Perform these steps carefully because there are many parts in the compressor. All parts must be accounted for.
    1. Grasp the muffler and remove it by rotating it counterclockwise.
    2. Remove the cover plate (four screws).
    3. Remove the ribbed head (four screws), and lift the ribbed head off the housing.
    4. Remove the gasket, valve plates, and intermediate plate from the housing.
      Some of the parts can stick to the ribbed head, and some parts can stick to the compressor.
    5. Remove the black o-ring. Grasp the counterweight, and then rotate the counterweight until the connecting rod is at top center.
    6. Grasp and hold the connecting rod, and then loosen and remove the retainer plate screw.
    7. Remove all the parts that were held in place by the retainer plate screw.
      Caution: Perform these steps carefully because there are many parts in the compressor and all parts must be accounted for. There can be as many as nine washers (shims) that need to be reinstalled.
    8. Remove the piston seal from the retainer plate.
    9. Inspect all the parts, and verify that the parts are clean of all dirt and debris. If they are not clean, clean them.
  3. Inspect all the KNF compressor internal parts, and verify that the parts are clean of all dirt and debris. If they are not clean, clean them.
    Note: Handle the internal parts only by their edges.
    1. Carefully clean and remove all seal particles, using methanol or alcohol and a lint free cloth, from the valve plates, intermediate plate, and all other parts with seal particles.
      Caution: Do not damage the tongue area of the valve plates.
  4. Reassemble the compressor.
    1. Insert the cylinder into the housing (larger OD upward).
    2. Stack the retainer plate, replacement seal, support, and washers (shims), onto the screw in the correct order. Refer to the seal assembly in the drawing for the exact order.
      Caution: The valve plates have a minor curvature to them and must be installed correctly. Lay the valve plates on a flat surface, and examine them to determine which way the plates curve.
      Caution: Perform these steps carefully because there are many parts in the compressor and all parts must be accounted for. There can be as many as nine washers (shims) that need to be reinstalled.
      Caution: Assemble the pump correctly, to prevent improper pump operation.
    3. Insert a Philips screwdriver into the screw, and hold the assembly vertically with the screw pointing upward using the screwdriver.
    4. Rotate the counterweight until the connecting rod is up fully. Grasp the pump with your other hand, and then rotate the housing so that the connecting rod is horizontal. Rotate the seal to the horizontal with the screw-end facing the connecting rod, and then carefully insert the seal (with the screwdriver in the screw) through the cylinder.
    5. Tighten the screw into the connecting rod.
    6. Set the compressor down with the seal area facing upward.
    7. Assemble the remaining parts in the order shown. Stack the parts on top of the cylinder and o-ring. Refer to the assembly drawing. Orientation of parts is critical to correct operation.
      1. Assemble the replacement o-ring.
      2. Assemble the upper valve plate.
      3. Assemble the lower valve plate.
      4. Assemble the replacement gasket.
      5. Assemble the ribbed head. The W-mark must line up with the W-marks on the intermediate plate and housing.
    8. Insert the four screws into the ribbed head, and then carefully tighten the screws.
      Caution: The ribbed head must sit squarely on the cylinder and o-ring. Improper tightening causes the o-ring to leak. Each screw must be tightened only a small amount at a time. Tighten a screw a small amount, and then move to an opposite corner and tighten that screw a small amount.
    9. Install the four screws into the cover plate.
  5. Reinstall the compressor.
    1. Place the compressor with rubber gasket beneath it onto the instrument chassis and secure with three screws (3mm driver).
    2. Route the compressor power cable through the instrument chassis and connect at J5 on the Pneumatic Interface PCB.
    3. Connect the pneumatic tubing to the fitting on the compressor head.
      Caution: Verify that the tubing is not kinked.
    4. Reinstall the left rear lower panel.
      1. Loosen the three screws (3mm driver) for the panel tabs so that there is sufficient space to slide the panel tabs beneath the washers.
      2. Place the panel tabs (bottom inner part of panel) into the chassis slots and pivot the panel into place so that the three tabs slide into place beneath the three screws.
      3. Secure the three screws (3mm driver) to hold the panel tabs in place.
      4. Open the syringe pump door and install the lower left screw (3mm driver) to secure the panel to the instrument chassis.
    5. Turn on the instrument. See Turning On the Instrument for more information.
  6. Run the bulk feeder exerciser.
    1. Initialize the instrument. See Initializing the Instrument for more information.
    2. In the Console App run the Vessel Exerciser, Bulk Feeder, for 100 reps.
    3. Select the SYSTEM RUNNING button and go to Stop > Pause to enter the instrument into a YELLOW (Paused) status.
    4. In the Console App go to Menu > Tools > Diagnostics > Intrusive Diagnostics and select Start Session.
    5. Select Exerciser to open the Exerciser page.
    6. Select RV Feeder.
    7. Type 100 in the Repetition box.
    8. Select Request.
    9. Confirm that you observe no errors for low system pressure.
  7. Complete the verification of service repair. See Verification of Service Repairs for more information.
