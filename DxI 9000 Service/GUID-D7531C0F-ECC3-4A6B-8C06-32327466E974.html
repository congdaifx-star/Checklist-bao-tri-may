Operating Principles for the Sample Presentation Unit (SPU)

This topic includes information about the theory of operation, or operating principles, for the sample presentation unit (SPU) module.

Function

The purpose of this module is to deliver sample containers in sample racks to the pipetting subsystems for sample aliquoting. This is where the user can load sample racks for processing and then remove them when finished.

Location

The SPU is located on the front center exterior of the instrument. The presentation tray extends inward toward the back center of the instrument.

1 Sample presentation unit (SPU) onload/offload
2 Sample presentation unit (SPU) tray

Operational Overview

The following steps describe how the SPU module operates and interacts with the user and the rest of the instrument:

  1. The SPU pusher moves to the home position and unlocks the SPU door/cover. This may happen if the module is idle or if the user selects the Load button to home the pusher, which allows the user to load additional racks.
  2. The user places racks in the SPU onload area and closes the cover.
  3. The SPU presentation carrier moves to the transfer position at the intersection of SPU presentation and SPU onload areas.
  4. The SPU onload pusher pushes racks (seek) until the first rack is detected by the end-of-rail sensor.
  5. When the rack is detected, the onload pusher pushes out of the sensor (seek inactive) and pushes the rack onto the presentation carrier.
  6. The SPU presentation carrier will move the rack to check for the presence or absence of sample containers, to read barcodes, and to identify container types within the sample rack.
  7. Depending on the barcode read and vision output, the rack will either move to the sample pipettors or, if an error scenario exists, will be offloaded.
  8. After the rack has been processed, the presentation carrier will move back to the transfer position.
  9. The next rack or the onload pusher will push the rack into the offload area.
  10. The user opens the door/cover and removes the racks from the offload area. (The offload door is always unlocked.)

Component Locations and Functions

The following illustrations and tables show component locations and their descriptions and functions.

# Component Description and Function
1 Camera Background Provides a clean background to enhance the readability of the visual features of sample rack and sample containers.
2 BCR Camera Reads sample rack barcodes, reads sample container barcodes, detects sample container types, detects the presence or absence of sample container caps.
3 Rack Guide Blocks Biases the sample rack against the tooling datum to improve rack position repeatability.
4 Rack Transfer Carrier Takes the sample rack from the onload section, sends it to the aliquotors, and returns the rack to the offload section.
5 Home Sensor Registers home position for the rack transfer carrier.
6 Motor Moves the rack transfer carrier to each position along the presentation lane.
7 PU Coupler The linkage between the rack transfer carrier and the carriage on the linear rail.
8 Linear Rail Provides a precise one-dimensional motion guide.
9 Belt The timing belt moves the carriage precisely along the linear rail.
10 Belt Tensioner Provides proper belt tension to reduce slack and to improve carriage positional repeatability.

# Component Description and Function
1 Rack Rails Supports and guides the movement of sample racks. The user loads sample racks onto the right-hand section and unloads sample racks from the left-hand section.
2 Rack Gate Separates the onload and offload sections. Prevents sample racks from falling into the transfer location.
3 Rack Pusher Pushes the rack from onload section onto the rack transfer carrier.
4 Motor Moves the rack pusher to complete rack transfer functions.
5 LED/Button Interface Board A printed circuit board (PCB) to branch powers and signals between the dMCC and low level devices.

# Component Description and Function
1 Home Sensor Registers the home position for the rack transfer carrier.
2 Onload Door Hinges Provides a hinge function for the SPU onload door.
3 Onload End of Rail Sensor Detects wait-in-queue racks in the onload section. Determines push-onto-carrier steps.
4 Offload Door Hinges Provides a hinge function for the SPU offload door.
5 Offload Full Sensor Triggers the system to issue an alert when the number of offloaded racks reaches 21.
6 Retain Bracket (mounted on Chassis) Holds the SPU onload and offload unit on the chassis during mounting and removal to free the installer's hands from holding the weight of SPU.
7 Handle Provides a grabbing point when mounting or removing the SPU onload and offload unit from the chassis.
8 Swing Mechanism Linkage Bars Part of a four-bar linkage mechanism to define and constrain the swing open and close position and path.
9 Belt Tensioner Provides proper belt tension to reduce slack and to improve carriage positional repeatability.
10 Belt The timing belt moves the carriage precisely along the linear rail.
11 Linear Rail Provides a precise one-dimensional motion guide.
12 Door Mount Weldment This is part of the SPU structural components. It ties two back rails together to provide structural rigidity, and provides SPU door mount points.
13 Support Leg This leg will rest on a catcher plate mounted on the chassis to support the weight of the SPU.

# Component Description and Function
1 SPU Mount Plate Part of four-bar linkage mechanism that defines and constrains the swing open and close position and path.
2 Swing Lock Strike Assembly Provides a lock point for the locking pin.
3 Swing Lock Sensor Detects whether the swing mechanism is locked.
4 Swing Lock Handle The place where the user grasps and unlocks the SPU to swing open.
5 Swing Lock Locks the onload and offload unit onto the chassis to prevent mis-alignment from the presentation unit.
