Replacing Sample Probe

This procedure describes how to replace the sample probe assembly.

Special notes regarding this procedure:
  • It is not necessary to remove the screws in the retainers that hold the tubing and cable in position. Loosening them reduces the chance of dropping hardware into the instrument.
  • Two of the reference drawings show the front service guide removed. It is not necessary to remove the guide. This was done to provide a clear view of the cable and tube routing.
  • The close proximity of the bulk feeder to the sample pipettor can make this procedure difficult to perform; however, it can be performed without removing either module.
  • The sample probe can be accessed from the front or rear (if the rear covers are removed).

Refer to these drawings while performing this procedure.

Note: The screws to be loosened are shown in green.
  1. Power off the upper switch on the power distribution PCB.
  2. Remove the sample probe.
    1. Move the sample probe over the wash tower.
    2. Disconnect the fitting (A), and allow fluid to drain out of the sample probe.
    3. Manually, move the sample pipettor fully forward and then downward.
    4. Disconnect the electrical connector (at location B), using a needle-nose pliers to grasp it if needed.
      Caution: When pulling on the connector, be careful not to damage it.
    5. Cut the cable-tie on the front guide (C) and the cable-tie on the rear guide, if present (not shown), and remove them.
    6. Loosen, but do not remove, the screw in the retainer (D).
      There should be sufficient clearance for removing the cable and tubing.
    7. Loosen, but do not remove, the screw in the other retainer (G).
      There should be sufficient clearance for removing the cable and tubing.
    8. Loosen the two screws in the retainer plate (E), and then slide the plate off the screws.
    9. Remove the probe, tubing, and cable assembly from the instrument.
  3. Install the sample probe.
    1. Place the body of the sample probe into the mounting slot and rotate it to a position where the fluid tube is toward the front of the instrument.
    2. Lift the probe upward, until the limit ring (F) rests against the bottom of the probe mounting block. Adjust the end of the clear tubing over the cable, so that it is centered in the retainer plate.
    3. Install the retainer plate (E), and tighten the two screws.
    4. Route the tubing and cable between the retainer (G) on the front guide and then up the guide. Attach a cable-tie in the cable-tie slot.
      Caution: Route the tubing into the track closest to the front of the instrument.
    5. Verify that the cable and tubing are seated properly in the guide tracks, and then tighten the screw to secure the retainer (G) into position.
    6. Route the tubing and cable over and down into the tracks in the rear guide and behind the retainer (D).
    7. Verify that some portion of the clear tubing on the cable is retained, and then tighten the screw to secure the retainer (D) into position.
      Caution: Route the tubing into the track closest to the front of the instrument.
    8. Connect the tubing fitting (A) to the connector.
    9. Connect the electrical connector (B) to the RF PCB housing.
    10. Verify that the service loop peak is between 0.5 in and 0.25 in above a horizontal line drawn between the tops of the two guides. Manually move the pipettor up and down to ensure that the service loops are of sufficient height to allow smooth operation.
  4. Align the sample pipettor module.
    Note: Refer to the SP: Aligning Sample Pipettor procedure.
  5. Verify proper operation of the sample pipettor.
    1. For instruments connected to an LAS only: With the sample pipettor carriage in the z-home position, manually move the sample pipettor toward the LAS sampling location. As the pipettor passes through the opening in the rear of the instrument, verify that the tubing and cable service loop are low enough to clear the opening.
    2. Perform a sample pipettor prime of ten cycles. After priming, verify that there are no leaks and that there are no bubbles of significant size in the sample probe tubing loop.
  6. Verify system operation.
    1. Perform system verification as stated in the Verification of Service Repair document .
      Refer to the Admin: Verification of Service Repair document.
