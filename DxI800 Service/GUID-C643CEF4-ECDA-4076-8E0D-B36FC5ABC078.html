Definitions

Adjustment

Physically moving parts, assemblies, or modules to a desired location or position using only mechanical means. Software is not involved in the adjustment.

Example of an adjustment: Loosening the mounting tower to base mounting screws on the sample pipettor and moving the sample pipettor module to a position where the sample probe is in the correct axis with the sample storage module and the SPU, then tightening the mounting screws.

Alignment

When software is involved in positioning a device it is called an alignment. The three types of alignments used on the DxI instrument are:
  • Manual alignment—Uses software to
    • move a device to a location.
    • step the device into a position where it is correctly aligned.
    • saves the offsets into software as the alignment values.
  • Semi-automatic alignment—
    • selects the semi-automatic alignment button which powers off the motor for that device.
    • manually moves the device to the alignment position.
    • selects OK that powers on the motor causing the instrument to move the device to the home position.
    The number of steps moved for the primary, and in some cases secondary, device are counted and become the alignment offset.
  • Automatic alignment—
    • places a tool into or onto the device to be aligned.
    • selects auto-align.
    • moves the device to a general location on the secondary device.
    • moves the device up and down in a predetermined pattern to search for the alignment location (the alignment position is determine by searching in two axes). In some cases, the secondary device also moves during automatic alignment.
    • The software saves the auto-align values found from searching.
    • The aligner removes the alignment tool.
