Replacing Transfer Pump Head

This procedure describes how to replace the pump head on the wash buffer supply transfer pump or the liquid waste transfer pump.

Perform this procedure when the peri-tubing is damaged or the pump head fails.

Note:
  • Pump heads were manufactured previously with a toothed clamping input and output jaw that restricted tube motion (A).
  • Pump heads are manufactured now with a smooth (toothless) non-clamping output jaw (B).
  • The mounting bracket tab for releasing the transfer pump head is in the same location on each pump assembly (C).
  1. Remove the pump head from the transfer pump.
    Note: The transfer pump head comes with a spare mounting plate. The plate can be discarded or used, as needed.
    1. Remove the mounting bracket screws (three 9/64-in screws).
    2. Lift the bracket up, to allow access to the pump heads.
    3. To free the pump head cover from the tubing, open and close the pump head cover.
    4. While holding the pump head in the left hand, push the mounting bracket tab with the right hand.
    5. With the mounting bracket tab depressed, rotate the pump head assembly counter-clockwise 1/8 turn.
    6. Remove the pump head from the mounting plate.
  2. Install the pump head on the transfer pump and replace the tubing.
    1. Note the orientation of the motor drive shaft.
    2. Adjust the pump head drive shaft to be able to engage the motor drive shaft when you begin to re-engage the pump head to the mounting plate. Use the old pump head drive shaft orientation as a guide to align the new pump head drive shaft.
    3. Position the pump head onto the mounting plate.
    4. Once the two shafts engage, the pump head drops into the mounting plate. Rotate the pump head 1/8 turn clockwise until the tabs click indicating the pump head is locked into position.
    5. Install the transfer pump mounting bracket.
    6. Clamp off the system Tygon (clear) tubing, to avoid draining the wash buffer or liquid waste.
      Clamp off the input side when replacing the liquid waste transfer peri tubing, and clamp off the output side when replacing the wash buffer transfer peri tubing.
    7. Remove the old peri tubing.
    8. Cut the new peri tubing to the same length as the old peri tubing.
      The new peri tubing is provided with the transfer pump head.
    9. Install the new peri tubing onto the in-line fittings.
    10. When the peri tubing has been replaced, remove the clamp.
  3. Verify the operation of the pump.
    Warning: Motor auto control for each transfer pump will need to be turned off temporarily (check box cleared) to run either transfer pump. Once you turn off the motor, you must reselect the motor auto control check box before leaving the Peristaltic and Vacuum Pumps screen.
    Warning: Confirm that the tubing route is not reversed (causing fluid to flow in the wrong direction) or twisted (causing tubing to fail prematurely).
    Warning: Confirm that the tubing is not pinched in the pump head jaws and that there is sufficient slack on the input (left) side of the pump head.
    1. From the Main Menu select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Diagnostics > Peristaltic and Vacuum Pumps.
    2. From the Peristaltic and Vacuum Pumps screen, turn off the motor auto-control and turn on the transfer pump motor.
    3. Select OK at the message indicating you are about to turn off the motor auto-control.
    4. Slowly close the pump head cover engaging the tubing with the moving rollers.
    5. Let the motor run for 10 to 15 seconds.
      Note: The wash buffer transfer pump moves buffer into the upper reservoir. The liquid waste transfer pump removes waste fluid from the intermediate waste jar.
    6. Turn off the transfer pump motor.
    7. Turn on the motor auto-ontrol.
    8. Prime all using default settings.
    9. Run System Check.
Additional Information: Original transfer peri pumps had a requirement from the vendor to reseat the tubing after 30 minutes of use. Pumps with a toothless jaw (on the output side) configuration do not require this action.
