Hardware Verification Following Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF)/AS Reported Safety

Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish a standard hardware verification protocol for service personnel and customers following a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) on the DxI instrument

When to Perform

FSEs are to perform hardware verification after a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) is filed. Hardware verification may be waived if the customer admits to poor sample quality or operator error as the root cause of the erroneous result. Verification may also be waived if the customer is willing to run hardware verification including a system check and precision run. If applicable, document in the ServiceMax work order or Oracle service request why service was waived. If service is not waived, then field service must perform system hardware verification as soon as possible.

Background

When a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) is filed, the call center can generate a field service request categorized as "As Reported Safety" (ARS) in ServiceMax or "Type 1 CF" in Oracle, and field service must assess and verify the system hardware using this procedure. As part of the CF investigation, field service makes a reasonable effort to identify root cause during instrument inspection, repair, and verification. Field service documents the root cause of a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) is filed at the lowest level component reasonable to claim, preferably at the individual part number level, or if not impossible at the individual part then at the module level (for example, aspirate probes, spinner grippers, peri-pump tubing, fluidics). If multiple parts are replaced proactively, do not document them as the root cause unless there is clear evidence of malfunction. If the root cause is determined to be operator error, then document that in the service request (for example, operator did not seat aspirate probe correctly). If no cause is found, or the problem could not be reproduced, have the documentation reflect this conclusion.

In some scenarios, the CF event cause might be apparent and easily resolved over the phone, but the customer refuses to perform the necessary verification steps and requests service. CRTS is an acronym used by Call Center meaning the customer refuses to trouble shoot over the phone. In these instances, field service is requested to go on site, or attempt again to troubleshoot with the customer over the phone. Any troubleshooting tasks and resolutions via phone contacts must be documented in ServiceMax or Oracle. If there is a delay in the FSE ETA, the customer may be more willing to assume some of the actions.

Customer Verification

If the erroneous result is from a single sample (for example, a Troponin or BhCG flier) and the customer repeats the sample twice and the repeats are consistent and believable, the customer can verify the system to eliminate hardware as source of error.

For example, an initial Troponin result is 0.10 ng/mL. Repeat results are 0.01 and 0.00 ng/mL. Low-level QC is acceptable and no other assay performance issues are identified.

Verification consists of a system check, passing QC, and a 15 rep per pipettor precision run (of the analyte causing the CF, near the dose level of the sample). Call Center documents this verification and attaches data in the Oracle service request.

In other cases, field service must investigate the CF event and evaluate instrument hardware.

Root Cause Assessment

When performing the initial instrument assessment, it is important to have a clear understanding of the failure, the assay characteristics, and then which diagnostic test to run. Apply logical troubleshooting and critical thinking. To decide assessment actions, refer to the information in the Instrument: Troubleshoot Assay (General) and Assay Characteristics and Troubleshooting Sorter documents. Ask for assistance, if you are unsure how to proceed. Consider the following:

  • Is the CF event for one assay or multiple assays?
  • Is the CF event based on one sample or multiple samples?
  • Review the event log messages near the time of the result.
  • Is the problem high RLUs or low RLUs?
  • Are the patient results false positive, false negative, or general imprecision?
  • What are the assay specific characteristics, use the Assay Characteristics and Troubleshooting Sorter tool:
    • Sandwich or competitive
    • One step or two step
    • Sample volume pickup
    • Dilution assay

Verifying Hardware

Perform these steps to verify hardware performance.
  1. (OPTIONAL) Customer runs system check (as requested by Call Center) before the field service on-site visit or as part of system verification.
  2. (OPTIONAL) Perform the HS System Check procedure.
    Note: Refer to the Tests: HS System Check procedure.

    If HS System Check fails, troubleshoot the test until it passes before proceeding to the next step.

    Note: Refer to the Tests: HS System Check Troubleshooting procedure.
  3. Perform a precision run of 15 reps per pipettor using a control for the test generating the CF. Use a low-level control or similar dose to the sample that generated the CF. Evaluate results against the performance goals outlined in the Defining Performance Goals document in the Access Systems Applications Manual.

Small Volume Sample and Dilution Assays

If the result generating the CF was from an assay with a small volume sample pickup, a dilution assay (Dil-BhCG, Dil-Ferritin, or Dil-AFP), or from an assay that makes an automatic dilution (Myoglobin, Cortisol, AFP, Rubella IgG, etc.), then special consideration should be taken.

Reviewing Maintenance and Sample Handling

  1. Verify customer maintenance.
    • Review maintenance log sheet.
    • Verify the aspirate probes are being cleaned (using clean brushes per the procedure) and replaced.
    • Verify the duck-bill valve is being replaced.
  2. Verify sample handling.
    • Review lab sample tube handling. Refer to Product Launches - Preanalytical Sample Handling in Access Systems Applications database.
    • Review centrifuge spin time and speed according to the tube manufacturer recommendations. Refer to the AccuTnI Support Portfolio - Preanalytical Factors- Centrifugation section in the Applications database.
    • If the system is connected to automation, verify the components are functioning correctly. Check sample tube transfer areas, spin points, etc. Look for signs of splashing in these areas.
    • Review the appearance and quality of the sample.

Completing the Verification

Perform these steps to complete the hardware verification documentation.
  1. Obtain a 30-day diagnostic copy-to-disk file. From the main menu, select Diagnostics > Copy to Disk, select a drive and start date, and then select OK.
    Note: The diagnostics copy-to-disk file contains additional information beyond the normal copy-to-disk from the Test Results screen. Additional information includes results, consumables data, priming profiles, RV map, SV maps, running processes, reagent storage map, configuration settings, network configurations, and free memory.
  2. Attach all the data files to the service request in ServiceMax, Oracle or the CF, or e-mail the documentation to cfcomplaints@beckman.com. Documentation to include:
    • Copy of the event log
      Note: Refer to the Software: Transferring Event Log Files (Verbose and Terse) procedure.
    • HS System Check, preferably the graphed results
    • Precision data (optional)
    • Diagnostics copy-to-disk (.csv file)
