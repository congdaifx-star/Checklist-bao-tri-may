Testing Analytical Module Motor Slippage

This procedure describes how to test motor slippage for the wash carousel, incubator wheel, and probe plate drive motors.

Warning: The photomultiplier tube (PMT) is a very sensitive photon measuring device. Any background or extraneous light coming into contact with a powered PMT can saturate the PMT and reduce the gain. This reduces the performance of the PMT, and it can take a long time for the PMT to recover or it might never recover fully. A magnetic field close to a powered PMT also reduces the performance of the PMT. The magnetic field causes the electrons to follow a curved path instead of moving in a straight line. When you are performing service in the area of the luminometer, observe the following rules.
  • Never shine a flashlight around the luminometer while the PMT is powered on.
  • Never bring a strong magnetic field close to the luminometer while the luminometer is powered on.
  • Before removing the covers on the analytical module, turn off the power to the PMT.
  • Cap the PMT whenever it is removed from the analytical module.

This table lists the acceptable motor slippage for the analytical module drive motors.

Device Allowable Slippage
Incubator Wheel 5 Steps
Wash Wheel 5 Steps
Aspirate Arm 12 Steps
Dispense Arm 12 Steps
  1. Remove the analytical module cover.
  2. Perform these steps to test for motor slippage in any of the analytical module motors.
    Note:
    • Wash carousel motor slippage errors occur most often during the maintenance assay because the carousel reverses direction. Slippage errors during the maintenance assay can indicate a loose motor drive gear.
    • The dispense and aspirate arms move together during operation. The aspirate arm moves down first, followed by the dispense arm. The dispense arm moves up first, followed by the aspirate arm
    1. Exit the User Interface using the steps in the "Exit - Console to Windows Desktop" procedure.
    2. Connect to the AU using Desktop Sharing.
    3. Open the SMC test program by double clicking the SMCTest.exe shortcut in the AU desktop or by selecting Start > All Programs > Windows Explorer > C:\ > AU > SMCTest.exe .
    4. In SMCTest, select the motor for the suspect device, (ie. WashWheel.Motor) and then select Enable.
    5. Enter a numerical value in the text box, then select + to advance the device motor.
    6. Select Home to home the device motor. Repeat homing and advancing in the + and - directions several times and look at the Home Slippage value.
      Note: For maximum allowable slippage, refer to the Allowable Slippage table.
    7. Reinitialize the system by selecting Diagnostics > Initialize System > Start.
    8. If repair is needed, then perform the necessary repair and retest.
  3. Perform these steps if receiving multiple pick and place errors along with dispense arm motion errors.
    Note: Test for wash wheel runout, if you are receiving multiple pick-and-place errors along with dispense arm motion errors.
    1. Select WashWheel.Motor > Enable.
      Note: Confirm that the dispense plate and aspirate plate are raised and clear of the wash carousel.
    2. Select Home until a Home Slippage value of 0 is achieved.
    3. Enter 5800 in the window above the Enable button, and then select the + button to rotate the carousel 180°.
    4. Select Home and note the Home Slippage.
      If the Home Slippage is Then the motor runout is
      within two steps of 0 or 11600,
      acceptable. Repeat the steps several times for confirmation.
      • If the drive gear has been rotated 90° and the Home Slippage is less than 2, then motor runout is acceptable.
      • If the drive gear has not been rotated 90°, then proceed to the next step.
      greater than two steps from 0 or less than two steps from 11600,

      unacceptable. Replace the motor.

    5. Remove the substrate probe from the dispense arm.
    6. Remove the analytical module cover and the light shield.
    7. Select the Disable button, and then manually rotate the carousel to clear the home tab from the home sensor.
    8. Mark the intersection of the wash wheel teeth and the drive gear teeth.
    9. Lift the wash wheel slightly, manually rotate the drive gear 90° relative to the wheel position, and then reseat the wheel.
    10. Repeat the Test for Wash Wheel Runout with the drive gear in this new position.
  4. If the wash wheel motor was replaced, then perform system verification as stated in the Admin: Verification of Service Repair document.
