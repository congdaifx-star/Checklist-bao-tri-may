Troubleshooting Substrate System

Purpose

This procedure provides instructions for troubleshooting issues in the substrate system.

When to Perform

Perform this procedure when troubleshooting:
  • Substrate system software and Event Log messages:
    • Backlash and drawback issues
    • Selector valve out of position
  • System Check RLU issues.
  • Substrate RLU issues.
Note: Substrate temperature troubleshooting information is located in the Fluidics: Troubleshooting Substrate Temperature document

Backlash and Drawback Issues

Backlash or Drawback Event Log Message Message or Flag Explanation Possible Causes Troubleshooting
Excessive substrate pump backlash detected.
  • The pump backlash is greater the suggested maximum backlash tolerance of 260.
  • The details instruct the customer that non-urgent service is required. Contact Customer Tech Support.
  • Substrate pump belt is stretched.
  • Substrate build-up on:
    • Piston
    • Seal
    • Pump housing
  • Substrate valve (bottle selector or pump) is defective.
Note: For detailed instruction for checking the listed possible causes, refer to the Troubleshooting Substrate Fluidics: Pump, Tubing, Fittings, and Detector procedure in the Fluidics: Troubleshooting Substrate Fluidics document.
Large substrate pump backlash detected.
  • The pump backlash was between 220 and 260.
  • This blue informational event informs the customer that no immediate action is required. The Service Engineer should service the pump at the next visit.
Probe drawback has been compromised. The probe will need to be primed.
  • The instrument experienced a hardware failure during the Utility Assay.
  • The sample pipettor can aliquot from sample containers, but the DxI does not transition to the Running mode until the substrate and dispense probes are primed successfully.
Any hardware event that can interrupt the Utility Assay.
Note: This message is not a substrate module failure.
  1. Prime the dispense and substrate probes to enable the system to get to the Running mode.
  2. Search the Event Log for a hardware event that caused the interruption of the Utility Assay. Resolve the root cause of that event.
Substrate probe drawback is not calibrated. Tests cannot be run.
  • The calibration has not been completed successfully.
  • No tests will be run until the substrate probe drawback calibration completes successfully.
  • Substrate drawback calibration failure caused by:
    • Failure of the peri-pump.
    • Failure of the aspirate probe 3 tubing.
    • Failure of substrate bubble detector to detect these substrate delivery issues:
      • Kinked substrate tubing
      • Damaged substrate probe
      • Substrate valve (bottle selector or pump) failures
      • New hardware or software installed
Note: For detailed instruction for checking the listed possible causes, refer to the Troubleshooting Substrate Fluidics: Pump, Tubing, Fittings, and Detector procedure in the Fluidics: Troubleshooting Substrate Fluidics document.

Other Event Log Messages and Issues

Other Event Log Messages Possible Causes Troubleshooting / Servicing
The Bulk Supplies screen in the user interface blinks continuously. Broken sensor wire on substrate loader. Refer to the Fluidics: Troubleshoot Substrate Loader procedure.
The substrate loader door will not stay closed.
  • Broken flag arm on the bottle present sensor.
  • Substrate door open or closed sensors are not operating correctly.
Inspect and test the substrate loader flags and sensors.
Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Troubleshoot Substrate Loader procedure.
The substrate selector valve is out of position. Initializing the instrument with either:
  • Expired substrate loaded.
  • No substrate bottles loaded
Load and scan a new bottle of substrate, and then initialize the instrument.

RLU Issues

RLU Issue Possible Causes Troubleshooting
  • RLUs increased after a substrate bottle change.
  • Assay Imprecision: A shift in QC or a calibration failure.
  • Substrate loader gasket is loose or missing.
  • Substrate loader springs are misplaced or missing.
For detailed instruction for checking the possible causes, refer to the Fluidics: Troubleshoot Substrate Loader procedure.
System Check failure due to... Low substrate RLU
  • Substrate expired.
  • Substrate contaminated (possible with Citranox).
  • Substrate bubble detector detected these substrate delivery issues:
    • Substrate tubing kinked
    • Substrate probe damaged
    • Substrate valve (bottle selector or pump) failed
  • Luminometer failure
  • Cable or connection issue with the cable from the DAQC PCB to:
    • Luminometer
    • LED
  1. Verify proper substrate equilibration and storage. Refer to the "Substrate" document in the "Access Systems Applications" Database.
  2. If the instrument is a new install or the substrate was recently decontaminated, then prime the substrate an additional 30 primes and rerun the System Check.
  3. View the Event Log for signs of a substrate delivery issue. The most likely Event Log Message is Substrate dispense failure: Air in the Fluidics Line or other pump related dispense failure. Refer to the Fluidics: Determining Percent Fluid During Troubleshooting.
  4. Test for substrate contamination and Luminometer issues.
    Note: Refer to Fluidics: Confirming Contamination During Substrate Troubleshooting procedure.
High substrate RLU
  • External light source
  • Spill in analytical module
  • Contaminated substrate
  • Stored DCF is incorrect
  • Luminometer failure
  • Cable or connection issue with the cable from the DAQC PCB to:
    • Luminometer
    • LED
  • DAQC PCB failure
  • Electrical noise in the system or environment resulting in negative feedback to the DAQC PCB
  1. Check for external light leakage. Securely close all internal module covers and instrument covers. Cover any nearby windows that might be allowing excessive light in.
  2. Perform a Dark Count Read.
    Note: Refer to the Tests: Performing Dark Count Read procedure.
  3. Test for substrate contamination and luminometer issues.
    Note: Refer to Fluidics: Confirming Contamination During Substrate Troubleshooting procedure.
  • High substrate CV
  • Assay imprecision:
    • QC out of range
    • Increased QC CVs
    • Calibration failures
    • Non-repeatable patient results
  • Spill in analytical module
  • Substrate bubble detector detected these substrate delivery issues:
    • Substrate tubing kinked
    • Substrate probe damaged
    • Substrate valve (bottle selector or pump) failed
  • Priming failure. The substrate system can require 5 to 15 primes to eliminate any air introduced to the system during troubleshooting.
  • Luminometer ground cable is loose or damaged.
  1. Perform a Dark Count Read.
    Note: Refer to the Tests: Performing Dark Count Read procedure.
  2. Look in the Event Log for signs of a substrate delivery problem. The most likely Event Log Message is Substrate dispense failure: Air in the Fluidics Line or other pump related dispense failure. If this Event Log Message is found, then refer to the Fluidics: Determining Percent Fluid During Troubleshooting.
  3. Verify that all luminometer ground cables are secure.
