DxI Fluidics: Troubleshooting Vacuum and Wash Buffer Systems

Purpose

This document provides instructions for troubleshooting the vacuum and wash buffer systems.

When to Perform

Perform troubleshooting when there are issues in one or more of these areas:
  • Vacuum Pump
  • Buffer Supply Peristaltic Pump
  • Wash Collar
  • Sample or Reagent Wash Towers
  • Fluidics Reservoir
  • Liquid Waste Peristaltic Pump

Pipettor and Wash Tower Errors

Fluidics Event Log Error Possible Causes Troubleshooting
Pipettor failed to wash. Some other issue caused pipettor wash sequence to fail. Search the event log for an accompanying message at about the same time. Refer to the associated event log message.
Examples:
  • Reagent pipettor wash tower vacuum outside limits.
  • Device Reports Error (details list: Device Motor Controller).
Multiple retries were needed to obtain adequate wash tower flow. If the result is below the warning level (even if warnings is disabled) after priming and during unpark, up to 19 peri-flow retries are attempted before the pipettor is disabled. These retries occur at five second intervals.

If this error continues after a prime or reoccurs, then refer to the troubleshooting for Wash Tower external flow conditions below limit error.

Wash tower external flow conditions below limit.
  • Peristaltic pump tubing in wash tower peristaltic pump is worn, flattened, or leaking.
  • Wash buffer delivery failures:
    • New Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor
      • Broken cap or tube in wash buffer container.
      • Broken or loose quick -connect or disconnect at the Bulk Fluid Tray Interface PCB cover.
    • Old Cap/Tube Assembly
      • Broken cap or tube.
      • Broken or loose quick-disconnect fitting on wash buffer container.
    • Empty wash buffer container.
  • Sensor failure on vacuum / pressure sensor interface PCB.
  • Obstruction in vacuum line, t-fitting, or clot filter.
  • Failure in wash tower nozzle:
    • Damaged, obstructed ports.
    • Incorrect (Access) wash tower nozzle installed during PM.
  • Leak or kink in vacuum line or fitting.
  • Weak vacuum pump.
  • Inadequate wash buffer dispense from associated precision pump.
  • Defective or loose cable, connections, or PCB:
    • J1 on Vacuum / Pressure Sensor Interface PCB.
    • J17 on top of Upper Left Card Cage.
    • J2 on DAQC PCB
  1. Select ConfigureSystem SetupSystem Revision.
  2. Verify that the APF revision is correct for the type of wash buffer. The last digit of the assay protocol file (APF) revision matches the wash buffer type. Examples:
    Examples:
    • APF version 1.10.126.2 is Wash Buffer II.
    • APF version 1.10.27.1 is original Wash Buffer.
  3. Inspect the wash tower peristaltic pump for worn, leaking, or flattened tubing. Replace if necessary.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Testing Transfer Pumps During Troubleshooting procedure.
  4. Test the vacuum system.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Testing Vacuum System procedure.
  5. Compare Vacuum Sensors.
Note: For addition information on wash tower external flow, refer to Fluidics: Wash Buffer Confirmation Operating Principles document.

Vacuum Errors

Vacuum Event Log Error Possible Causes Troubleshooting
Sample wash tower vacuum outside limits.
  • Obstruction in vacuum line or t-fitting.
  • Sample pump only: Obstruction in clot filter.
  • Sensor failure on vacuum / pressure sensor interface PCB.
  • Leak in vacuum line or fitting.
  • Weak vacuum pump.
  • Debris in vacuum pump or vacuum line.
  • Compromised wash buffer supply to reagent probe or wash tower.
  • Defective or loose cable, connections, or PCB:
    • J1 on Vacuum / Pressure Sensor Interface PCB.
    • J17 on top of Upper Left Card Cage.
    • J2 on DAQC PCB.
Test the vacuum system.
Note: Refer to Fluidics: Testing Vacuum System During Troubleshooting procedure.
Reagent Pipettor Wash Tower 1 vacuum outside limits.
Reagent Pipettor Wash Tower 2 vacuum outside limits.
Reagent Pipettor Wash Tower 3 vacuum outside limits.
Reagent Pipettor Wash Tower 4 vacuum outside limits.
Wash collar vacuum below limit with no vessel present.

After multiple aspirations without a reaction vessel (empty wash wheel positions), an air gap forms in the aspirate probe. The air gap creates decreased vacuum pressure that can cause the duck-bill valve to close slowly and create this event log message.

  • If this error occurs with wash collar vacuum failure, then troubleshoot using the previous section.
  • If this error occurs alone, then ignore the error. The error is informational only.
Wash collar vacuum failure.
  • Inadequate seal on duck-bill valve.
  • Clogged or damaged Aspirate Probe 1.
  • Leak or kink:
    • Aspirate Probe 1 wash buffer supply line or waste line.
    • Wash collar vacuum waste line.
    • Aspirate probe fitting or wash collar line fitting.
  • Sensor failure on vacuum/pressure sensor interface PCB.
  • Debris in vacuum pump or vacuum lines.
  • Weak vacuum pump.
  • Loose connections at the front or back of the connection panel for Aspirate Probe 1.
  • Loose or damaged tubing connections on the vacuum pump and interface PCB.
  • Worn aspirate peristaltic pump tubing.
  • Dirty Wash Pump Valve 2.
  • Defective or loose cable, connections, or PCB:
    • J1 on vacuum/pressure sensor interface PCB.
    • J17 on top of upper left card cage.
    • J2 on DAQC PCB.
  1. Inspect the duck-bill valve and replace it, if necessary.
  2. Inspect the wash collar manifold. Verify that the threads for the retaining nut that secures the duck-bill valve are not damaged. Check the red bushing inside the manifold for burrs.
  3. Check Aspirate Probe 1 for damage or clogging.
  4. Check for loose connections at the front and back of the connection panel for Aspirate Probe 1.
  5. Check for damaged or loose connections at the red-and-white fitting and the tubing from Wash Pump 2.
  6. Check for a plugged, kinked, or leaking vacuum line from the wash collar to Vacuum Pump 3.
  7. Test the vacuum system.
    Note: Refer to Fluidics: Testing Vacuum System During Troubleshooting procedure.

Wash Buffer Errors

Wash Buffer Event Log Error Possible Causes Troubleshooting

Air detected in the wash buffer supply line.

Wash buffer bubble detector priming failure

Air or excessive restriction has compromised wash buffer delivery

New Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor
  • Broken cap or tube in wash buffer container.
  • Broken or loose quick -connect or disconnect at the Bulk Fluid Tray Interface PCB cover.
  • Broken or kinked sensor tube.
  • Broken holding clips on sensor tube and draw tube.
  • Missing shim on the wash buffer container's neck.
  • Kinked wash buffer supply tubing.
Old Cap/Tube Assembly
  • Broken cap or tube in wash buffer container.
  • Broken or loose quick-disconnect fitting on wash buffer container.
  • Kinked wash buffer supply tubing.
Others
  • Empty wash buffer container or incorrectly calibrated wash buffer drawer.
  • Transfer pump tubing installed directionally incorrect.
  • Failing wash buffer container switching valve.
  • Defective or loose cable or connections to wash buffer container switching valve:
    • Lower cabinet interface PCB: J1, J2, J3, and J4.
    • Wash buffer drawer/bulk fluid trace interface PCB: J1.
  • Defective or loose cable or connections to vacuum pressure sensor:
    • Lower cabinet interface PCB: J1, J2, and J3.
    • DAQC PCB: J1.
  • Failing vacuum pressure sensor on lower cabinet PCB.
  • Cracked bubble trap (clear Lexan on lower cabinet interface PCB).
Troubleshoot Wash Buffer Air and Restriction error messages.
Caution:
  • A successful prime of the wash buffer bubble detector is required to run samples again. Priming with Disable Process Monitoring selected does not count as a successful prime. Sometimes up to five successive bubble detector primes must be performed to clear the error.
  • Both wash buffer containers must have enough wash buffer to prime the bubble trap.

Other Fluidics Errors

Fluidics Event Log Error Possible Causes Troubleshooting

Device reports error.

Event Log Details list:
  • Subsystem: FluidLevelControl.
  • Device: Motor Controller.

This error is not caused by a vacuum issue. The wash buffer transfer pump is not rotating or the sensor is not sensing the rotation at the right frequency.

Check for these failures:
  • Peristaltic pump tubing not seated correctly (can occur during preventative maintenance).
  • Peristaltic pump rotor failure.
  • Peristaltic pump motor failure.
  • Flattened peristaltic pump tubing.
  • A loose or defective electrical connection:
    • Peristaltic and vacuum pump PCB: J1, J3, J15, J17.
    • MCC backplane: J31.
    • MCC 3.
  1. If a PM was just performed, then reseat the peristaltic tubing in the wash buffer transfer pump in the fluidics drawer and initialize.
  2. Troubleshoot the transfer pumps to help further determine the root cause.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Testing Transfer Pumps During Troubleshooting procedure.
Internal pump wash monitor failure during wash dispense.

This error is not caused by a vacuum issue. The pump pressure sensor is sensing an abnormal pressure when dispensing fluid. This pressure sensor is located on the pump immediately behind the red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting.

Refer to this event log error in the Fluidics: Troubleshooting Pumps (Sample/Wash/Precision) procedure.
(Upper, Lower ) Float sensor error message.
  • Buffer tank not filling or defective float sensor.
  • Wash buffer container empty or not connected correctly.
  • Peristaltic pump tube has a hole or is installed directionally incorrect.
  1. Inspect these items and replace if broken:
    • New Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor
      Note: Replace the Cap/Tube Assembly if there are damages to any component on the assembly. Refer to Replacing Wash Buffer Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor procedure for more details.
      • Wash buffer cap or tube.
      • Quick -connect or disconnect fitting at the Bulk Fluid Tray Interface PCB cover.
      • Holding clips on sensor tube and draw tube.
    • Old Cap/Tube Assembly
      • Wash buffer cap or tube.
      • Quick-disconnect fitting on the wash buffer cap.
  2. New Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with pressure sensors .
    • Verify that the front and back wash buffer containers are not empty or leaking. If the wash buffer container is empty, but the user interface indicates the container is >5% full, re-calibrate the wash buffer drawers.
  3. Old Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with weight sensors.
    • Verify that the front and back wash buffer containers are not empty or leaking. If the wash buffer container is empty, but the user interface indicates the container is >10% full, re-calibrate the wash buffer drawers.
    Note: New Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with Pressure Sensors, refer to Calibrating Wash Buffer Supply Pressure Sensors procedure. Old Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with Weight Sensors, refer to the DxI Fluidics: Adjusting and Calibrating Weight Sensors procedure.

  4. Verify float sensor operation (move the float and observe the LEDs on the fluidics PCB).
  5. Inspect the wash buffer transfer peristaltic tubing and replace it if necessary.
  6. Verify that the wash buffer valve can draw buffer from both wash buffer positions.
Waste pump slippage. Liquid waste transfer pump is not rotating or the sensor is not sensing the rotation at the right frequency.
Check for these failures:
  • Peristaltic pump tubing not seated correctly (can occur after preventative maintenance).
  • Pump rotor failure.
  • Motor failure.
  • Loose electrical connection:
    • Peristaltic and vacuum pump PCB: J1, J3, J16, J18.
    • MCC backplane: J31.
    • MCC 3.
  1. If a PM was just performed, then reseat the peristaltic tubing in the liquid waste transfer pump in the fluidics drawer and initialize.
  2. Troubleshoot the transfer pumps to help further determine the root cause.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Testing Transfer Pumps During Troubleshooting procedure.

Troubleshooting Wash Buffer Air and Restriction Error

Error Perform these steps
Restriction detected in the wash buffer supply line Error
Caution: A successful prime of the wash buffer bubble detector is required to run samples again. Priming with Disable Process Monitoring selected does not count as a successful prime.
  1. Select Diagnostics > Prime Fluidics. Select the Wash Buffer Bubble Detector option, and then prime. Repeat this step after any hardware changes or repairs.
  2. Inspect and replace if damaged:
    • New Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor
      Note: Replace the Cap/Tube Assembly if there are damages to any component on the assembly. Refer to Replacing Wash Buffer Cap/Tube Assembly with Pressure Sensor procedure for more details.
      • Wash buffer cap or tube.
      • Quick -connect or disconnect fitting at the Bulk Fluid Tray Interface PCB cover.
      • Broken or kinked sensor tube.
      • Holding clips on sensor tube and draw tube.
    • Old Cap/Tube Assembly
      • Wash buffer cap and tube
      • Quick-disconnect fitting on the wash buffer cap
  3. Verify that the front and rear wash buffer containers are not empty or leaking.
    If the wash buffer container is empty but the user interface indicates that the container is ( >10% full for old drawer or > 5% full for new drawer), then re-calibrate the wash buffer drawers.
    Note: New Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with Pressure Sensors, refer to Calibrating Wash Buffer Supply Pressure Sensors procedure. For Old Wash Buffer Supply Drawer with Weight Sensors, refer to the DxI Fluidics: Adjusting and Calibrating Weight Sensors procedure.

  4. Verify that the wash buffer valve can draw buffer from both wash buffer positions. You may have to prime and observe the wash buffer line with only one buffer container at a time.
    Possible causes of a failure to draw wash buffer include a failed valve or loose or damaged electrical connections or PCBs. If a failure occurs, check the cable and connections to the wash buffer container switching valve:
    • Lower cabinet interface PCB: J1, J2, J3, and J4.
    • Wash buffer drawer/bulk fluid trace interface PCB: J1.
  5. Inspect for leaks, cracks, obstructions, or failures:
    • Wash buffer lines
    • Bubble trap (clear Lexan on the lower cabinet interface PCB).
    • Wash buffer transfer pump tubing
    • Wash buffer transfer pump
  6. Reseat electrical connections and PCBs to the bubble detector sensor:
    • Lower cabinet interface PCB: J1, J2, and J3.
    • DAQC PCB: J1.
  7. If all other troubleshooting fails to resolve the issue, then consider replacing the lower cabinet Interface PCB (contains the bubble detector sensor and vacuum pressure sensor).
