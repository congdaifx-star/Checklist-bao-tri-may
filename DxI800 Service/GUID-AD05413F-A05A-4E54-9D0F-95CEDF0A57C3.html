Troubleshooting Motor Motion Errors in Analytical Module

Purpose

This document provides information for troubleshooting errors that are related to:
  • Incubator wheel
  • Wash carousel
  • Wash arm motion
  • Peristaltic pump

Cautions and Warnings

Warning: The photomultiplier tube (PMT) is a very sensitive photon measuring device. Any background or extraneous light coming into contact with a powered PMT can saturate the PMT and reduce the gain. This reduces the performance of the PMT, and it can take a long time for the PMT to recover or it might never recover fully. A magnetic field close to a powered PMT also reduces the performance of the PMT. The magnetic field causes the electrons to follow a curved path instead of moving in a straight line. When you are performing service in the area of the luminometer, observe the following rules.
  • Never shine a flashlight around the luminometer while the PMT is powered on.
  • Never bring a strong magnetic field close to the luminometer while the luminometer is powered on.
  • Before removing the covers on the analytical module, turn off the power to the PMT.
  • Cap the PMT whenever it is removed from the analytical module.

Causes of Errors

If multiple motors are having slippage, then refer to the information about the device in which slippage occurs first in the Event Log.

Causes of Errors: Wash Carousel

If the wash carousel slippage is caused by Then perform these troubleshooting steps

If the wash wheel slippage errors are intermittent, then search the wash wheel slippage trace files. Refer to the AU: Searching Wash Wheel Slippage Trace Files During Troubleshooting procedure.

If the wash wheel slippage errors are frequent, then run an RV exerciser and observe the wash arm, pick-and-place, and wash wheel movements to further direct troubleshooting.

Physical issues preventing the wash carousel from rotating:
  • Debris or a foreign object in the wash carousel. Examples: Gripper -spinner finger, damaged RV, duck-bill valve, screw, or wash buffer build-up
  • Loose or misplaced analytical module covers
  • Defective idler bearings
  • Interference between the vessel positioner and the wash carousel
  • Wash carousel home flag is misaligned in the z-direction to the home sensor
  1. Verify the analytical module covers are intact and not interfering with the movement of the wash carousel.
  2. Disable the wash carousel motor from Device Diagnostics, and then manually move the wash carousel to detect any rough spots or movement issues.
  3. Verify that the vessel positioner (by the luminometer) is centered and that the wash carousel is not rubbing on the vessel positioner.
  4. Verify the wash carousel tensioner holds the wash carousel in position. Adjust the tension by rotating the thumbscrew on the wash carousel tensioner.
  5. Verify that the wash carousel home flag does not rub or catch on the home sensor. If the teeth are not flush, then:
    1. Verify the wash carousel teeth are flush with the wash carousel motor gear.
    2. Adjust the flag or home sensor position.
    3. Inspect the red idler bearings for any missing o-rings, washers, or damage to the bearings.
      Caution: These parts are very easy to lose in the wash wheel. Do not lose any parts, if removing them.


  6. Inspect the wash carousel slots for any foreign objects. Remove the wash carousel and inspect the wash carousel trough for any foreign objects, wash buffer build-up, or RV dusting.
    Note: RV dusting is an indication of analytical module or pick-and-place alignment issues. Refer to these documents:
    • AU: Aligning Analytical Module
    • P&P: Aligning Pick-and-Place Module
Wash carousel drive motor issues:
  • Defective wash carousel drive motor
  • Incorrectly positioned wash carousel motor drive gear
  • Loose drive gear set-screws
  • Loose wash carousel motor
  1. Confirm that the wash carousel motor drive gear is in sufficient contact with the wash carousel teeth.
  2. Look for loose screws in the wash carousel motor. Secure or replace any loose screws.
  3. Test the wash carousel drive motor for runout and slippage.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Analytical Module Motor Slippage procedure.
Electrical issues:
  • Loose electrical connections
  • Damaged cables
  • Defective MCC 7 PCB
  • Damaged or defective sensor
  1. Reseat MCC 7 or swap MCC 4 and 7. If the issue is a bad MCC, then when MCC 4 and 7 are swapped, the failure moves from the wash carousel to Reagent Pipettor 2 motion errors.
  2. Reseat and inspect these cables (replace if damage is seen or suspected):
    • Home sensor and encoder
    • Analytical Interface PCB: J1, J2, J11, J14, J20
    • MCC backplane: J71, JP71, J2, J3
    • COMC PCB: J1, J2
Pick-and-place issues (incorrectly placing RVs in the wash carousel)
  1. Look for fallen RVs where the wash pick-and-place and incubator pick-and-place intersect with the wash carousel. Fallen RVs indicate alignment or pick-and-place collet issues.
  2. Inspect the pick-and-place collets for damage or build-up on the piston with a flashlight and mirror. Clean or replace the collets.
  3. Verify the pick-and-place alignments to the wash carousel. Refer to these documents:
    • P&P: Adjusting Incubator Pick-and-Place
    • P&P: Aligning Pick-and-Place Module
Corroded or defective encoder
  1. Test the wash carousel encoder.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Encoders During Troubleshooting procedure.
  2. For DxI 800 with serial number less than 600751, verify the hole in the analytical module has been filled with silicone sealant.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Installing Silicone Sealant During Troubleshooting procedure.
RV ejection issues (mechanical issues preventing the wash arm from ejecting RVs):
  • Broken eject plate
  • Defective gripper-spinner
  • Dispense plate alignment causing interference with wash arm and wash carousel movement
  • Loose or wobbly eject plate guide rods, screws, or washers
  • Loose or broken eject plate cylinder mounting tower
  • Incorrect eject plate gap
  • Air leak or loose pneumatic fitting to the eject plate cylinder
  • Low air pressure
  1. Inspect the eject plate. If the eject plate is broken, replace it.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Replacing Eject Plate procedure.
  2. Inspect the gripper-spinner with a flashlight and mirror. Look for damage or build-up on the piston. Clean or replace it.
  3. Perform the analytical module adjustments.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Adjusting Analytical Module procedure.
  4. Verify that the screw and washers that hold the small and large guide rods for the eject plate are secure. These screws are located underneath the dispense plate.

  5. Verify the eject plate adjustment.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Adjusting Eject Plate procedure.
  6. Verify that the two pneumatic connections to the eject plate cylinder are secure and connected to the correct locations. From the front of the instrument, the left air line goes to the upper cylinder (orange connector). The right air line goes to the side connector on the cylinder (red connector).
  7. Verify that the wash arm and eject plate eject the RVs correctly.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Verifying RV Ejection During Troubleshooting procedure.
  8. Replace the spinner-grippers mixers.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Replacing Gripper-Spinner procedure.
  9. Verify the operation of the pressure system. Replace the pneumatic tubing, fittings, or compressor as required.
    Note: Refer to the Pneumatics: Troubleshoot Pressure System procedure.

Causes of Errors: Incubator Wheel

If the incubator wheel slippage is caused by Then perform these troubleshooting steps
Electrical issues:
  • Loose electrical connections
  • Damaged cables
  • Defective MCC 7 PCB
  • Damaged or defective sensor
  1. Reseat MCC 7 or swap MCC 4 and 7. If the issue is a bad MCC, then when MCC 4 and 7 are swapped, the failure moves from the incubator wheel to Reagent Pipettor 2 motion errors.
  2. Reseat and inspect these cables (replace if damage is seen or suspected):
    • Home sensor and encoder
    • Analytical interface PCB: J1, J2, J9, J13, J19
    • MCC backplane: J71, JP71, J2, J3
    • COMC PCB: J1, J2
Incubator wheel drive motor issues:
  • Loose drive gear set-screws
  • Loose incubator motor screws
  • Defective incubator wheel drive motor
  • Drive gear incorrectly positioned to the incubator wheel
  1. Confirm that the incubator wheel motor drive gear is in sufficient contact with the incubator wheel teeth. Adjust the tension by rotating the thumbscrew on the incubator wheel tensioner.
  2. Look for loose screws in the incubator wheel motor. Secure or replace any loose screws.
  3. Test the incubator wheel drive motor for runout and slippage.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Analytical Module Motor Slippage procedure.
Physical issues preventing the incubator wheel from rotating:
  • Foreign object in incubator wheel. Examples: Gripper-spinner finger, duck-bill valve, screw, wash buffer build-up
  • Loose or misplaced analytical module covers
  • Incubator magnetism shield peeling
  • Defective idler bearings
  • Misaligned home flag to home sensor
  1. Verify that the analytical module covers are intact and not interfering with the movement of the incubator wheel.
  2. Disable the incubator wheel motor from Device Diagnostics, and then manually move the incubator wheel to detect any rough spots or movement issues.
  3. Inspect the red idler bearings for any missing o-rings, washers, or damage to the bearings.
    Caution: These parts are very easy to lose in the wash wheel. Do not lose any parts, if removing them.


  4. Verify that the incubator home flag does not rub or catch on the home sensor. If an issue is detected, adjust the flag or home sensor position.
  5. Inspect the incubator wheel for any foreign objects. Remove the incubator wheel and inspect the area for any foreign objects or wash buffer build-up. Inspect the incubator magnetism shield, verifying that the shield is secured completely to the inner surface of the incubator wall. If the shield is damaged, contact Technical Support for a new shield (the part is not available in Oracle).

Corroded or defective encoder Test the incubator wheel encoder.
Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Analytical Module Encoders During Troubleshooting procedure.

Causes of Errors: Aspirate Probe Plate

If the aspirate probe plate slippage is caused by Then perform these troubleshooting steps
Mechanical issues:
  • Loose coupler
  • Bent aspirate probe
  • Interference with analytical module covers
  • Incorrect alignment between the aspirate probe and RV
  • Incorrect mechanical adjustment to the wash carousel
  • Interference above or below the probe plate, or wires
  • Incorrectly routed tubing
  • Dirty lead screw or guide shaft
  • Defective aspirate probe plate drive motor or lead screw
  • Slippage of the aspirate probe plate drive motor
  1. Inspect the aspirate probe pate drive (lead screw) and guide shafts:
    • Clean any dried buffer or debris from the shafts.
    • Do not lubricate the shafts on the redesigned wash arm.
    • Replace any broken shafts.
  2. Secure the coupler.
  3. Replace the aspirate probes
  4. Position the analytical module covers securely.
  5. Perform the analytical module adjustments.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Adjusting Analytical Module procedure.
  6. Perform the analytical module alignments.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Aligning Analytical Module procedure.
  7. Position the wires and tubing so they are clear of moving parts.
  8. Replace the motor or lead screw.
  9. Test for slippage of the aspirate probe plate drive motor.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Analytical Module Motor Slippage procedure.
Electrical issues:
  • Damaged or defective sensor
  • Loose electrical connections
  • Damaged cables
  • Defective MCC 7 PCB
  1. Reseat and inspect these cables (replace if damage is seen or suspected):
    • Home sensor
    • Analytical interface PCB: J2, J12
    • Upper analytical interface PCB: J1, J6
    • MCC backplane: J71, JP71, J72, J3
    • COMC PCB: J1
  2. Reseat MCC 7 or swap MCC 4 and 7. If the issue is a bad MCC, then when MCC 4 and 7 are swapped, the failure is moved from the aspirate plate to Reagent Pipettor 1 motion errors.

Causes of Errors: Dispense Probe Plate

If the dispense probe plate slippage is caused by Then perform these troubleshooting steps
Mechanical issues:
  • Loose or broken coupler
  • Bent aspirate probe
  • Interference with analytical module covers
  • Incorrect mechanical adjustment to wash carousel
  • Interference above or below the probe plate, wires, tubing
  • Dirty lead screw or guide shaft
  • Defective dispense probe plate drive motor or lead screw
  • Slippage of the dispense probe plate drive motor
  • Excessively deep RV engagement alignment
  1. Inspect the dispense probe plate drive (lead screw) and guide shafts:
    • Clean any dried buffer or debris from the shafts.
    • Do not lubricate the shafts on the redesigned wash arm.
    • Replace any broken shafts.
    • Replace any broken couplers.
      Note: Refer to the AU: Replacing Coupler procedure.
  2. Secure the coupler.
  3. Perform the analytical module adjustments.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Adjusting Analytical Module procedure.
    If the adjustment does not prevent the dispense cup alignment tools from moving or lifting, then place washers underneath the dispense arm screws to eliminate any issues where the adjustment falls into place where the screws previously wore a spot in the dispense arm.
  4. Perform the analytical module alignments.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Aligning Analytical Module procedure.
  5. Position the analytical module covers securely.
  6. Position the wires and tubing clear of moving parts.
  7. Replace the motor or lead screw.
  8. Test for motor slippage.
    Note: Refer to the AU: Testing Analytical Module Motor Slippage procedure.
Electrical issues:
  • Damaged or defective sensor
  • Loose electrical connections
  • Damaged cables
  • Defective MCC 7 PCB
  1. Reseat and inspect these cables (replace if damage is seen or suspected):
    • Home sensor
    • Analytical interface PCB: J2, J10
    • Upper analytical interface PCB: J1, J5
    • MCC backplane: J71, JP71, J72, J3
    • COMC PCB: J1
  2. Defective MCC 7 PCB. Reseat MCC 7 or swap MCC 4 and 7. If the issue is a bad MCC, then when MCC 4 and 7 are swapped, the failure is moved from the dispense plate to Reagent Pipettor 1 motion errors.
