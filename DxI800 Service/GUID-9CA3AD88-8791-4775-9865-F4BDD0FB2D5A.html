Installing KNF Compressor

This procedure describes how to install the KNF compressor and brackets when the compressor needs replacing.

Required Materials: Use this table to determine what parts to order for the instrument being serviced.
Instrument Serial Number Actions - When Replacing Part Number to Order and Install
600000 to 600799 Hargraves compressors, install brackets and compressor. A30736
600800 to 601319 Hargraves compressors with new KNF compressor brackets installed in manufacturing. Install compressor. A30735
601320 and above No action required. KNF compressor and brackets previously installed. None
Any instrument that has a new KNF compressor installed If KNF compressor fails, then repair the compressor. See the DxI Parts List for repair kit part number.
  1. Prepare the instrument.
    1. Verify that there is adequate space to access the compressors.
      The compressors are located behind the right rear cover of the instrument.
      Note: For instruments connected to an automation line, it is necessary to separate the instrument from the automation line.
    2. Remove power from the motors by toggling the switch on the Power Distribution PCB labeled "All DC power (except CPU and reagent coolers)."
    3. Open the fluidics and liquid waste drawer to provide extra space to hold the accumulator when replacing the compressor.
    4. Remove the lower right rear cover of the instrument and place it in a location where it is safe from damage.
    5. Lift the intermediate waste container from its location and place it outside of the instrument to allow access inside the compartment.
    6. Lift the blue accumulator from its location and place it behind the bottle support rack in the area created by opening the liquid waste drawer.
      Caution: Take care not to kink or stretch tubing or electrical connections.
  2. Remove the old compressors and brackets.
    1. Remove the five screws that hold the bottle support rack in place, and carefully remove the rack from the instrument.
    2. Remove the two screws securing the lower left metal door mount to the lower cabinet base.
    3. Remove the drip cover from the lower cabinet interface PCB.
    4. Disconnect J8 and J10 connectors from the lower cabinet interface PCB.
      These two connectors are for the existing compressors on the instrument.
    5. Depressurize the system by pressing the red button on top of the accumulator. Hold the button in until all the pressure is released from the accumulator.
    6. Disconnect the air line from the fitting that is attached to the accumulator bottle.
      This airline runs between the compressors and the accumulator. The tubing should be labeled Accumulator and have a Compressor T label on the other end.
    7. Remove the two screws securing the compressor mounting plate and rubber vibration pad to the instrument, and then remove the plate and pad from the instrument.
      If the gasket holds firmly to the wall, pull the gasket off.
  3. Install the KNF compressor brackets.
    1. Place the white L-shaped main bracket for the compressor in the lower left corner of the compartment.
      The screw holes line up with the two holes from the original bottle support rack and the two holes from the door mounting bracket.
    2. To secure the main bracket to the side of the instrument, place the screw in the hole in the main bracket that is closest to the back of the instrument.
    3. Place the door mounting bracket under the L-shaped main bracket, and secure the bracket into place using the two screws and washers.
    4. Install the bottle support rack. Use a socket-head screw to attach the bottle support rack and main bracket to the side of the instrument.
      Use the hole that is closest to the front of the instrument.
    5. Secure the bottle support rack to the frame using the four button-head screws.
  4. Install the KNF compressor.
    1. Locate the rubber pad, and align the rubber pad on top of the main bracket, so that the holes line up.
    2. Place the compressor on top of the rubber pad, so that the holes line up and the pump motor is facing the front of the instrument. Secure the pump into position using the three screws provided.
    3. Working with the end of the tubing that is connected to the clear end of the check valve, insert the tubing into the elbow fitting on the compressor. Route the tubing under the bottle support bracket and up to the accumulator. Rotate the elbow fitting on the compressor so that it is facing down.
      Caution: If the instrument has a bubble trap, do not to damage the bubble trap hardware or tubing when creating a drip-loop in the tubing.
      Note: Routing the tubing in this manner creates a drip-loop and prevents liquid from entering and corroding the compressor.
    4. Place the accumulator and intermediate waste bottle into the bottle support rack, and verify that the tubing is not kinked.
    5. Insert the other end of the tubing into the fitting on the accumulator where the original tubing was. Use caution not to kink the tubing.
    6. Connect the pump wiring connector to J8 on the lower cabinet interface PCB.
    7. Replace the drip cover on the lower cabinet interface PCB.
    8. Close the fluidics and liquid waste drawer.
  5. Restore power to the motors by using the switch on the Power Distribution PCB labeled "All DC power (except CPU and reagent coolers)".
  6. Verify the proper operation of the compressor.
    1. Initialize the instrument, and select Clean out all positions and Remap.
      This exercise of all the pneumatics devices verifies that the compressor is operational.
    2. Verify that the secondary pressure regulator is set to 20 psi.
    3. After initialization is complete, check to see if compressor is running.
      • If the compressor powers on, allow it to pump until it powers off.
      • If the system calls for the compressor to power on, and the compressor does not do so, then there is a major air leak in the tubing or a fitting or the pump is defective or assembled incorrectly. Determine the cause of the failure and repair it. Verify that the compressor obtains maximum pressure and then powers off.
      Note: The pump should power off between 34 psi and 35 psi and power on when the pressure drops below 32 psi.
    4. SelectDiagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Analog Devices > Analog Data and view the Air Compressor value to monitor the system pressure.
    5. Check for air leaks by monitoring the pressure level obtained after initialization for 1 minute. Verify that the pump does not start during that time.
      The compressor must remain off for 1 minute or more between cycles.
      • If the off-time is one minute or more, the compressed air system is not leaking. This check is complete.
      • If the off-time is less than one minute, there is a leak that must be located and repaired. After repairing the leak, repeat the pressure check.
  7. Reinstall the lower right rear cover.
  8. Leave the repair kit taped to the left wall by the KNF compressor.
  9. Perform system verification as stated in the Verification of Service Repair document.
