Power Distribution PCB Operating Principles

This document details the operating principles for the power distribution PCB.

PCB Function

The CPU power distribution PCB regulates or distributes voltages for use in the upper left card cage.

PCB Location

Operational Overview

The CPU power distribution PCB converts the 24 V DC system power into the lower voltages that are needed in this card cage. The voltages are created with two DC-to-DC converters and two switching regulators. The supplies can be checked by viewing the LEDs mounted to the front right of the PCB. The supplies can be checked with a meter also, using the test points located next to the LEDs.

Component Locations

Specific information regarding the components for this PCB is not provided. Service personnel are advised not to change or repair any PCB unless directed to do so by STS Chaska.

PCB Functions and Features

The CPU power distribution PCB includes:
  • DC-to-DC converters and switching regulators
    • DC-to-DC voltage converters are switching regulator circuits that use inductors, transformers, or capacitors as an energy-storing element to transfer energy from input to output in discrete packets. Feedback circuitry regulates the energy transfer to maintain a constant voltage within the load limits of the circuit. Why use a switching regulator? Linear regulators are essentially the only other choice. Linear regulators only step down voltages and efficiency is equivalent to the output voltage divided by the input voltage. On the other hand, switching regulators operate by passing energy in discrete packets over a low-resistance switch, so they can step up, step down, and invert voltages. In addition, they offer higher efficiency than linear regulators.

Interacting Devices

The power distribution PCB interacts with:
  • CPU PCB
  • Communications PCB
  • Data acquisition and control PCB
  • NVRAM PCB
  • CPU backplane PCB
  • Card cage cooling fans
  • Hard disk drive
