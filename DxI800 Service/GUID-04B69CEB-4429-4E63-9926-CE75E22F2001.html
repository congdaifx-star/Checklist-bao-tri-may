Decontaminating Surfaces of Parts and Equipment

This procedure describes how to decontaminate the surface of parts, components, or equipment. This procedure also lists the locations of other documents containing decontamination information and instructions.

Required Materials:
Part Name Quantity P/N
Protective latex gloves 1
Eye protection (that is, safety glasses, goggles) or protective face mask 1
Protective clothing (lab coat, disposable apron, or gown) 1
Decontaminant—If possible use the preferred decontaminate, otherwise substitute one of the alternates listed. 1
Lint-free tissues 2 (for each component)
Zip-lock plastic bags 1 (for each component) 101068
Small biohazard labels 1 (for each component) 101972
Merchandise return label 1
Absorbent pillow 1 (for each component)
Fitting plugs 1 5443A
Prepared Federal Express Air Bill 1
Approved fiberboard box with packaging peanuts 1
Approved packaging tape 1
Decontamination label from the United States Customer Operations Database 2

Decontaminants that can be used for these procedures:

Preferred Decontaminant:
  • 0.5% solution of sodium hypochlorite (prepared by mixing one part household bleach with nine parts water). Use either regular or ultra bleach to make the sodium hypochlorite solution.
Alternate Decontaminants:
  • Zepynamic A decontaminant
  • Refer to local documentation or the Returned Instrument Decontamination Procedure in the United States Customer Operations Database for alternative decontaminants.

Company policy requires that the surfaces of all instruments and parts be decontaminated before packaging the instruments or parts for return.

Warning: All blood products and other Potentially Infectious Materials (PIM) should be treated as though they are infectious and should be decontaminated accordingly.
Warning: Users will be in contact with PIM, contaminated parts or tools, and/or potentially contaminated surfaces during these procedures. Handle and dispose of biohazard materials according to proper laboratory procedures.
Warning: Proper body, hand, eye, and facial protection is required.
Warning: Use bleach only to clean the exterior surfaces of the instrument and instrument parts. Do not use bleach to flush tubing or internal instrument components.
Warning: If Zepynamic A is used to decontaminate the surface, then decontaminate in a well-ventilated area or fume hood.
  1. Decontaminate the parts, components, or instruments.
    1. Remove the component or part using the correct assembly or disassembly procedure.
    2. Thoroughly clean the surface of the component with a lint-free tissue moistened with water.
    3. Apply the decontaminant.
      • If the decontaminant is liquid, then apply it directly to the contaminated surface.
      • If the decontaminant is in spray form, hold the spray 6 to 8 inches from the component and spray the decontaminant onto the component until the surface is thoroughly wet.
    4. Wait 10 minutes. Do not disturb the surface of the component during this time.
    5. Wipe off any residual decontaminant using a dry, lint-free tissue.
  2. Prepare the part or component for shipment.
    1. Cap off the openings of the component (pumps, valves, bottles) using fitting plugs.
    2. Prepare an RGA tag and attach it to the component.
    3. To prevent PIM from leaking into the shipping container, place the component in a primary water-tight receptacle ( zip-lock)plastic bag.
    4. Place an absorbent pillow and the primary water-tight receptacle inside a secondary water-tight receptacle (second zip-lock plastic bag) to create a double water-tight container for shipping.
    5. Attach the small biohazard label to the outside of the larger zip-lock plastic bag.
  3. Repeat the decontamination and the preparing for shipment steps for each component to be shipped.
  4. Complete shipment packaging and documentation.
    1. Place the individually wrapped component inside an approved fiberboard box with packaging peanuts, or equivalent.
    2. Prepare an itemized list of the enclosed components, including a list of all PIM contaminated components.
    3. Prepare two copies of the decontamination label.
    4. Place the itemized list, decontamination label, and listed components inside the fiberboard box.
    5. Seal the fiberboard box with approved packaging tape.
    6. Attach the decontamination label to the outside of the box.
    7. Complete all sections of the returned goods authorization label.
    8. Attach the completed label to the outside of the box, preferably on the top side.
  5. After the part or component is correctly packaged, dispose of all body, hand, eye, and facial protection according to proper laboratory procedures.
