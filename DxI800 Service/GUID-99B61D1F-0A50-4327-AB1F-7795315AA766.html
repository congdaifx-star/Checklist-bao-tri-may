Comparing Vacuum Sensors During Troubleshooting

This procedure describes how to test the vacuum system.

Compare vacuum sensors when troubleshooting the vacuum system. Perform this test across multiple pipettors and compare the vacuum values.

Warning: Keep all body parts away from the wash towers during this test. This procedure causes the pipettor probes to move downward into the wash towers quickly. Contact with this moving assembly can cause severe personal injury.
Compare the vacuum sensors.
  1. Select Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices > Peristaltic and Vacuum Pumps.
  2. Measure the vacuum on reagent pipettor 1/2.
    1. Power on the vacuum pump for reagent pipettor 1/2.
    2. One at a time, power on the peristaltic pumps.
    3. Note the vacuum values displayed for 10 to 20 seconds.
    4. Power off the vacuum pump for reagent pipettor 1/2.
  3. Measure the vacuum on reagent pipettor 3/4 (DxI only).
    1. Power on the vacuum pump for reagent pipettor 3/4.
    2. One at a time, power on the peristaltic pumps.
    3. Note the vacuum values displayed for 10 to 20 seconds.
    4. Power off the vacuum pump for reagent pipettor 3/4.
  4. Determine which of the wash towers, if any, is different from the other wash towers.
    General guidelines for values when operating with:
    • Original Wash Buffer: 50 to 100 mm Hg.
    • Wash Buffer II: 10 to 50 mm Hg.
    Note: The values displayed using this test method can fluctuate widely from 20 to 100.
  5. Power off the vacuum pump using Toggle Off.
  6. Select Done to exit the Peristaltic and Vacuum Pumps screen.
  7. Determine the component that is not functioning properly, and repair or replace the component.
