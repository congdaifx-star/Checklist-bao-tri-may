Troubleshooting Pumps - Sample/Wash/Precision

Purpose

This document provides instructions for troubleshooting issues in these pumps:
  • Sample
  • Wash
  • Precision
Caution: Do not immediately disassemble or rebuild a precision pump when troubleshooting. First perform the troubleshooting steps for the specific pump. Rebuilding a precision pump requires these verifications:
  • Pipettor matching
  • Low volume matching
  • Pressure sensor calibration
  • Assay calibration
  • Dil-factor adjustment

Troubleshooting -Substituting Parts

These components can be substituted or swapped to assist with troubleshooting:
  • Precision pumps
  • Wash pumps
  • Home sensors
  • Pressure sensors
  • Belts
  • Piston and seals
  • Fittings
  • Valves

Failure Modes - Aspiration and Pressure Sensor Errors

Event Log Message or Behavior Explanation Causes Resolution
Aspirate Probe 1 pressure above limit during staged aspirate The pressure sensor for Wash Pump 2 is sensing abnormal pressure during RV aspiration at Aspirate Probe 1 by the peristaltic pump. This pressure sensor is located on the wash buffer reservoir immediately to the right of Wash Pump 2.
  • Aspirate peri-pump tubing obstruction.
  • Aspirate probe obstruction.
  • Pressure sensor failure.
  1. Check the aspirate probe and peri-pump for kinked tubing or obstructions.
  2. Clean or replace the aspirate probe.
  3. Check the aspirate flow rate.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Troubleshoot Peristaltic Pumps procedure.
  4. Prime Dispense Probe 1 and Aspirate Probe 1.
  5. Swap the pressure sensors and recalibrate the pressure sensor on the other pump.
Note: The pressure sensor on Wash Pump 2 does not require calibration.
Aspiration Monitor Detects Possible Obstruction

The pump pressure sensor is sensing abnormal pressure when aspirating or dispensing fluid. This pressure sensor is located on the pump immediately behind the red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting.

QSS is a result flag indicating that insufficient sample was withdrawn from a sample container or vessel.

QSD is a result flag indicating that insufficient sample or reagent volume was dispensed into a vessel or SV.

  • Aspiration monitor detects possible obstruction errors:
    • Occasional patient samples: Poor quality samples with clots or fibrin.
    • Daily Clean or Special Clean only:
      • Citranox dilution incorrect.
      • Contrad or Citranox expired or thickened.
  • Probe obstruction
  • Pump pressure sensor failure
  • Electrical connection loose or defective
  • Pressure sensor calibration inaccurate
  • Fluids path leaking or obstructed:
    • Red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting damaged
    • Probe obstruction
    • Pipettor tubing or connectors kinked or damaged
    • Union fittings cracked/damaged (two used in sample pipettor fluidics path)
    • O-rings missing
      • between pump and fluidics reservoir
      • between pump and pressure sensor fitting.
  • Valve issues:
    • Electrical connections
    • Loose connection to pump
    • Orientation of rotor, stator, and gasket incorrect
    • Dried wash buffer impeding valve movement
    • Valve damaged
  • Idler pulley shattered or missing teeth
  • Upper precision pump components (spring, spring seat, retaining ring) broken or corroded
  • Upper wash buffer reservoir empty. Float sensor stuck to the top of the reservoir (only causes internal pump wash monitor failure errors).
  • Pump belt damaged, worn, or stretched
  • Pump piston or seal binding
  1. View the event log details to determine on which pump the error is occurring.
  2. Refer to the troubleshooting procedures for the pump with the error.
Dispense Monitor Detects Possible Obstruction
QSS or QSD flags on Test Results
Internal pump wash monitor failure during wash dispense The pump pressure sensor is sensing abnormal pressure when dispensing wash buffer into the wash tower. This pressure sensor is located on the pump immediately behind the red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting.
Pressure sensor calibration: The operation failed. Sample or reagent (precision) pipettor curve determination failed.

The pressure sensor curve failed. This pressure sensor is located on the pump immediately behind the red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting.

Wash pump aspiration flush failure

The pressure sensor on Wash Pump 2 is sensing abnormal pressure when dispensing wash buffer to Aspirate Probe 1 (flushing). This pressure sensor is located on the wash buffer reservoir immediately to the right of Wash Pump 2.

Process monitoring filtered baseline out of limits

The pump pressure sensor is sensing abnormal pressure when monitoring the baseline voltage.

Note: The wash pump does not monitor the pressure monitoring baseline.
  • Pressure sensor failure
  • Loose or defective electrical connection
  • Damaged cable between pressure sensor interface PCB and DAQC PCB
  • Pressure sensor interface PCB or DAQC PCB failing.
  1. Calibrate the pressure sensor.
  2. Swap the pressure sensors and then recalibrate both sensors.
  3. Replace the pressure sensor.
Note: If the error is on a sample or precision pump, the pressure sensor will likely work on the wash pump (depending on the severity of the pressure sensor failure).  Perform a  System Check to verify the instrument does not create errors on the wash pump: Wash Pump aspiration flush failure or Aspirate Probe #1 pressure above limit during staged aspirate. Replace the pressure sensor at the first opportunity.

Troubleshooting

If the issue is Perform these troubleshooting steps

Valve Slippage
  1. Clean the valve with deionized water and reassemble.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Cleaning and Maintaining Precision and Wash Valves procedure.
  2. Lubricate the valve cap of the pump.
    Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Lubricating Ceramic Valve Cap procedure.
  3. Verify that the valve home sensor (the home light turns on and off as the valve is rotated) functions in Device Diagnostics.
    Note:
    • If the home light is always on when the valve is rotating, then the root cause is likely a valve home sensor failure.
    • Normal instrument behavior: When the valve home sensor is unplugged (for the precision or wash pump), the valve home light turns on.
  4. Inspect the valve home for:
    • corrosion to the sensor.
    • damage in the wires.
  5. Swap pumps temporarily. After troubleshooting, return the precision pumps to the original locations to avoid having to run pipettor matching, low volume matching, dil-factors, and recalibrating assays.
    • If the issue stays on the original pipettor circuit, then troubleshoot the fluidics lines and electronics for this pipettor circuit. Refer to Cables and PCBs chart to determine which cables and PCBs to reseat.
    • If the issue moves with the pump, then continue to troubleshoot the valve components.
  6. Reseat the MCC PCB, ribbon cables, and power harnesses. Refer to Cables and PCBs chart to determine which cables and PCBs to reseat.
Unexpected Height Errors on Multiple Pipettors
  1. For integrated platforms and DxI on automation lines (LAS), determine whether the samples with issues are being loaded on the UCTA or SPU.
    • For SPU-based errors: Verify the correct tube and rack configuration. From the Main Menu, select Configure > System Setup > Rack ID Setup.
    • For LAS-based errors: Verify that the connection unit has the correct register settings for the type of tube used. For configuration guidance, refer to the final table in LAS: DxI Connection Unit Operating Principles document.
  2. Prime the sample pipettor and inspect the sample probe for signs of gel or obstruction.
  3. Perform the level sense test on the reagent pipettors.
    Note: Refer to the Tests: Performing Ultrasonics Level Sense Test procedure.
    • For SPU-based errors: Refer to the Tests: Performing SPU RF Level Sense Testprocedure.
    • For LAS-based errors: Refer to the Tests: Performing LAS RF Level SenseTest procedure.
    Note: Early level sense by the sample pipettor can cause unexpected volumes in SVs.
  4. Verify the pipettor alignments:
    • Refer to the SP: Aligning Sample Pipettor procedure.
    • Refer to the RP: Aligning Reagent Pipettor procedure.
  5. Verify that the upper wash buffer reservoir contains buffer. A rare failure mode for pressure errors occurs when the float sensor on the upper wash buffer reservoir adheres to the top of the reservoir. If the float sensor is stuck to the top of the reservoir, then clean the float sensor with DI water and relaunch the AU.
  6. Inspect sample probe for damaged or missing components. Replace the sample probe if
    • the Teflon tubing on probe tip is damaged
    • the probe tip is bent
    • there are signs of gel or a clot.
    Note: Refer to the SP: Replacing Sample Probe procedure.
  7. Inspect the sample pipettor tubing and connections for damaged or missing components. Replace any damaged or missing fittings or tubing if
    • the red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting to the pump is damaged.
    • the sample pipettor tubing is damaged.
  8. Verify power supply voltages. Refer to the "Adjust - Power Supply" procedure for details.
    Note:
    • Power supply voltages below 24V can cause intermittent sample pump motion errors. The voltage specification is 24.5 V ±0.1 V with the power distribution switches in the off position.
    • Refer to the Electronics: Adjusting Main Power Supply Voltage procedure.
  9. Reseat the MCC PCB, ribbon cables, and power harnesses. Refer to Cables and PCBs chart to determine which cables and PCBs to reseat.
  10. Remove the sample pump. Inspect the sample pump for damaged or incorrectly installed components.
    1. Inspect the valve. If the valve is loose or damaged, replace it.
    2. Inspect the lower black end-cap.
      • If a torque wrench and the 1-1/16 inch socket tool are available, then secure the lower cap using these specifications. Instruments with:
        • Brown Delrin guide: Torque specification of 12 in-lb (1.4 N•m)
        • Beige or gray guide: Torque specification of 10 in-lb (1.1 N•m)
      • If the tools are not available, secure by hand tightening the lower cap on the bottom of the pump. Then use a wrench to rotate the black lower cap an additional 30° beyond hand tight.

    3. Inspect for belt damage. If any damage is found, replace the belt and clean the pump.
      Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Cleaning Pumps (Precision/Sample/Wash) procedure.
    4. Verify that the belt flag is not tilted and rubbing on the acrylic pump block. If the belt flag is tilted, replace the belt (if damaged). Verify that the home flag does not rotate to the side when securing the screw.
    5. Verify that these o-rings are undamaged and positioned correctly:
      • between the pump and fluidics reservoir
      • between the pump and pressure sensor
      • in the piston and seal.
        Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Cleaning Pumps (Precision/Sample/Wash) procedure for pictures showing the positioning of the o-ring.
    6. Inspect and clean or replace any damaged ribbon cables, power harnesses, or piston seals.
      • If the issues continue on a previous version pump (brown Delrin guide with one or two o-rings), then install the pump reliability components.
        Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Installing Pump Reliability Components (Sample/Precision/Wash) procedure.
      • If parts are not available for the mod or the pump is a new pump (beige or gray guide, two o-rings), then clean the pump.
        Note: Refer to the Fluidics: Cleaning Pumps (Precision/Sample/Wash) procedure.
Unexpected Height Errors on Single Pipettor
  1. Check for a vessel in the carriage by the wash tower.
  2. Check for a damaged or bent pipettor tip or transducer.
  3. Align the reagent pipettor to the wash tower and vessel.
    Note: Refer to the RP: Aligning Reagent Pipettor procedure.
  4. Inspect the reagent pipettor circuit for a fluid leak. For a pipettor tubing leak, disable the pipettor y- and z-motors in Device Diagnostics. Move the pipettor and watch the probe tip for a drip to form or increase in size. Replace any broken parts.
    Inspect the:
    • O-rings in the pump. Replace the missing or damaged o-rings
      • between the pump and fluidics reservoir.
      • between the pump and pressure sensor
    • Red-and-white quick-disconnect fitting to the pump and replace if damaged
    • Union fitting in upper pipettor tubing and replace if damaged.
  5. Inspect the precision pump upper pump components (spring, retaining ring, or spring seal). Replace any broken or damaged parts.
  6. Perform the level sense test on the reagent pipettors.
    • DxI: Refer to the Tests: Performing Ultrasonics Level Sense Test procedure. Troubleshoot any failures.
    • DxI on automation: Refer to the LAS: RF Level Sense and Sampling Problemsprocedure. Troubleshoot any failures.
      Note: The LAS: RF Level Sense and Sampling Problems procedure can be used on non-automation DxIs to detect excessive noise on the sample pipettor RF circuit.

Fluidics Pump Priming and Drawback Calibration Software Details

Parameter Software Details
Slippage
  • Sample Pump: 50 steps.
  • Precision Pump: 100 steps.
  • Wash Pump: 150 steps.
Backlash
  • Sample Pump: 155 steps.
  • Precision Pump: no software specification.
  • Wash Pump: no software specification.
Note:
  • Sample pump backlash is checked during initialization, priming, and the Utility Assay.
  • Precision and wash pumps do not check whether backlash exceeds the software specification. No backlash event log message is reported.
  • Precision pumps correct for these amounts of backlash:
    • High resolution mode (5 µL to 239 µL): up to 100 steps.
    • Low resolution mode (239 µL to 2.24 mL): up to 200 steps.
  • Wash pumps correct up to 200 steps of backlash.
