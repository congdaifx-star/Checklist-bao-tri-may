Troubleshooting Analytical Module Heater

Summary

This document provides troubleshooting and repair information for the analytical module heaters.

Heater Failure Symptoms

Because the heaters and thermistors are all attached to the same housing, one device can affect the behavior of the other devices.

Note: The heater duty cycle is viewed in the Temperature/Voltages screen in Diagnostics.
Symptom Inspect
Temperature readings are slightly above or below the limits.
Note:
  • It is common for temperatures to temporarily overshoot the limits when the instrument is powered on, or when covers are opened and closed.
  • Check the displayed case temperature. Heaters cannot cool.
  • Associated heater or thermistor for misconnection.
  • Heater for disconnected or broken wire.
Duty Cycle of one heater remains abnormally high, and the duty cycle of the adjacent heater is abnormally low (>0.4 on one and <0.1 on the other, for example).
  • Thermistor for poor connection to a thermistor.
  • Thermistor for defective thermistor.
  • Thermistor for interchanged thermistor or connectors.
  • Heater for interchanged heater connectors.
Duty Cycle is abnormally high (1.0), but the heater does not become warm. Associated heater for misconnection, disconnection, or a broken or corroded wire.
Note: The three wash carousel heaters are wired together in series, and the incubator and RV loader heaters are wired together in a series separate from the wash carousel heaters. A single disconnection or broken wire affects all elements in the associated series circuit.
Temperature errors that are widely out of specification or intermittent can post a hardware error event.
Note:
  • <-21 °C = Open thermistor or connection.
  • >50 °C = Shorted thermistor or shorted wire.
Thermistor for poor connection, faulty thermistor, misconnected thermistor, or damaged wire.

Thermistor for poor connections, swap in a spare thermistor or borrow the case temp thermistor. Reseat the Temp/Voltage/Vacuum connector J19 on the backplane. Inspect the I/O PCB connector pins on the backplane.

Wash Carousel Top Heater Failure

Note: The heater in the wash carousel top area is the location of most heater failures causing temperature out of limit errors.
Symptom Inspect
Wires in this area are often corroded or broken. This type of failure is the most common cause of temperature errors (low) relating to the wash carousel. Heater leads attached to the heater pad below the mixer motor.
Note: The heater under the mixer motor area is PN 28-006848A Heater, Wash Carousel Top.
Heater has high resistance with broken or corroded wires Heater resistance. If the heater has high resistance with broken or corroded wires, repair the defective heater connection.

Repairing a defective heater connection

Required Materials (for repairing heater):
  • Soldering iron
  • Electronic-grade rosin core solder
  • Single-edged razor blade
  • Screw driver
  1. Remove the reddish insulating material over the solder connection or at the point where the wires attach to the heater.
  2. Create an area where the wires can be resoldered. Clean the heater connection area and carefully remove circuit pad insulation from the heater area.
  3. Attach the wire.
    • If the wire attachment point has not broken off, then tin the area with fresh solder and resolder the wire to the existing attachment point.
    • If the wire attachment point has corroded so that the wire cannot be reattached, use a small flat-blade screwdriver to scrape off enough of the plastic surface coating to expose a section of the metal heater element where the wire broke off. Apply solder to the exposed element. Confirm adhesion of the solder. Do not overheat the element while soldering. Overheating the element can delaminate it from the backing. Solder the lead to the tinned element.
      Caution: Ensure that the solder connection is secure before proceeding.
  4. Test the solder connection by measuring the heater resistance.
    • If the resistance is 2 to 20 Ohms, the connection is good. Mount the wire to the housing using tape and silicone RTV to prevent corrosion and damage to the solder joint.
    • If the resistance is extremely high or infinite, the heater is defective and requires additional repair or replacement.
      Note: For replacement instructions, refer to the Replacing Heaters in Analytical Module procedure.
