Cleaning and Maintaining Pump Valves (Precision/Wash)

This procedure describes how to maintain the ceramic valves on the precision pumps and wash pumps.
Note:
  • The precision and wash valve gaskets and stators are not interchangeable.
  • When reassembling the valves, confirm that the correct parts are installed.
  • Perform this procedure as part of preventative maintenance or when precision or wash valve problems are suspected.
  1. Disassemble the valve.
    1. Home the valve.
    2. Place a lint-free towel under each dispense probe and the main pipettor to catch any buffer fluid that drains out when the valve is opened.
    3. Remove the valve cap.
    4. Remove the black rubber washer that fits between the cap and rotor.
      Note: The washer can adhere to the cap, when the cap is removed.
    5. Remove the ceramic rotor from the fluidics manifold.
      Note: If the rotor is difficult to remove, push the side of the shaft to loosen the rotor.
    6. If the ceramic stator comes out of the valve when the rotor is removed, separate the rotor and stator.
    7. If the stator remains in the valve when the rotor is removed, remove the stator from the manifold.
      Note: If the stator is difficult to remove, insert the end of the rotor shaft into the center hole of the stator and carefully pry sideways and up. Confirm that the shaft is inserted into the stator before prying the stator loose. If the shaft is not inserted, the stator can chip and become unusable.
    8. Remove the black rubber gasket from inside the valve housing in the fluidics manifold.
      Note: If o-rings are being used in the precision valve, replace the o-rings and the brown Delron o-ring holder with the three-hole rubber gasket.
  2. Clean the valve.
    1. Soak all the removed parts in distilled water. Wipe the parts clean with a lint-free towel.
    2. Clean the fluidics manifold valve housing with distilled water and a lint-free towel.
  3. Inspect the valve.
    1. Inspect all the parts of the valves that were removed and cleaned.
    2. If any of the parts are damaged, worn, or defective, then replace the part.
      Note: If there has been a problem with the valve sticking, then replace the stator. New stators have a frosty surface finish and 12-22 marked on the bottom side (Mod 5980).
    3. Inspect the bearing located inside the cap. If the cap-bearing assembly is rusted or damaged, then replace the cap assembly.
      Note:
      • The valve cap is not part of the valve service kit.
      • If there is resistance removing the rotor from the manifold, inspect for wear on the rotor shaft slot. If wear or damage is evident, further inspection and repair of the pump might be required.
        Note: Refer to the Troubleshooting Fluidics and Fluids Tray Hardware Issues procedure.

  4. Assemble the valve.
    1. Lint or debris interferes with gasket sealing. Confirm that the valve parts and the cavities in the manifold are clean before reassembly.
    2. Install the gasket into the manifold so that the slot in the gasket aligns with the alignment pin and the holes in the gasket align with the holes in the manifold.


    3. Install the ceramic valve stator into the fluidics manifold so that the slot in the stator aligns with the alignment pin and the holes in the stator align with the holes in the manifold.
    4. Install the rotor so that the black line on the rotor faces to the right rear (black dot on manifold) when the valve is in the home position.


    5. Install the black rubber washer on the valve rotor.
    6. Install the cap over the valve parts. Rotate the cap clockwise until the cap is tight.
      Note:
      • Do not overtighten the valve cap.
      • Place the black plastic spacer under the precision valve cap (#2).


  5. Verify the valves are assembled correctly.
    1. Home each valve several times. Confirm that the valve moves and the indicating line on the rotor points to the right rear corner of the instrument or to the black dot on the manifold.
    2. Complete one main pipettor priming cycle and one wash pump prime cycle, confirming that all the air is removed from the precision pump, wash pump, and fluidics manifold.
      Note:
      • Installing a rotor, a gasket, or a stator in an incorrect orientation can produce a pump error during priming because the misalignment blocks the fluid ports.
      • Any small air leak from debris or a poorly seated gasket causes a failure to prime. Often, reassembly of the valve components alone corrects the problem.
  6. Verify system operation.
    1. Perform the required system verification.
      Note: Refer to the Verification of Preventive Maintenance document.
    2. Contact your local support organization with any questions or concerns.
