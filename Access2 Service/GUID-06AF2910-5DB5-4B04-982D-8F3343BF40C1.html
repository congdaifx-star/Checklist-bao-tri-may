Installing Access 2 Instrument

This procedure describes how to install an Access 2 Immunoassay System.

When to Perform the Procedure: Perform this procedure if the Access 2 has never been installed.

Time to Complete the Procedure: The total amount of time required is 4 to 7 hours for hardware installation. For full installations, including hardware, software, configuring the system, calibrating assays and running controls, the total amount of time required is a minimum of 8 hours.

Required and optional parts or tools for installation:
  • Cloth, lint-free
  • Gloves, rubber (without talc)
  • Pipettor interlock switch bypass key
  • Pliers, side cutting
  • Screwdrivers, Phillips and slotted
  • Tool, alignment, feeler gauge (0.010)
  • Tool, alignment, RV and pipettor
  • Tool, alignment, reagent carousel pack
  • A calibrated multimeter -
  • A temperature meter or thermocouple module

Verifying Inventory: Ensure that these materials are available for installing the system. Refer to the document Sales Group Configurations in the Access Systems Applications database for a list of parts and components shipped with each system sales configuration.

Note: Installers outside the U.S. must make adjustments as needed to accommodate the 220 V AC power requirements.

  1. Unpack and place the analytical unit (AU).
    1. Inspect the tilt and shock indicators on the packing box. If either indicator is red, then inspect for damage and determine if the installation can proceed.
    2. Cut the straps attaching the packing box to the pallet.
    3. Pull straight up to remove the box.
    4. Remove any remaining cardboard, Styrofoam padding, or plastic wrap from the instrument.
    5. Review the system site requirements specified in these documents:
      • Space and Environmental Requirements in the Reference Manual in the Applications Database.
      • Presite Qualifications Checklist, Access 2 in the Installation and Deinstallation section of the Access Service Manual.
    6. Prepare the bench area for the AU and the peripherals.
      Caution: The analytical unit weighs approximately 200 pounds (91 kg) and requires at least two people to lift. Do not lift without having prepared an area to receive the instrument.
      • A minimum of 7 feet (213 cm) of bench space to accommodate all components, or 6 feet (183 cm) without the PC.
      • A minimum height of 30 inches (76 cm) above the bench top for access to the interior of the AU from the top.
    7. Confirm that the surface prepared for the AU is flat, stable, and level.
    8. Lift the AU using the indentations located at each corner of the AU base plate.
      Caution: Do not lift the AU using any other portion of the base plate, panels, or covers.
    9. Carefully place the AU on the prepared bench space leaving:
      • 12 inches (30 cm) on the left side of the AU for the fluids tray.
      • 36 inches (91 cm) on the right side for the monitor, printer, and computer.
      • 2 inches (5 cm) behind the AU for the top cover to open.
    10. Keep the packing and the pallet with all packing material until installation is completed successfully, and then discard the material.
  2. Install the fluids tray.
    1. Unscrew the black plug from the substrate fitting on the left side of the instrument, and discard the plug.
    2. Remove the red caps from the fittings on the left side of the instrument and discard them.
    3. Unpack the fluids tray and related components.
    4. Install the Wash Buffer Reservoir Level Sensor to the wash buffer reservoir.
      Note: Steps i and ii may be performed in reverse order.
      1. Use a swab to place a small amount of wash buffer around the inside of the gasket.
      2. Insert the gasket into the opening in the wash buffer reservoir.

      3. Press the float sensor into the gasket until the sensor is flush against the gasket.
        Caution: When pressing the sensor, do not apply direct pressure to the wires.

    5. Route the buffer tube through the tubing clamp inside the fluids tray.
    6. Slide a tubing clamp over each of the fluids tray tubes. Connect the tubes to the bulkhead fittings on the instrument, and then tighten the clamps onto the tubes.
      Note:
      • The waste tubing connections are interchangeable.
      • Wetting the tube helps slide the tube onto the connection.
      • If the site is using a waste drain, install the waste drain tubing. Refer to the Installing External Waste Drain Tubing procedure.
    7. Connect the fluid line from the substrate bottle cap to the substrate fitting on the instrument, and tighten the fitting until a click is heard.
      Note: The CnS substrate fitting design has a fitting lock on the cap. Do not remove it.
    8. Connect the fluid tray sensor cable to the left side of the instrument.
    9. Slide the fluids tray to within approximately 1 inch of the instrument, easing the wires and tubes into the opening on the tray housing.
      Note:
      • Do not pinch or kink the tubing.
      • Place the spare waste bottle and waste air filter bottle with the customer CARE Kit.
    10. Attach a wash buffer dispense nozzle onto a new bottle of wash buffer, invert the buffer bottle, and insert the nozzle into the wash buffer reservoir receptacle.
      Note:
      • Completely peel the foil seal from the bottle so that the nozzle seals well.
      • Load a second bottle of buffer to fill the buffer tank.
  3. Open the instrument front panel and the top cover.
    1. To open the front panel, pull on the bottom of both sides of the panel, and then lift upward.
    2. To release the top cover, loosen the captive screw located above the pipettor gantry tower. Remove and discard the screw.
    3. Close the front panel, and then lift the front of the top cover up and back until the cover rests in a vertical position.
    4. Reopen the front panel.
  4. Remove the packing material from the wash arm and the pipettor.
    1. Manually raise the wash arm and remove the packing foam from between the wash arm and wash carousel housing.
    2. Cut and discard the cable-ties and the blue band securing the main pipettor carriage to the gantry.
    3. Manually rotate the drive gear to move the pipettor downward.
    4. Remove the packing foam from above the ultrasonic transducer.
    5. Remove the tape and wrapping from the pipettor gantry shaft.
    6. Manually move the main pipettor carriage end-to-end across the entire length of the pipettor gantry.
    7. Remove the excess grease from the ends of the upper pipettor gantry shaft and from the bearings on the x- and z-carriage with a lint-free cloth.
      Note: Do not wipe the grease from the entire length of the shaft. Remove excess grease only at the shaft ends and around the carriage bearings.
  5. Install the cassettes and tubing into the peristaltic pump.
    Note:
    • Hold the cassette between the thumb and forefinger as shown in the reference graphic.
    • No tubing is installed in the third cassette from the top of the pump.
    • The cassettes are located in the waste bag area, and are not installed in the peri-pump by manufacturing.
    1. Confirm that the tubing is positioned in the cassette with the retainer clips in the cassette teeth.
    2. Confirm that the black tab on the cassette is on the left and the spring is on the right, when facing the installed pump.
    3. Insert the right side of the cassette into the pump until it is seated fully in the pump.
    4. Rotate the left side of the cassette into the pump until it clicks into position.
    5. Press the center of the cassette to confirm that the cassette is seated fully into the pump.
    6. Repeat, to install the remaining cassettes.
    1Ridged portion on the right side of the cassette - Grasp this portion of the cassette with your forefinger.
    2Spring
    3Area in front of the ridged black tab - Grasp this area of the cassette with your thumb.
    4Middle of the cassette - Press this area to seat and lock the cassette into position on the pump.
  6. Confirm that all the PCBs and connectors are seated correctly in the electronics module.
    1. Loosen the two screws that secure the front of the electronics module cover, and remove the cover.
      Note: If the screws are unscrewed too far, the pipettor can hit the screws.
    2. Place your fingers at the left and right ends of the top edge of each of the circuit boards and press down (side-to-side) firmly.
    3. Confirm that all connectors are seated correctly. For all ribbon cable and individual connectors that are attached to the circuit boards, firmly press them into their receptacles.
  7. Confirm components can move freely and connections are secure.
    1. Manually move the pipettor fully left and right on the gantry and then down and up.
      Note: Manually rotate the large drive pulley located on the left end of the pipettor gantry to lower and raise the pipettor carriage.
    2. Lightly grasp each aspirate probe above the fitting that secures the probe to the wash arm, pull up slightly, and then release.
      The spring-loaded probes should move up and down freely.
    3. Throughout the instrument, confirm that:
      • Tubing fittings are snug but not overtightened. Do not tighten the fitting on top of the substrate probe.
      • Tubes are not twisted or kinked.
      • All electrical connections are secure.
      • No hardware is out-of-place or loose.
      • The analytical module and sample or reagent carousel are positioned and secured in the correct locations.
  8. Assemble the monitor arm.
    1. Remove the monitor arm components from the packaging.
    2. Mount the monitor arm onto the mounting post on the AU, with the tension adjustment screw facing upward.
      Note: Before mounting, place one brass bushing between the arm and the bracket.
    3. Insert the keyboard tray support rods fully into the holes on the mounting bracket, and then tighten the two set-screws.
    4. Attach the monitor mounting plate to the tilt-and-turn mechanism using four (#10-32 x 3/8-in) counter-sink screws.
    5. Remove the monitor stand from the monitor. Attach the monitor mounting plate and tilt-and-turn mechanism to the back of the monitor using the four (M4 x 12 mm) screws removed from the monitor.
    6. Mount the keyboard and monitor mounting bracket onto the arm.
      Note: Before mounting, place one brass bushing between the arm and the bracket.
    7. Mount the monitor and tilt-and-turn mechanism to the post on the keyboard and monitor mounting bracket.
    8. Adjust the tension adjustment screw (3/32 Allen) on the arm, so that the arm height remains stable when using the keyboard.
      Note: Turn the screw counter-clockwise) to increase the tension and raise the arm.
    9. Tighten the set-screws at these locations:
      • Monitor tilt-and-turn mechanism to keyboard and monitor mounting bracket (monitor should not tilt or turn easily).
      • Keyboard and monitor mounting bracket to monitor arm (bracket with keyboard and monitor should still turn).
      • Monitor arm to mounting post (arm should still turn).
  9. Install the printer.
    1. Remove the printer from its packaging. Prepare the printer according to the manufacturers instructions. Then place the printer by its final location.
      Caution: Do not load any manufacturer provided software. Loading unknown software can affect PC operation.
    2. Connect a power cord from the printer to the wall outlet and then power on the printer.
      Note: If an UPS is required by owner, do not connect the laser printer power cord to the UPS. It overloads the UPS and causes a beep when printing.
    3. Connect the USB printer cable to the printer.
      Note: Connect a network cable from network port 3 on the PC to the printer network hub if a network printer will be installed.
  10. Optional: Install UPS.
    Note: Skip this step if an UPS is not required by owner.
    1. Remove the UPS from its packaging and place it by its final location.
    2. Connect the power cord from the UPS to the wall outlet and power on the UPS.
      Note: Connect the UPS internally battery and allow it to charge according to the UPS vendor instructions.
  11. Connect the network hub and cables.
    1. Connect the network hub power cable to the wall outlet and power on the hub.
      Note: If an UPS is required by owner, connect the network hub power cable to the UPS and power on the hub.
    2. Connect a blue network cable from the analytical unit network card to the network hub.
      Note:
      • The network cable connects to the instrument network interface card, which is the card closest to the rear of the analytical unit.
      • A yellow crossover cable is provided for troubleshooting purposes. The crossover cable can be used for a direct connection between the AU and the PC when a network hub is not available. When using a crossover cable, wait for Windows to start on the PC before powering on the instrument. Otherwise, the AU NIC does not initialize correctly and there is no communication.
      • The network port on the instrument CPU PCB is not used.
    3. Connect a blue network cable from the PC network port 1 connector to the network hub.
      Note: If a network hub is not available, the yellow crossover cable is used.
  12. Install the PC.
    1. Remove the PC from its packaging. Then place the PC by its final location.
    2. Connect the power, USB, and video cable connectors to the touchscreen monitor.
    3. Connect the cables to the connectors on the back PC. Refer to the configuration diagrams below.
      Configuration for new Mini PC that replaces the existing console PC.

      Windows 10 PC

      Front

      • External power pack to PC power connector.

      Rear

      1. External CD drive connected to a USB 3.0 connections (4 blue USB ports).
      2. Connect the following to USB 2.0 connections (6 USB ports available-not blue. Use available USB 3.0 port if more than 6 ports are needed):
        • Monitor Touch Cable
        • Printer
        • Mouse
        • Keyboard
        • USB Backup Drive
        • USB Barcode reader
      3. Network cable from Network Hub to Ethernet connection 1.
      4. LIS Serial Port Cable to the COM2 connection.
      5. Video cable to HDMI port connection.
      6. (Optional) RMS cable to ethernet connection 2

      Windows 8 PC

      1. External CD drive connected to a USB 3.0 connections ( 2 blue USB ports).
      2. Connect the following to USB 2.0 connections ( 6 USB ports available-not blue. Use available USB 3.0 port if more than 6 ports are needed):
        • Monitor Touch Cable
        • Printer
        • Mouse
        • Keyboard
        • USB Backup Drive
        • USB Barcode reader
      3. Network cable from Network Hub to Ethernet connection 1.
      4. LIS Serial Port Cable to the COM2 connection.
      5. External power pack to PC power connector.
      6. Video cable to mini port connection.
      Note: Access 2i installation requires specific configuration to prevent communication errors with the DxC or CTA.
      • Connect the DxC serial port cable to the COM2 serial port connector on the Access Mini PC.
      • Connect the CTA serial port cable to the bottom right USB port on the Access Mini PC. The CTA must be configured as COM4.
        • Configuring the CTA port to COM4.
        1. Press Windows key, right click on the Computer icon and select Properties
        2. Select Device Manager, click Ports, and view the available ports.
        3. Unplug the cable for the CTA from the PC and see which port number disappears.
        4. Plug the CTA cable back into the PC and determine the assigned port number for the cable.
        5. If the port is named COM4, right click on the port, select Properties > Port > Settings > Advanced and change the COM Port Number to COM4 (in use) from the dropdown menu.
        6. Select Yes to the message about the port being used by another device.
        7. Reboot the PC and Verify COM4 is still listed under Ports in Device Manager
    4. Connect the PC and video monitor power cords to the wall outlet.
      Note: If an UPS is required by owner, connect the PC and video monitor power cords to the UPS backup power connectors (not the surge-protected-only connectors).
    5. Tighten all the connector hold-down screws, attach the cable-ties, and neatly route the cables.
  13. Power on the system.
    1. Connect the power cord from the Access 2 instrument to the wall outlet.
      Note:
      • Connect the Access2 to a PowerVar isolation transformer is optional. If a PowerVar isolation transformer is required by owner, then connect the power cord from the PowerVar isolation transformer to the wall outlet (not to the UPS used for the PC).
      • The PowerVar power conditioner (for NACO only) is an isolation transformer used to keep instrument ground leakage current within agency limits.
      • Refer to the Access 2 Peripheral Connection Diagram document.
    2. Power on the PowerVar (if it is required by owner and installed), the instrument, the monitor, and the PC.
      Note: In a networked system, power on only the server PC and AU until the server installation is complete. Powering on other PCs and AUs causes a network conflict.
  14. After the PC boots to Windows, calibrate the touchscreen monitor.
    Note: When rebooting the Privacy & Security compliant Windows 8 or Windows 10 PC, you must enter security password "Access2Admin" when prompted to launch Windows.

    Note: Refer to the Peripherals: Calibrating Touch Screen Monitor (Access or DxI) procedure.
  15. Configure the printer. Refer to the Peripherals: Installing LaserJet Printer (DxI and Access2) procedure.
  16. Verify the date and time settings from Windows desktop. If the Console PC's "Date&Time" and "Time Zone" settings are incorrect, customers may lose onboard samples to early expiration time and Access 2 will not get any UI messages. Refer to Peripherals: Verifying the Date and Time Settings from Windows Desktop (DxI and Access2) procedure.
  17. Install the Access 2 system software and APF.
    1. Insert the Access 2 System software CD into the CD-ROM drive of the PC.
      Note: The installation software starts automatically.
      Note: If the software installation fails with "PC and Instrument operating systems do not match. Contact your local service representative for assistance." message, then verify the software CD is compatible with the console PC. Refer to Software Chapter's Release Notes section in the Access Service Manual or the Peripherals Chapter in the Access Service Parts List for more information.
    2. During the software installation, accept the system default choices by selecting [Enter] or the highlighted option.
      Note:
      • A programmed AU requires entry of the correct AU IP address, which is normally 192.168.2.4 or 2.6.
      • The instrument is rebooted during the installation process. Do not remove the CD. The CD can be removed after installation when the PC reboots.
      • If there is a network error at the start of the installation, then refer to the Troubleshooting Network Errors Access 2 procedure.
    3. Remove the CD from the drive when the Setup Complete window displays, and then select the Yes, I want to restart my computer option.
      Note: If installing software on a Mini PC select the PC will automatically reboot. When the UI asks for an APF cd select cancel and proceed to step d.
      Note: When rebooting the Privacy & Security compliant Windows 8 or Windows 10 PC, you must enter security password "Access2Admin" when prompted to launch Windows.

    4. (Mini PC Access 2i Only) Configure the system to enable backups to complete.
      Note: Immediate and scheduled backups will not complete after software version 6.2.3 is loaded until these steps are completed.
      1. Press the Windows key and open the Fast Objects Administrator icon. If the icon is not available on the desktop, type "fast" and then open the program.
      2. Select File > Open Database and type localhost for Host and LXiAccessLocal for the Database.
      3. Select Configuration > Configure Recovery.
      4. Check the box for Enable FastObjects Recovery then select Apply and OK.
      5. Close the FastObjects Administrator program.
      6. Reboot the PC.
        Note: When rebooting the Privacy & Security compliant Windows 8 or Windows 10 PC, you must enter security password "Access2Admin" when prompted to launch Windows.

    5. When the There are no defined assays. Please insert the Assay installation CD message is displayed after the PC reboots, insert and then load the correct APF CD.
      Note: If APF or AAF CD is not available, download APF or AAF files from beckmancoulter.com site or Access Applications Service Manual. Refer to Chapter 10.2.9 Software Download APF Installation Instructions procedure.
      • If installing APF setup.exe file (downloaded from beckmancoulter.com) from USB drive or CD:
        • Navigate to the USB drive or CD drive. Double-click on the file setup.exe.
        • Select Setup to transfer the APF files to your system.

        • From the following window, press [Enter] to return to the Explorer window.

        • Close the Explorer window by selecting the X in the upper right corner.
        • Relaunch the user interface and select OK to install the APF.
      • If installing APF from USB drive (downloaded from the Access Applications Service Manual):
        • For Windows 10 and Windows 8, press Windows key, type regedit and select Registry Editor or regedit.exe to open Registry Editor. For Windows XP, press the Windows key, type regedit and select OK to open Registry Editor.
        • Select HKEY_LOCAL_MACHINE > SOFTWARE > Beckman > NexStep.
        • In the Nexstep folder, right click on APFLoadPath, select Modify, change the value from D: to E: or F:.
        • Relaunch the user interface and select OK to install the APF.
        • Restore the APFLoadPath value from E: or F: to D:, using the Registry Editor.
      • If installing APF from CD (downloaded from Access Applications Service Manual), relaunch the user interface and select OK to install APF.
      • If installing AAF from CD (downloaded from Access Applications Service Manual):

        Update the AAF (DxC600i)

        1. At the DxC console, a window informs you that there is a version mismatch. Select <OK> to close the window.
        2. From the DxC Main Menu, be sure that the DxC is in the Standby mode.
        3. Select the Setup icon to display the System Setup screen.
        4. On the right side of the screen, use the scroll bar and then select <27> Chem Update to display the Chem Update screen.
        5. Under the System heading, select the AAF option. Select <OK> .
        6. The screen prompts you to install a chemistry update diskette into the diskette drive. Open underneath the Access 2 analyzer and insert the AAF CD into DxC computer. Select <OK>.
        7. A Chemistry Update message appears. Select <OK> to accept the update.
        8. When the update is complete, a message appears and confirms that the update was successful. Remove the AAF or APF/AAF from the DxC computer. Select <OK> to close the Chem Update window.
        9. Back up the system parameter information. For more information on performing a system backup, see your system documentation.
        10. Clearly label the backup diskette with the title System Parameters Only. Include the software and chemistry database versions, the instrument serial number, and today’s date on the label. Store the backup diskette in a safe place.
    6. When the user interface starts, select Configure > System Setup.
      Note: Windows 8 may require adjusting the screen resolution to make the user interface appear as a full screen. To Adjust the resolution right click on the desktop and select Screen resolution and select the lowest resolution (1024 x 768).
      1. Change the System ID to the Oracle Instance Number.
      2. Set the Date and Time Formats.
      3. Select the Language (for screens, reports, and online Help).
    7. Select Configure > System Setup > Reports Setup.
      1. Select the printer name from the drop-down list (use the list even if the printer is listed as the selected printer).
      2. Enter the Report Header information (with customer input).
    8. Select Configure > PCAdmin.
      1. Set the Auto Delete.
      2. Set the Scheduled Backup Time.
      3. Run an Immediate Backup.
  18. Confirm the mechanical alignments.
    1. Select Diagnostics > Initialize System > Yes.
      Note:
      • It might be necessary to home the RV shuttle, the incubator belt, and the wash wheel to clear an RV clean-out error during initialization.
      • The wash and precision valves can dry out during shipping and cause valve motion errors during initialization. If these errors occur, then remove the valve cap and crack the rotor loose from the stator. When finished, reinstall the valve cap.
    2. Confirm the main pipettor x- and z- axis alignments.
      Note: Refer to the Pipettor Alignment / Adjustment procedure.
      Note: The manufacturing alignments within the modules do not require adjustment, unless there is an error during testing that indicates an alignment issue.
  19. Decontaminate and prime the system.
    1. Load RVs and a waste bag.
      Note: For Access 2 Software 3.5.0 and Higher - If the waste bag fails to load with the Done button greying out, then
      • Remove the waste bag.
      • Reload the waste bag and ensure it is properly seated.
      • Wait approximately 3 seconds for waste bag Done button to become available, then select Done.
    2. Calibrate the incubator belt and run the vessel holder exerciser.
    3. Decontaminate the substrate system.
      Note: Refer to the Decontaminating Substrate System procedure.
    4. Prime the fluidics and check for leaks. Select:
      • Pipettor: 12.
      • Dispense probes: 12.
  20. Verify the ultrasonic voltages.
    1. Use a thermocouple to verify the ultrasonic transducer temperature is within specification. The transducer temperature specification is 35 °C to 39 °C.
    2. Verify the ultrasonic voltages.
      Note: Refer to theAdjustment: Ultrasonics Transducer procedure.
  21. Calibrate the obstruction detection.
    1. Select Device Diagnostics > Analog Devices > Pressure Monitor > Enable > Determine Curve > Yes. If the curve fails, then troubleshoot obstruction detection.
      Note: Refer to the Troubleshooting Obstruction Detection procedure.
  22. Test the waste level sensor
    1. Fill the waste container with water and place it into the waste container position to verify the UI indicates a full waste container.
  23. Verify vacuum pump operation.
    Note: The air filter bottle can become clogged with dried buffer residue causing slow vacuum increase or limiting maximum vacuum. Replace the air filter if it blocks the air discharge from the vacuum pump.
    1. Power on the instrument and allow the instrument to initialize.
    2. From the Main Menu, select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices.
    3. Select the Pump On and Probe Wash Valve options. Confirm that the vacuum reaches a minimum of 450 mm Hg.
    4. Clear the Pump On option and confirm that the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds with the valve on. If the vacuum drops more than 50 mm Hg in 30 seconds, then inspect the vacuum system for leaks (fittings, vacuum bottle cap, tubing).
    5. With the vacuum valve still closed, power off the vacuum pump and confirm the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds.
    6. Clear the Probe Wash Valve option and confirm that the vacuum immediately falls to almost zero.
    7. Optional - the automated Vacuum Test can also be run. Select F2 Vacuum Test to run the test.
  24. When finished with the instrument installation, install the software and verify alignments on any client systems.
    • In a workgroup, the server PC must remain on with the user interface active during installation of the client system.
    • Each client system must be installed to completion before proceeding to the next client system. (Do not turn on any component of an additional client system until the previous client system has been completely installed.)
  25. Run a System Check.
    1. Run a System Check and verify that ALL System Check specifications are met. If specifications are not met, then troubleshoot System Check results.
      Note:
      • Refer to the Tests: System Check procedure.
      • Refer to the Tshoot: System Check Troubleshooting procedure.
    2. Verify that the drift correction factor (listed as “Drift Corr” on the report) is between 0.9 and 1.1.
    3. Verify that the dark count reading listed on the System Check report is <100 RLUs.
    4. Print the System Check report and the Alignment report and place them in the customer Maintenance and Service Log.
  26. Configure the system, with customer input where applicable.
    Software Function Configure the Following
    Bar Code Reader Setup
    • Set Bar Code Symbology
    • Enable or Disable Bar Code Read of Sample IDs
    LIS Setup
    • Configure the LIS and verify communication, if applicable.
    Tests Setup
    • Enable all customer tests that the customer will use
    • Set the default units and sample type
    QC Setup Set up all controls that the customer will use
    Test Panels Setup Configure any test panels the customer will use
    Derived Results Setup Configure any derived results the customer will use
    Reflex Tests Setup C Configure any reflex tests the customer will use
    System Configuration Worksheets Print configuration setups from their respective menus or fill out the system configuration worksheets, located in the Help System or Reference Manual.
    • System Setup Configurations
    • Tests Configurations
    • Test Panel Configurations
    • Derived Result Configurations
    • Reflex Test Configurations
    Other
    • Place bar code labels on the sample racks. Choose labels corresponding to the customer sample tube requirements.
    • Refer to “Racks and Sample Containers” in the Help System or Operator’s Guide for assistance.
    • Confirm that the Utility assay is enabled. Refer to “Routine Maintenance” in the Help System or Operator’s Guide for assistance.
  27. Evaluate system performance (calibrate assays and run controls).
    Note: If the customer has requested assay verification studies, then skip this section and refer to “Installation Assay Verification Protocol” in Chapter 7: “Installation” in the Access Systems Applications database for instructions.
    1. Configure the calibrators and controls. Use the customer controls. If customer controls are unavailable, use the Lyphochek controls that were shipped with the system.
    2. Program the system to calibrate the assays.
    3. Program ten replicates of each control.
    4. Pipette correct amounts of each calibrator and control into 2-mL sample cups, and place the cups in the correct sample rack positions.
    5. Load the sample racks onto the sample carousel, and select Run to start sample processing.
    6. Observe instrument operation during the run, and confirm that the run completes normally without any abnormal event log messages.
    7. Calculate the mean and %CV for each of the controls. Confirm that the means fall within the acceptable ranges, and that the %CVs are consistent with the "Assay Verification Parameters Quick Reference" in the Applications Database.
    8. Place a copy of each assay calibration report in the Installation Documentation section of the customer’s Installation Implementation Guide, or in the “Assays” section of the customer’s Maintenance and Service Log.
    9. Print a copy of each QC Data report for the customer to review and approve.
  28. Clean up the system and complete the documentation.
    1. Unload all sample racks and any empty reagent packs from the AU.
    2. Perform an immediate backup to confirm that the backup system works. Select Configure > PC Admin > Immediate Backup > Access2Support > OK.
    3. Wipe the exterior of the AU with a moistened cloth and wipe any precipitate caused by leaks or spills (use the cleaner that the lab routinely uses).
      Note: In the absence of a preferred laboratory disinfectant, use a bleach solution containing approximately 500-ppm available chlorine (a 1/100 dilution of standard household bleach in water). Bleach solutions stronger than 500 ppm are corrosive and damage the exterior of the Access 2 instrument.
    4. Ensure that all covers fit snugly, and that all hardware is in position.
    5. Record the system ID on the system identification card and apply the card holder to the front of the AU.
      Note: If multiple systems are networked, place client or server ID cards in the holders.
    6. If the local language is not English, then apply the local language Reagent Pack Caution Label to the reagent load door.
    7. Complete the Access 2 Installation Record. Include the service request number or numbers.
    8. Assemble all system manuals, and then review the completed installation record with the customer. Have the customer sign the installation record in the space provided. Place a copy in the Installation Documentation section of the Installation Implementation Guide.
    9. Complete the Service Request or document the service activity in the appropriate system and place a hard copy of the report or service record in the Service section of the Maintenance and Service Log.
    10. Report any major hardware problems found during the installation to your local support organization.
  29. Following the instrument hardware installation, assay verification will be performed as outlined in the Access 2 Installation Implementation Guide ( 105422D.pdf ), located in the Applications manual.
