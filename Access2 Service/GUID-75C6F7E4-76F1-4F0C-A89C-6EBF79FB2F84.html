Installing External Waste Drain Tubing

Required Materials:
  • B41734 Kit, Waste Tubing Installation: 7240A Qty. 2 (Barb-to-Barb Fitting), 7154A Qty. 1 (25-ft tubing), B41866 Qty. 1 (Tag, A2 Kit Reminder)
This procedure describes how to divert the waste air and waste fluid from the waste bottle to a waste drain. The waste drain tubing can be installed on any Access instrument. Install this modification when a customer wants waste to flow directly into a drain rather than into a waste bottle.
Warning: Service personnel are in contact with potentially infectious materials during this procedure. Handle and dispose of biohazard materials according to correct laboratory procedure. Correct hand, eye, and facial protection is required.
Warning: Sodium azide can react with lead and copper plumbing to form highly explosive metal azides. When disposing of waste fluids, flush them with a large volume of water to prevent azide build-up.
  1. Install the waste drain tubing.
    1. Confirm that the instrument is not running, so that no waste is being discharged.
    2. Prepare two lengths of 1/8-in ID tubing to route from the instrument to the drain.
    3. Remove the waste air and waste fluids tubes from the fitting on the top of the waste bottle cap.
    4. Using a 1/8-in double-barb fitting for each connection, connect one 1/8-in ID pink tube to the waste fluids tube and another 1/8-in ID pink tube to the waste air tube.
      Note: If double-barb fittings are unavailable, then remove the waste bottle tubing from the instrument and connect the pink tubing to those fittings.
    5. Empty the waste bottle and place the bottle back in the fluids tray.
  2. Route the waste drain tubing.
    1. Route the waste drain tubing to a sanitary drain.
      Caution: Back pressure caused by waste draining improperly can cause poor aspiration and assay performance issues. Vacuum under limit errors can indicate an obstruction of the waste air line.
      When installing and routing the waste drain tubing, observe these rules:
      • Avoid dips in the waste drain tubing to prevent fluid from pooling. Pooling can restrict the waste air flow.
      • Avoid sharp bends in the waste drain tubing to prevent the tubing from kinking and blocking the flow of waste fluid.
      • To prevent excess back pressure in the waste system, never route the waste drain tubing higher than the fluid fittings on the instrument.
      • When extending the waste tubing length beyond 10 feet, use caution.
    2. Insert the waste drain tubing into the drain, past the sanitary drain trap, making sure the tubing is not restricted.
      Note:
      • If the tubing is not inserted past the trap, noise and foam might be coming from the drain.
      • If the tubing hangs up on the drain trap, clean out the plug, and then ask for assistance from maintenance with inserting the tubing into the drain.
    3. Inspect the routed waste tubes for kinks or pinching, and then reposition the fluids tray against the instrument.
  3. Prime the fluidics, and confirm that the waste fluid flows smoothly through the waste tubing to the drain.
  4. Verify vacuum pump operation.
    Note: The air filter bottle can become clogged with dried buffer residue causing slow vacuum increase or limiting maximum vacuum. Replace the air filter if it blocks the air discharge from the vacuum pump.
    1. Power on the instrument and allow the instrument to initialize.
    2. From the Main Menu, select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices.
    3. Select the Pump On and Probe Wash Valve options. Confirm that the vacuum reaches a minimum of 450 mm Hg.
    4. Clear the Pump On option and confirm that the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds with the valve on. If the vacuum drops more than 50 mm Hg in 30 seconds, then inspect the vacuum system for leaks (fittings, vacuum bottle cap, tubing).
    5. With the vacuum valve still closed, power off the vacuum pump and confirm the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds.
    6. Clear the Probe Wash Valve option and confirm that the vacuum immediately falls to almost zero.
    7. Optional - the automated Vacuum Test can also be run. Select F2 Vacuum Test to run the test.
  5. Perform the System Check procedure and verify that the results meet specification.
