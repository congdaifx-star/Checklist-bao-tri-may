Troubleshooting - Peri Pump Flow Rate - B Fluidics

This document provides a method for testing the fluid moving capability of the peristaltic pump and its tubing. These tests include RV aspiration and vacuum bottle emptying.

Failure of the pump to properly aspirate an RV will result in high or erratic RLUs.

Failure to keep the vacuum bottle empty can result in fluid being drawn into the vacuum pump which can pump fluid into the waste air filter or damage the vacuum pressure transducer.

RV Aspiration Test

Perform these steps to conduct a flow rate test on any of the lower 3 cassettes and related tubing of the peristaltic pump.

  1. Remove the aspirate probe that is suspected to be contributing to poor aspiration.
  2. Fill an RV with 1000 microliter of wash buffer.
  3. Power on the Peristaltic Pump.
  4. Insert the aspirate probe into the reaction vessel and at the same time start a stopwatch or mark time on a clock.
  5. As soon as the reaction vessel is empty, note the elapsed time.
  6. Check the amount of elapsed time on the stop watch.
  7. The RV should be emptied in less than 10 seconds. If not, troubleshoot the Aspirate 1 system.
  8. Reinstall the aspirate probe. Power off the vacuum pump and Peristaltic Pump and repeat for the remaining aspirate probes.

RV Aspiration Test Troubleshooting

Check these areas to determine the cause of an RV Aspiration Test failure.

  1. At the intake side of the Peristaltic Pump:
    • Check the aspirate probe for partial or complete clogging.
    • Check for smooth operation of the peristaltic pump rollers. Each roller should rotate smoothly and should be clean and free of debris.
    • Check for a loose fitting between the aspirate probe and the Peristaltic Pump tubing.
  2. At the Peristaltic Pump cassette or tubing.
    • Check the cassette and tubing in the cassette of the Peristaltic Pump that pumps fluid from the reaction vessel.
  3. At the output side of the Peristaltic Pump:
    • Check all fittings, ferrules, and tubing including those on the fluids tray, and inspect the waste air filter for defects.

Vacuum Bottle Aspiration Test

Perform these steps to conduct a flow rate test on the upper 2 cassettes and related tubing of the peristaltic pump.

  1. Remove the vacuum bottle cap and fill the bottle approximately 1/4 full of wash buffer.
  2. Replace the vacuum bottle cap.
  3. Disconnect the waste fluid tube at the waste bottle (the tube which carries waste fluid from the bulkhead fitting closest to the rear of the instrument to the waste bottle).
    Note:
    • If a waste drain adapter is being used, remove the fluid tube end that attaches to the ‘T’ fitting.
    • f a waste drain kit is used and there is a splice fitting 3" from the waste fluid outlet on the bulkhead fitting, separate the barb/tubing on the drain side and add an extension tube to the waste fluid out splice.
  4. Place the disconnected end into a small graduated cylinder or glass (this container must have a minimum capacity of 20 ML
  5. Power on the Peristaltic Pump.
  6. Once fluid starts to flow out of the fluid tube from the bulkhead fitting and into the container, mark time with a stopwatch or a clock.
  7. When 10 seconds has elapsed, stop the Peristaltic Pump.
  8. The pass/fail criteria for this test is: In 10 seconds there should be a minimum of 8 ML of accumulated fluid in the test container. If not, troubleshoot the peristaltic pump, cassettes and related tubing.
  9. Reconnect all tubing and other parts that were removed to perform this test.

Vacuum Bottle Aspiration Test Troubleshooting

Check these areas to determine the cause of an RV Aspiration Test failure. If there is less than 8 ML of fluid in the container, a problem exists in one of the following 3 areas:

  1. The intake side of the Peristaltic Pump.
    • Check for loose fittings between the vacuum bottle and the manifold on the left side of the Peristaltic Pump.
  2. The Peristaltic Pump cassette or tubing.
    • Check the two cassettes and tubing on the cassettes of the Peristaltic Pump that pump fluid from the vacuum bottle.
    • Check for smooth operation of the peristaltic pump rollers. Each roller should rotate smoothly and should be clean and free of debris.
  3. The output side of the Peristaltic Pump.
    • Check all fittings, ferrules, and tubing. The tubing may be kinked or clogged.
