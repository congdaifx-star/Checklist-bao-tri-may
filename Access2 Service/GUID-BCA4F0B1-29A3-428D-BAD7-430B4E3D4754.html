Decontaminating Substrate System

This procedure describes how to decontaminate the substrate system.
Note: Before decontaminating the substrate system, confirm that the system is contaminated. Refer to the Confirming Substrate System Contamination procedure. If the system is a new installation or the substrate pump has been replaced or rebuilt, then decontamination is required and confirming contamination is not necessary.

Summary

This document describes when decontamination of the substrate system is necessary.

When required

Decontamination of the substrate system is required when:
  • An Access 2 system is initially installed.
  • A substrate pump is replaced or rebuilt (pump chamber cleaned and new seals installed).
  • Contamination in the substrate system is confirmed.

Symptoms

The symptom commonly associated with substrate contamination is a Relative Light Unit (RLU) mean for the substrate portion of the system check that has trended upward and exceeded the upper specification limit of 9000 RLUs.
Note:
  • The appearance of an orange or red-tinted surface film inside the pump chamber or on the pickup tube in the substrate bottle can be associated with substrate contamination. Wipe off this film before decontaminating.
  • If contamination returns or if excessive priming was required to clear out the Citranox following decontamination, then disassemble and clean out the substrate pump, or replace the substrate pump, substrate bottle cap, and associated tubing.

Other Issues

It is best to confirm that the system is contaminated before decontaminating.

Other issues can seem to be substrate contamination, when they are not:
  • The vent tube from the buffer tank is connected to the substrate bottle vent tube (check-valve) in error. This error can cause elevation of the substrate RLUs.
  • An aspiration failure occurs and the substrate probe dips into an RV that is filled with a reactive solution, contaminating the exterior of the substrate probe. This contamination of the probe can cause a sudden shift from normal substrate system check values to very high values. Wash or replace the substrate probe.
  • Substrate RLUs can vary from lot to lot. The 2003 range was 7030 to 8500 RLU.
  • A low reference LED setting from manufacturing can cause high substrate RLUs at installation and thereafter.
  • A high substrate CV with normal RLUs is usually not a symptom of substrate contamination.

Procedure

  1. Decontaminate the substrate fluidics system.
    1. Open the instrument front cover, and insert a cover interlock switch override key.
    2. Prepare 30 mL of 20% Citranox.
      • Aliquot 24 mL of deionized water into a small container.
      • Add 6 mL of Citranox cleaning solution.
    3. Remove the substrate probe, thoroughly clean the external surface with 20% Citranox, and reinstall the probe.
      Note:
      • If the probe tip is damaged, replace the probe.
      • If this instrument is a new installation, skip this step. The substrate probe does not need to be cleaned.
      • Cleaning contamination off the substrate probe with 20% Citranox, or replacing the substrate probe can resolve a high RLU problem without decontaminating the substrate system.
    4. Wipe down the surfaces of the substrate bottle cap and draw tube using a lint-free tissue and Citranox.
    5. Place the draw tube into the container of Citranox.
    6. From the priming screen, change the substrate prime cycles to ten, and start priming substrate only.
      Note: To improve effectivity of Citranox do not prime out of substrate pump immediately. Allow Citranox to sit in pump for a period of time then move onto step G.
      Note: If this instrument is a new installation, from the Supplies screen load and then scan a new bottle of substrate. This new bottle can be used during the priming steps after the decontamination.
    7. Remove the draw tube from the container of Citranox. Wipe the draw tube and cap dry using a clean lint-free tissue.
    8. Change the prime cycles for the substrate to two and dry prime the substrate only.
  2. Load the new substrate and prime the Citranox out of the system.
    1. From the Supplies screen, load and scan a new bottle of substrate. To save time, select the No prime option.
    2. From the Priming screen, select 20 cycles, followed by an additional ten cycles (the maximum number of cycles allowed is 20), and start priming.
  3. Run System Check.
    1. Run the substrate portion of the system check.
    2. If the substrate portion of the system check has any zero RLUs or any RLUs that rise more than 500 counts from start to finish, then prime the substrate an additional 20 cycles and repeat the substrate portion of the system check.
    3. Repeat the priming and running steps until the substrate RLUs are in range and are not rising more than 500 counts from start to finish.
    4. Run the entire System Check routine and confirm that all specifications are met.
  4. Verify system operation.
    Note:
    • If controls are out of limit, contamination could have affected previous calibration results and recalibration might be necessary.
    • If the DCF (Drift Correction Factor) is not between 0.90 and 1.10, then adjust the luminometer. Refer to the Adjusting Luminometer Settings procedure.
    1. Perform the required system verification.
      Note: Refer to the Verification of Preventive Maintenance document.
    2. Contact your local support organization with any questions or concerns.
