Flushing Vacuum Pump

This procedure describes how to flush the vacuum pump on Access 2 instruments.

Warning: Service personnel are in contact with potentially infectious materials during this procedure. Handle and dispose of biohazard materials according to correct laboratory procedure. Correct hand, eye, and facial protection is required.
Perform this procedure when:
  • Receiving vacuum under limits errors.
  • Vacuum fails to reach 450 mm Hg with the pump and valve on.
  • Performing preventive maintenance.
  1. Prepare the instrument.
    1. Confirm that the instrument is in Ready or Not Ready mode.
    2. Select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices.
    3. Open the top cover of the instrument and locate the vacuum jar.
    4. Remove the fitting labeled Vac. Pump from the jar.
  2. Flush the vacuum pump.
    1. Fill a syringe (without needle) with deionized water.
    2. Enable the vacuum pump by selecting the Pump On option.
    3. Connect the syringe to the vacuum pump tubing and aspirate the deionized water.
      Caution: Avoid holding the syringe close to the luminometer or any PCB.
    4. If the pump does not draw the water, then gently push the syringe to force the water into the vacuum pump tubing.
      Caution: Push gently. Excessive force can damage the vacuum pump.
    5. Repeat until 100 mL of deionized water has been flushed through the vacuum system.
    6. Clear the Pump On option.
    7. Reconnect the vacuum tubing to the vacuum jar.
  3. Verify vacuum pump operation.
    Note: The air filter bottle can become clogged with dried buffer residue causing slow vacuum increase or limiting maximum vacuum. Replace the air filter if it blocks the air discharge from the vacuum pump.
    1. Power on the instrument and allow the instrument to initialize.
    2. From the Main Menu, select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices.
    3. Select the Pump On and Probe Wash Valve options. Confirm that the vacuum reaches a minimum of 450 mm Hg.
    4. Clear the Pump On option and confirm that the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds with the valve on. If the vacuum drops more than 50 mm Hg in 30 seconds, then inspect the vacuum system for leaks (fittings, vacuum bottle cap, tubing).
    5. With the vacuum valve still closed, power off the vacuum pump and confirm the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds.
    6. Clear the Probe Wash Valve option and confirm that the vacuum immediately falls to almost zero.
    7. Optional - the automated Vacuum Test can also be run. Select F2 Vacuum Test to run the test.
  4. If the vacuum does not operate as expected, troubleshoot the fluidics and fluids tray.
    Note: Refer to the Troubleshooting Fluidics and Fluids Tray procedure.
  5. Run controls and confirm that controls meet customer expectations.
  6. Verify system operation.
    1. Perform the required system verification.
      Note: Refer to the Verification of Preventive Maintenance document.
    2. Contact your local support organization with any questions or concerns.
