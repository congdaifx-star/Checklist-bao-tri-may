Peristaltic Pump Troubleshooting

Purpose

This document provides a method for testing the fluid moving capability of the peristaltic pump and associated tubing. These tests include RV aspiration and vacuum bottle emptying.

Failure Modes

Failure of the pump to aspirate an RV can cause high or erratic RLUs.

Failure to keep the vacuum bottle empty can cause fluid to be drawn into the vacuum pump. Fluid in the vacuum pump can pump fluid into the waste air filter or damage the vacuum pressure transducer.

Troubleshooting

Table 66: Troubleshooting Procedures
Procedure Steps
RV Aspiration Test - Flow rate test on any of the lower three cassettes and related tubing.
  1. Remove the aspirate probe that is suspected of contributing to poor aspiration.
  2. Place 1000 µL of wash buffer into an RV.
  3. Power on the peristaltic pump.
  4. Insert the aspirate probe into the reaction vessel and at the same time start a stopwatch or mark time on a clock.
  5. As soon as the reaction vessel is empty, note the time.
    • If all of the wash buffer is aspirated from the reaction vessel in less than 10 seconds, then the minimum flow rate meets the guideline flow rate.
    • If all of the wash buffer is not aspirated from the reaction vessel in less than 10 seconds, then troubleshoot:
      • Aspirate probes and tubing for leaks or clogs.
      • Peristaltic pump tubing for leaks.
      • Peristaltic pump rollers for smooth operation and freedom from debris.
      • Tubing between the aspirate probe and the peristaltic pump for a loose fitting.
      • All fittings, ferrules, and tubing at the output side of the pump and on the fluids tray.
      • Waste air filter for defects.
      • Cassette and tubing in the cassette of the peristaltic pump.
  6. If the flow rate volume is adequate for the probe, then reinstall the aspirate probe into the probe plate. Confirm that the tubing for the probe is routed correctly.
  7. Power off the vacuum pump and peristaltic pump and repeat for the remaining aspirate probes.
Vacuum Bottle Aspiration Test - Flow rate test on the upper two cassettes and related tubing.
  1. Remove the vacuum bottle cap and fill the bottle approximately 1/4 full of wash buffer.
  2. Replace the vacuum bottle cap.
  3. Disconnect the tube that carries waste fluid from the bulkhead fitting closest to the rear of the instrument to the waste bottle.
    Note:
    • If a waste drain adapter is being used, remove the fluid tube end that attaches to the T-fitting.
    • If a waste drain kit is used and there is a splice fitting three inches from the waste fluid outlet, separate the barb and tubing on the drain side and add an extension tube to the splice.
  4. Place the disconnected end into a small graduated cylinder or glass (this container must have a minimum capacity of 20 mL).
  5. Power on the peristaltic pump.
  6. When fluid starts to flow out of the fluid tube from the bulkhead fitting and into the container, mark the time with a stopwatch or a clock.
  7. When 10 seconds has passed, stop the peristaltic pump.
    • In 10 seconds, a minimum of 8 mL of fluid should have accumulated in the test container.
    • If not, then troubleshoot:
      • Fittings between the vacuum bottle and the manifold on the left side of the pump for loose fittings.
      • Two cassettes and tubing on the cassettes of the pump that pump fluid from the vacuum bottle.
      • Pump rollers for smooth operation and freedom from debris.
      • All fittings, ferrules, and tubing on the output side of the pump for kinks or clogs.
  8. Reconnect all tubing and other parts that were removed to perform this test.
