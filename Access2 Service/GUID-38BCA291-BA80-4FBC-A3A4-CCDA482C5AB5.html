Luminometer Theory of Operation

General Description

The luminometer is a photomultiplier tube (PMT) that measures the amount of light produced by the chemiluminescent reaction in the reaction vessels. This measurement is transmitted as an electronic signal to the electronics module for conversion into a calculated result.

The luminometer includes four basic components:
  1. Photomultiplier tube (PMT)
  2. PMT socket
  3. Signal conditioner
  4. High-voltage power supply

The digital pulse-counter is located on the I/O PCB. The luminometer operates in the photon counting mode because of the low light levels involved with chemiluminescence and also because of the robustness of this mode.

Analog and Digital Circuitry Facts

  • The PMT output must be amplified and the threshold detected and prescaled before the I/O PCB can count the output.
  • The tube is operated at approximately 900 volts (anode--cathode).
  • The photocathode is held at a negative voltage and the anode is near ground potential.
  • The exact operating voltage is adjustable to compensate for the wide variations in tube gains (HV Control Voltage).

The analog and digital circuitry requires three voltages:

  • ±5 V DC: Provides low noise power sources for the amplifiers and the analog circuitry.
  • -5.2 V DC: Generated by the I/O PCB and provides the ECL (comparators) power.
  • The output from the comparators is then divided by the prescaler (4).

PMT High-Voltage Power Supply

  • The PMT requires a low noise, low ripple, and highly stable adjustable voltage supply.
  • The high-voltage portion of the supply is potted in a silicone rubber.
  • The remainder of the supply is an open frame design.
  • The power supply is powered from 24 V DC power.
  • The control input is a 0 V DC to +5 V DC signal for setting the output voltage to 0 V DC to -1250 V DC.

Automatic Luminometer Drift Correction

  • The instrument performs a reference LED reading and a crosstalk reading during each 36-second pitch, and then corrects for luminometer drift.
  • These readings are taken from the first empty wash carousel position following the RV that was read in the previous pitch. An RV can occupy only every third wash carousel position.
  • The crosstalk reading includes five 1-second reads. Following crosstalk readings, the reference LED is turned on and three readings are taken.

Crosstalk

Although the crosstalk and LED readings are taken every pitch, they are only checked and used once per rotation of the wash/ read carousel. The readings are checked and used at position 49,50, or 51, or every eleven minutes.

  • If the crosstalk reading exceeds 10,000 counts, then a Crosstalk counts outside limits message is posted into the Event Log.
