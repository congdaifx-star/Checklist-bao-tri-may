Tests: Aspirate Probe Volume Check

This procedure describes how to perform an aspirate probe volume check.

This procedure provides instructions for performing the aspirate probe volume check. The aspirate volume check confirms the ability of the instrument to fully aspirate wash buffer from the RVs.

Perform this procedure if it is suspected that the instrument has:
  • A partially or completely blocked aspirate probe.
  • Incomplete aspiration due to a waste system component failure.
  • Poor washing which is causing outliers or imprecision.

This procedure requires approximately 5 minutes to perform.

Perform the aspirate volume check.
  1. Select Diagnostics > Volume Checks > Aspirate Probes.
    Note: Three RVs in the shuttle are moved to the dispense stations and 500 µL of buffer is dispensed into each RV. The aspirate probes aspirate the buffer and the RVs are returned to the shuttle.
  2. Inspect the three RVs for residual buffer.
    • There should be essentially no fluid left in the RV.
    • When the RV is tipped downward, a small drop of buffer can be present but it should not run more than 1/2 way down the length of the RV.
    Note: The returned vessels are positioned in the shuttle as shown in the table.
  3. Repeat the volume check a minimum of three times to verify the system thoroughly.
    • If the aspirate probe volume checks pass inspection for residual buffer, then the system is working correctly.
    • If the aspirate probe volume checks do not pass inspection for residual buffer, then check for the following:
      • Aspirate probe
        • Partially or completely clogged.
        • Does not move up and down freely by manually lifting and releasing.
        • Tubing is catching on an obstruction when the wash arm descends.
        • Tubing length pulls the probe up when the wash arm descends.
        • Barbed fitting connection above the probe is loose.
        • Bent and not centered in the RV.
      • Wash arm height alignment.
      • Wash arm not level, not mounted flush to the linear slide bracket.
      • Restriction in the waste circuit (external air filter clogged).
      • Peristaltic pump aspirate tubing that is damaged or defective.
      • Peristaltic pump output tubing on the left side of the pump that has not been trimmed to the correct length and is kinked when the cover is closed.
