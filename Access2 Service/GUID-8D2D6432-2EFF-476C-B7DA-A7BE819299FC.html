Safety

This document provides safety information for:

  • Service personnel
  • Customers

Applicability

It is the responsibility of every person who performs service on any Immunoassay instrument/system to be fully aware of and abide by the safety rules established and presented in this document.

Location of Safety Documentation

Because of the volume of safety information that must be relayed to service personnel, all of the safety information is placed into this document and therefore does not need to be placed in every service document.

List of Safety Hazards for DxI

Working on, around or in a DxI 800 instrument involves safety hazards. These consist of:

  • Parts Disposal
  • Customer Safety
  • AC Power
  • DC Power
  • Biological Fluids
  • Moving Parts
  • Covers
  • Module Weight
  • Moving an instrument

Disposal of Parts

Handle and dispose of biohazardous materials according to proper laboratory procedures. Do not place BCI parts or assemblies into a standard trash can.

Customer Safety

Warning: No service person should:
  • leave an interlock bypass key with a customer.
  • leave an interlock switch bypassed on any instrument when leaving an account.
  • encourage customers to operate instruments with any interlock bypassed.

AC Power

The instrument contains 120 Volts AC from the power cord plug in to the instrument power supply.

DC Power

DC Power can be of value as high as 200 VDC. This voltage is dangerous and should be treated with respect.

Biological Fluids

During instrument repairs you may be in contact with potentially infectious materials. Treat all parts within the Access instrument as though they are contaminated with Biological Fluids. Assume that every instrument you work on is contaminated. Proper hand, eye, and facial protection is required whenever performing work on an Access instrument.

Moving Parts - Wire Damage

Wiring / Cabling should never be allowed to contact moving parts. Wires/cable that contact moving parts can eventually become compromised when their insulation fails or breaks down because it becomes:

  • Worn
  • Chaffed
  • Cut
  • Squashed

Any of those failures can result in a wire being shorted to ground or another voltage potential resulting in smoke or in some circumstances fire. All wires / cables must always be properly routed away from moving parts so that they cannot come in contact with them. If harnesses, wires or cables are near cable clamps/harness clamps that are intended to secure them, then these items should be placed in those restraints.

Moving Parts - Pinch Points

nstrument modules / parts do physically move during instrument operation and/or servicing. There exists a risk that a person could be injured by:

These devices could unexpectedly move and cause personal injury. A pinch or crush hazards exists on the:

  • Analytical Module - Wash Arm X and Z motions, Incubator Wheel Rotation, Wash Carousel Rotation, RV Shuttle, Incubator Belt, Mixer Motor/Belt.
  • Sample/Reagent Carousel Module - Rotation of either the Sample Ring or the Reagent Tray.
  • Reagent Pipettor - X or Z motions.

Covers

Always be aware of the location of the top and upper front covers when servicing the instrument. Either can fall and cause personal injury.

Module Weight Limits

Instrument modules may need to be removed from the chassis for service or to allow access to some other area of the instrument.

Warning: If a service personal is unable to safely lift and handle any instrument part in excess of 50 lbs., do not attempt to lift or move the module alone. Ask for and require assistance.

Moving An Instrument

The Access instrument can be safely moved within a lab or from lab to lab when these requirements are met:

  • A transportation cart capable of safely supporting the instrument weight is available.
  • Two people are available to move the instrument to the transportation cart.
  • Two people are available to lift the instrument onto the surface of the cart or the new location for the instrument.
