Optimized Preventive Maintenance Procedure

This document describes how to perform the Access 2 Preventive Maintenance (PM) procedures.

Warning: When servicing equipment, you must recognize the potential for exposure to blood-borne pathogens and apply all Beckman Coulter safety precautions. Use personal protective equipment, and correctly dispose of used parts.

Note:

Test counts can be determined by navigating to the test results screen, filtering for a period of time and multiplying the total tests by the appropriate number to get test counts per year. Sample volumes ≤ 192 tests per week or ≤ 833 tests per month qualify as low volume instruments. Do not choose a time period that is greater than the auto delete settings when calculating test volume or the calculations will not be accurate.

Note: The Low Volume Minor PM is optional for systems that meet the criteria to be considered a low volume instrument. The Low Volume Minor PM can be substituted for the Minor PM on these instruments. A Major PM is required as the next PM performed following a Low Volume Minor PM.

Note:Urethane pump belts [Grey] and matching belt flags (with etched dot) are now provided in Major PM kits replacing the neoprene pump belts [Black]. Discard the old-style belt flags (no dot) as they are not compatible with the new urethane belts. Please reference Technical Bulletin SB_IA2021109 for more details.

Additional Supplies: These are additional supplies that are used during the PM procedure:
  • Grease, Type 2 Lithium (PN 6981C)
  • Swabs, Non-Cotton (PN 104838)

  1. Perform the initial evaluation.
    1. Print the alignment report for future reference.
    2. Review the Event Log for any unusual performance issues. Note any issues to be addressed during the PM.
    3. Review the results of the last several System Checks. If the substrate mean is greater than 8700 RLUs, then refer to the Decontaminating Substrate System for troubleshooting purposes. Note any issues to be addressed during the PM.
      Note: Proactively addressing substrate levels that reach 8700 RLUs helps prevent substrate levels rising above 9000 RLUs between PMs.
    4. Review the QC for any unusual performance issues. Note any issues to be addressed during the PM.
    5. Review the operator logs, if available, and ask the operator to describe any instrument concerns or issues that they have. Note any issues to be addressed during the PM.
  2. Prepare the instrument for PM.
    1. Confirm that all required modifications are completed.
      Note:
      • Refer to Oracle for a list of open MODs for the instrument.
      • If there is time to upgrade the system and there are parts available, then install the parts at the appropriate step in the procedure.
    2. Review the analog data screen temperatures, voltages, and duty cycles. Confirm that the associated temperatures or voltages for each device are within the specified range.
      Zone Temperature Range °C
      Incubator 36.13 to 36.73
      Wash Carousel 36.23 to 36.63
      Substrate 36.23 to 36.63
      Refrigerator 1 to 5
      Note: Investigate any duty cycles that are approaching 1.00, as these duty cycles indicate a possible hardware problem.
    3. Use a thermocouple and confirm that the reagent storage temperature displayed in the UI agrees with the temperature measured by the thermocouple. If the temperature does not agree within 2 °C, replace the reagent storage thermistor.
    4. For a PM Major or Minor only: Unload any onboard reagent packs and place the packs in a refrigerator or cooler.
    5. Note any environmental issues to be addressed with the customer. These issues could include instrument clearances or the temperature of the lab.
      Note: Refer to the "Access 2 Instructions for Use" manual for information on lab temperature requirements.
    6. Remove the top front and bottom front covers, and bypass the interlock switch.
    7. Verify that all cooling fans are operating. Replace these items as needed:
      • Card cage (three)
      • Right side panel
      • Power supply (fan not serviceable, replace power sled)
      • Peltier
    8. Power down the instrument.
    9. Inspect the card cage for excessive dust accumulation.
      Note: Clean the dust out of the card cage, only if necessary. Otherwise, defer cleaning for a subsequent visit.
  3. Service the console PC.
    1. Inspect the PC fan inlet and outlet for blocked or inhibited air flow. Check fan operation as well. Clean if needed.
      Note: The fan inlet on the beige tower DTx PC is located behind the faceplate of the PC. To inspect or clean the inlet, remove the faceplate.
    2. For a Minor and Low Volume Minor PM only: Perform database size maintenance.
      Note: Refer to the Database - Managing Size procedure. Consult with the customer on the appropriate settings for their needs.
  4. Service the sample and reagent carousel module.
    1. For a Major PM Only: Remove the sample and reagent carousel module from the instrument.
    2. For a Major PM Only: Clean the area under the sample and reagent carousel module and remove any dust from the instrument exhaust fan located to the right of the module.
    3. If the reagent storage temperature duty cycle is >0.9, then clean the heat sink and consider replacing the Peltier.
    4. Remove and clean the reagent storage module cover:
      • Clean the underside of the cover.
      • Remove the sliding door on the cover and use a damp lint-free tissue to clean the door. Lift the right end of the door (enough to clear the end-stop) and slide it to the right until it is completely out of the slots.
      • Use a damp lint-free tissue or swab to clean the aspiration slot and sliding door channel.
      • Reinstall the sliding door with the magnet to the left, and confirm that the sliding door operates smoothly.
    5. For a Low Volume Minor PM only: Inspect the reagent storage wheel and interior of the module and clean if needed.
      For a Major and Minor PM only: Remove the reagent storage wheel and clean any residue from the reagent nests. Clean the interior of the reagent storage module.
    6. Reassemble the sample and reagent carousel module
    7. For a Major PM Only: Reinstall the sample and reagent carousel module into the instrument.
  5. Service the analytical module incubator belt and incubator belt pulley bearings.
    1. For a PM Major and PM Minor only: Remove the analytical module from the instrument.
      Note: Refer to the Replacing Analytical Module procedure.
    2. Remove all RVs from the waste chute (remove waste bag).
    3. Remove the plastic module cover and the light shield.
    4. For a PM Major and PM Minor only: Remove and inspect the vessel holders and the incubator belt. Inspect the incubator belt for cuts or cracks. Discard the belt and replace it if the belt is damaged. Do not replace the incubator belt during a PM unless the belt is damaged.
    5. For a PM Major Only: Discard all vessel holders.
    6. Confirm that the incubator belt pulleys rotate smoothly and freely in both directions and that the drive belt and motor are not loose.
      Note: Refer to the Replacing Incubator Belt Bearings procedure.
    7. Inspect the analytical module and identify the locations of any spills or residue including the light shield. Perform corrective action as needed to prevent future leakage.
    8. Clean the incubator track, waste chute, belt sensors, and mixing areas as required.
  6. Service the mixer area components.
    PM Major and Minor only:
    1. Remove the Mixer Motor / Belt tensioner assembly to free the mixer belt from the pulleys.
    2. Remove and discard the Mixer Belt.
    3. Remove the 3 mixer pulleys from the Analytical Module.
      • If performing a Major PM, then discard the mixer pulleys.
      • If performing a Minor PM, then clean the mixer pulleys with deionized water and inspect.
      Note:
      • If the mixer pulleys are difficult to remove or are covered with deposits, then apply deionized water to them before attempting removal.
      • If the plastic lower snap-in bushing does not come out of the module with the pulleys, then thread the long light shield mounting screw into the bushing and lift upward to remove.
    4. Install the 3 mixer pulleys.
    5. Remove the 3 mixer pinch roller assemblies.
      • If performing a Major PM, then discard the pinch roller assemblies.
      • If performing a Minor PM, then clean and inspect the pinch rollers for wear and spring to housing fit.
    6. Install the 3 mixer pinch roller assemblies.
    7. Move the Wash Arm linear slide up and down the lead screw. The movement should be smooth without binding between the nut and the lead screw. Also, the Wash Arm Linear Slide should not be loose (wiggle) when moved.
    8. Remove the two screws holding the linear nut band to the linear slide and then check the lead screw mountings/bearings for play.The lead screw shaft should not move up/down in the bearings more than .010" (0.25 mm). Replace if necessary. Refer to the Wash Arm, Lead Screw Replacement procedure for details.
      Note: Excessive up/down or side to side looseness could indicate bearing failure.
    9. Lightly lubricate the wash arm linear slide with type 2 lithium grease.
      Caution: Do not lubricate the lead screw. The plastic linear nut is self-lubricating.
    10. Replace the mixer belt and install the mixer motor and belt tensioner.
      Note: Confirm that the mixer belt is routed correctly over all the pulleys.
    11. Reattach the linear nut band to the linear slide.
    Low Volume Minor PM only:
    1. Inspect the mixer pulleys and pinch rollers for spills or residue. Clean or replace as needed.
    2. Move the Wash Arm linear slide up and down the lead screw. The movement should be smooth without binding between the nut and the lead screw. Also, the Wash Arm Linear Slide should not be loose (wiggle) when moved.
    3. Lightly lubricate the wash arm linear slide with type 2 lithium grease.
      Caution: Do not lubricate the lead screw. The plastic linear nut is self-lubricating.
    4. Reattach the linear nut band to the linear slide.
  7. Service the Wash-read Carousel and Luminometer.
    PM Major and Minor only:
    1. Remove the wash-read carousel insulating cover, the wash-read carousel cover plate, and the wash-read carousel.
    2. Clean any dried deposits off the wash-read carousel.
      Note: The wash-read carousel can be soaked in hot water to soften deposits.
    3. If performing a Major PM, then discard the wash carousel bearings, o-rings, and e-clips. If performing a Minor PM, then inspect the 6 wash carousel bearings. Replace any defective bearings.
    4. Inspect the luminometer lens. If the lens is dirty, carefully clean it with a non-cotton tipped applicator.
      Note:
      • Do not clean with canned air, it can dislodge the luminometer lens.
      • Protect the luminometer from bright light.
    5. Inspect and clean the RV retainer spring. Replace if damaged, flat, or corroded.
    6. Inspect the insulation surrounding the analytical module. If the foam is not secure, then tighten the Velcro straps. Replace any insulation fasteners that are loose.
    7. Reinstall the wash-read carousel, the wash-read carousel cover plate, and the wash-read carousel insulating cover.
    Low Volume Minor PM only:
    1. Inspect the insulation surrounding the analytical module. If the foam is not secure, then tighten the Velcro straps. Replace any insulation fasteners that are loose.
  8. Install the incubator belt.
    PM Major and Minor only:
    1. Install and tension the incubator belt.
      Note: Refer to the Replacing Incubator Belt procedure.
    2. Install the vessel holders, and the home position vessel holder (with a magnet on top).
      Note:
      • For a PM Major Only: New vessel holders
      • For a PM Minor Only: Same vessel holders
    3. Verify that the belt guides are flush with the incubator belt. Adjust any that are not aligned correctly.
      Caution: Do not reinstall the analytical module. The analytical module is installed in a later step.
  9. Service the fluidics module.
    Caution: Use paper towels to absorb any fluid drips or leaks from the probes and the main pipettor while performing these steps.
    1. Inspect all fluidics tubing and fittings for leaks or damage.
    2. Service the substrate probe.
      • For a Major PM: Replace the substrate probe.
      • For a Minor and Low Volume Minor PM: Inspect and then clean the substrate probe with Citranox. If the substrate probe is kinked or damaged, replace it.
    3. Service the aspirate probes.
      • For a Major or Minor PM only: Remove and replace the aspirate probes.
        Note:
        • Clean the new probes before installing them. Use the aspirate probe brushes to clean inside the replacement aspirate probes.
        • Clean the aspirate probes that were removed and return them to customer stock if they are not defective.
        • PM kits contain only one set of aspirate probes. The customer must order subsequent sets as a consumable. Confirm that the customer has the part number for ordering any needed spares.
        • Inspect the inventory of spare aspirate probes at the customer site, and confirm that the probes are clean. Discard any spare aspirate probes that are bent, worn, or defective (have poor spring action).
      • For a Low Volume Minor PM only: Remove, clean, and reinstall the aspirate probes.
        Note: Inspect the inventory of spare aspirate probes at the customer site and confirm that the probes are clean. Discard any spare aspirate probes that are bent, worn, or defective (have poor spring action) and confirm that the customer has the part number for ordering any needed spares.
    4. Clean the dispense probes with deionized water and a lint-free nylon swab. Inspect the probes, and confirm that they are straight.
    5. Trim to fit and install new peristaltic pump tubing (three small, two large tubes).
      Note:
      • Wipe any debris off the peri-pump rollers and cartridges and put the cartridges back correctly.
      • Refer to the Replacing Persitaltic Pump procedure.
    6. For a Major PM Only: Remove and clean the wash pump, Install a new seal and o-ring (refer to Replacing Wash Pump Seal for details), install a new manifold to pump o-ring, and replace the pump belt.
      Caution: Place the pipettor probe and dispense probes over absorbent material to absorb any draining buffer in these steps.
    7. For a Major or Minor PM only:
      • Remove the precision pump. Clean the pump, inspect the precision pump spring, and install new seals and o-rings.
      • For a Major PM only: replace the pump belt, and install a new manifold to pump o-ring
      Caution: The upper seal is smaller than the lower seal and has a small groove on its circumference.
      Note: Refer to the Replacing Precision Pump Seal procedure.
    8. Disassemble, clean, inspect, and reassemble the wash and precision valves.
      Note: Refer to the Cleaning and Maintaining Pump Valves (Precision/Wash)procedure.
  10. Service the pipettor module.
    1. Inspect the wash tower area for leaks or salt deposits. Clean as required.
    2. For a Major PM only: install a new wash tower nozzle and o-rings.
    3. For a Major or Minor PM only: Remove the main pipettor probe using 5/16-in and 1/4-in open-end wrenches and install and torque the new pipettor probe.
      Note: Do not use the pipettor torque wrench to remove the probe.
    4. Lightly lubricate the linear slide on the pipettor carriage assembly with type 2 lithium grease. Clean the upper and lower crossbars with a lint-free cloth, then apply a light coating of type 2 lithium grease only to the upper crossbar.
    5. Inspect the belt tensions and mounting clips for looseness.
    6. Inspect the service loop and replace it if it is cracked or worn. Confirm that the pipettor assembly moves smoothly through the full range of travel.
    7. Clean the instrument base plate, fluidics manifold area, power sled intake fan, left side wall, and fluidics tray area.
  11. Finish installing components and power on the system.
    1. Install the analytical module if it has been removed.
    2. Mount the wash arm and confirm that all cables and tubes are attached.
    3. Inspect the incubator belt, RV shuttle, and wash-read carousel paths to confirm that none of the paths interfere with another during initialization.
    4. Power on the instrument and initialize the instrument.
      Note: You might need to home the incubator belt, RV shuttle, and wash-read carousel in Diagnostics for initialization to begin.
  12. For a Major or Minor PM only: Verify the pipettor alignments.
    Note: Refer to the Pipettor Alignment / Adjustment procedure.
  13. Calibrate and align the incubator belt.
    1. Calibrate the incubator belt.
      Note: Refer to the Calibrating Incubator Belt procedure.
    2. Align the incubator belt at the home, shuttle transfer, wash-in, and wash-out positions.
      Note: Refer to the Aligning Incubator Belt procedure.
  14. Perform the vessel holder, incubator track, and wash carousel exerciser routines.
    Note: Refer to the Theory of Operation - System Exerciser Routines procedure.
  15. Align the aspirate probes.
    1. Verify that all probes are aligned in the center of the holes in the wash-read carousel housing.
    2. Verify that the aspirate probes move upward approximately 0.070 inches (1.8 mm) when pulled, and spring back down when released.
    3. Verify the pinch roller bracket adjustment and the aspirate probe z-alignment.
      Note: Refer to the Adjusting Wash Arm procedure.
  16. For a Major PM only:Check and adjust the RV sensor voltages.
    1. Clean the RV sensors with a non-cotton applicator and deionized water.
      Note: To access the RV sensors, rotate the slotted shaft in front of the RV shuttle motor to move the walking beam to a location where the sensors are visible.
    2. Check the voltages of the RV sensors at VS1 and VS2 on the analytical interface PCB and adjust as required.
      Note: Refer to the Adjusting RV Sensors procedure.
  17. Verify the ultrasonic transducer operation.
    1. Use a thermocouple to confirm that the ultrasonic transducer temperature is within specification. The transducer temperature specification is 35 °C to 39 °C.
    2. Check and adjust the ultrasonics voltage.
      Note: Refer to the Adjusting Ultrasonics Transducer procedure.
  18. Verify that the mixer speed is 2500 rpm ±20 rpm in the clockwise and counter-clockwise directions.
    Note: Refer to the Adjusting Mixer Motor Speed procedure.
  19. Vacuum System: Inspect all waste bottles and lids at the customer site (not only the in-use bottle).
    1. Order a replacement waste bottle (waste bottle comes with lid and couplings), if any of these conditions apply:
      • A manufacture date is not on the bottle.
      • The bottle has a manufacture date that is more than three years old (date code format on the bottle is mm/yyyy).
      • The bottle is discolored (yellow, orange, pink), deteriorated, cracked, or bulging.
      • The lid or couplings are damaged or deteriorated.
    2. If the glass vacuum bottle contains more than 1/8 inch (3 mm) of fluid, drain the bottle.
      Caution: An 1/8 inch or more of fluid in the glass vacuum bottle can indicate kinked large peri-pump tubing, clogged waste air filter, or restricted waste drain tubing.
  20. Flush the vacuum pump.
    Note: Refer to the Flushing Vacuum Pump procedure.
  21. Verify vacuum pump operation.
    Note: The air filter bottle can become clogged with dried buffer residue causing slow vacuum increase or limiting maximum vacuum. Replace the air filter if it blocks the air discharge from the vacuum pump.
    1. Power on the instrument and allow the instrument to initialize.
    2. From the Main Menu, select Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Digital Devices.
    3. Select the Pump On and Probe Wash Valve options. Confirm that the vacuum reaches a minimum of 450 mm Hg.
    4. Clear the Pump On option and confirm that the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds with the valve on. If the vacuum drops more than 50 mm Hg in 30 seconds, then inspect the vacuum system for leaks (fittings, vacuum bottle cap, tubing).
    5. With the vacuum valve still closed, power off the vacuum pump and confirm the vacuum does not drop by more than 50 mm Hg within 30 seconds.
    6. Clear the Probe Wash Valve option and confirm that the vacuum immediately falls to almost zero.
    7. Optional - the automated Vacuum Test can also be run. Select F2 Vacuum Test to run the test.
  22. If this is an integrated Access instrument, verify the CTA can transfer a vessel to the sample carousel if the sample carousel was removed during the PM.
    1. Load a sample through the CTA and verify the CTA Pick & Place transfers the AV to the Access sample wheel.
    2. Refer to theAligning CTA Pick & Place to Sample Carouselprocedure to align the P&P to the sample wheel if the AV is not successfully transferred.
  23. Verify operation of the dispense and aspirate systems.
    1. Prime the fluidics and check for any leaks.
      Note: Prime Pipettor, Dispense Probes, and Substrate 4 cycles each. If bubbles or air are observed, then perform additional primes.
    2. During the priming sequence, verify that all three dispense probes dispense fluid and all three aspirate probes aspirate fluid.
      Caution: If fluid is not aspirated or dispensed, press [Cancel] and perform corrective action.
    3. Perform the aspirate volume check.
      Caution: During aspiration, verify that each of the three aspirate probes raises up when touching the bottom of the RV and is not pulled upward by the tubing.
      Note: Refer to the Tests: Aspirate Probe Volume Check procedure.
    4. Perform the visual substrate volume check and inspect the three RVs for bubbles.
    5. Perform the visual dispense volume check for each probe and inspect the three RVs for bubbles.
    6. Perform the visual pipettor volume check for 50 µL and 200 µL.
    7. Perform a Service Reset via Diagnostics > Device Diagnostics > Analog Devices > Pressure Monitor. Select Service Reset. Enter Password (Access2Support), Select OK.
      Note: The Service Reset button only resets pack process monitoring values. The Service Reset button does not impact Obstruction Detection features.
    8. Calibrate the obstruction detection feature, if enabled.
  24. Verify operation of the instrument.
    1. Reinstall the light shield, plastic incubator cover, and the instrument covers. Close all covers.
      Caution: Do not damage dispense probe 1 when reinstalling the light shield. The probe can be removed and reinstalled if needed.
    2. Print the alignment and incubator belt calibration reports and leave them with the customer.
    3. Confirm that the cover interlock bypass key is removed from the front cover interlock switch and that the utility assay is enabled.
      Caution: Do not leave the interlock bypass key at the customer site.
    4. For a PM Major or Minor only: Adjust the luminometer HV control voltage so that the current LED reading is within 2% of the target LED.
      Note: Refer to the Adjusting Luminometer Settings procedure
    5. Perform the system check and correct any problems identified.
      Caution: Use original System Check solution. Using HS System Check solution causes Washed %CV failure.
  25. Verify system operation.
    1. Perform the required system verification.
      Note: Refer to the Verification of Preventive Maintenance document.
    2. Contact your local support organization with any questions or concerns.
  26. Finish maintenance tasks.
    1. Reload reagents if they were removed.
    2. Remind the customer that, although the tests verify the repairs made, good laboratory practice indicates all levels of QC be run before reporting patient results. Recalibration might be needed to correct QC shifts following the PM.
    3. Clean the work area and dispose of used parts or materials according to facility guidelines.
    4. Complete the PM Checklist and file any PM documents according to procedure.
    5. Leave a signed copy of the PM Checklist for the instrument maintenance record book if requested.
    6. Discuss any issues addressed during the PM with the customer.
