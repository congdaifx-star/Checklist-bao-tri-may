Troubleshooting -Thermal Hardware/Software

This procedure details the steps to troubleshoot systems with elevated internal case temperatures.

Symptom of Failure

Suppressed results and SYS flags on systems with thermal software installed (version 3.3 or higher) may be due to elevated internal case temperatures. If the internal case temperature reaches 36 C, or outside the range of 18-36, a temperature out of limits event will be posted to the event log. The Maintenance Review screen will also show an out of specification or red temperature.

Background

Why is internal case temperature important now? Thermal software monitors and utilizes internal case temperatures.

Thermal correction will consist of assay specific algorithms released in APF versions. They will utilize instrument temperatures (at time of patient sample processing and at assay calibration) to adjust patient dose values.

Internal testing has shown that an average system will ramp up 3.5 C in temperature from idle to full throughput over a time period of 4 hours. Systems that are near 32 C when idle or resting are at risk of suppressing results when run at maximum throughput.

  1. Verify internal case temperature. Mod 11057 data shows a mean internal case temperature of 26.7 C when systems are idle.
  2. Verify lab ambient temperature. Expected range 18-28 C. Verify other instrumentation in the lab, are internal temperatures also elevated? Assist/educate customer to adjust ambient temperature to acceptable range.
  3. Verify instrument position within lab. Is it in direct sunlight? Is it under a heating or cooling vent? Is it too close to another instrument? Is a PC right next to the left side air intake?
  4. Verify the thermal hardware mod, mod 11057 (or 11175 for A2i systems) was performed. Ensure card cage fan air flow direction. Air must flow from left to right, when facing the instrument, to properly maintain air temperature inside the instrument.
    Note: most elevated temperatures to date have been attributed to fans installed backwards.
  5. Ensure the internal case temperature thermistor is not touching any nearby devices, with the wire lead pointed up. This thermistor is located on the Fluidics Interface PCB on the left side of the instrument, left of the wash and precision pumps.
  6. Check the right side exhaust fan and ensure air movement. Replace if weak or not functioning. Compare air flow to other systems if possible.
  7. If internal case temperature still elevated, replace the third card cage fan, the fan nearest the pipettor gantry not previously replaced during mod 11057. Pipettor gantry removal is necessary.
  8. Check wash and precision pump motor temperatures, taping a temperature lead from a multimeter to the top of the motor and closing the instrument cover. Abnormally warm motors tend to run >32 C. Compare to systems with acceptable internal case temperatures if possible. Consider motor replacement.
  9. Another source of heat comes from the instrument's power sled. The older Magnetek and Astec models tend to run warmer than the current Cosel power supply. If there is a Magnetek or Astec present, consider replacing the power supply.
  10. Escalate unresolved issues to Chaska STS.
