Fluidics and Fluids Tray Troubleshooting

Fluidics

Table 62: Fluidics Hardware Issues
Item Problem Cause and Action
Peristaltic Pump
  • Does not run.
    • Inspect Fluidics PCB (has motor drive circuit)
    • Inspect Power Driver and I/O PCB (provides on/off control signal).
    • Inspect fuses.
  • Runs backwards or runs at incorrect speed.
    • Replace Fluidics PCB.
Precision Pump Errors or Failures
Note: A prec vlv / pmp error is always a pump problem. The valve has a specific error message.
  • Inspect for kinked buffer supply line or an obstruction in the buffer tank (generally fails at the start of the Utility Assay).
  • Valve rotor not oriented correctly (black line to right rear at home).
  • Valve rotor not attached securely to rotor shaft (pin loose).
  • Primary pipettor probe plugged.
  • Obstruction between precision valve and primary probe.
  • Damaged or defective drive motor, gear, belt, or coupler.
  • Broken upper piston return spring (the flat washer goes on top of the piston).
  • Pump seals damaged or worn.
  • Home sensor defective, or home flag jamming on sensor.
  • Defective SMD PCB (on older instruments check for Rev H or higher SMD PCB).
Precision or Wash Valve Errors or Failures
  • Defective, loose, or broken valve drive coupler on motor shaft.
  • Motor seized or corroded because of leaking buffer.
  • Defective home sensor.
  • Stepper motor driver or QSMC PCB failure.
  • Ceramic rotor or stator chipped near one of the fluid ports.
  • Dried buffer on valve rotor, stator surfaces, or outer edges.
  • Valve clogged with debris.
Wash Pump Errors or Failures
  • Home sensor corroded or otherwise damaged.
  • Stepper motor driver or QSMC PCB failure.
  • Defective belt, motor, gear, or coupler.
  • Clogged passage in pump or fluidics manifold.
  • Defective, loose, or broken valve drive coupler on motor shaft.
  • Valve rotor not oriented correctly (black line to right rear at home).
  • Valve rotor not attached securely to rotor shaft (pin loose).
Vacuum or Probe Wash Over limit errors
  • Vacuum sensor misreading vacuum, zero offset.
  • Clog in the T-fitting between the wash tower and the vacuum bottle.
  • Wash tower or valve clogged with a clot.
Under limit errors
  • Wash tower vacuum valve not closing fully (defective valve). Close the valve in digital devices or place your gloved finger directly onto the wash tower nozzle to check the valve.
  • Vacuum pump clogged with dried buffer causing slow vacuum increase. Flush out the residue.
    Note: Refer to the Flushing Vacuum Pump procedure.
  • Vacuum pump not running (pump, fluidics interface PCB, or power driver PCB).
  • Plugged or restricted waste air filter or waste air and fluids line (inspect external drain tube).
  • Incorrect alignment of pipettor probe to wash tower.
  • Vacuum fitting not tightened fully into the wash tower.
  • Intermittent connection of the sensors cable at J19 on the backplane.
  • Vacuum bottle cap or fitting loose, or bottle gasket not seated.
  • Vacuum system tubing leak.
  • Wash tower nozzle not tightened fully into wash tower.
  • Wash tower nozzle ports clogged.

Fluids Tray

Table 63: Fluidics tray hardware issues
Item Inspect for
Fluids Tray Sensor Errors or Failures Cable connector from instrument to tray corroded, open, shorted, or crimped.
Fluids Tray Substrate Area Failures
  • Substrate bottle cap fittings loose (air or insufficient substrate dispensed).
  • Substrate bottle internal draw tube is too short or missing.
  • Valve on the substrate air vent line connected backwards or to the wash buffer vent.
Fluids Tray Reservoir Area Failures Normally, an overflowing buffer tank is caused by a leak in the rubber seal between the bottle cap (called a valve) and the bottle. Air leaks into the bottle and allows fluid to flow out, overfilling the tank. Not allowing the bottle to completely empty before loading a new one can also cause overflow.
Common issues:
  • Wash buffer bottle cap (valve) seal failure.
  • Wash buffer bottle and valve have poor thread match (use valve with black dot).
  • Wash buffer reservoir outlet fitting is loose and leaking (needs Teflon tape to seal threads).
  • Wash buffer reservoir supply tank air vent tubing siphoning fluid (only if tank over fills).
  • Wash buffer reservoir cracked or seam defective.
Table 64: Fluids tray general issues
Area Problem Confirm That:
Buffer Buffer leaking onto countertop
  • Reservoir quick-connect is seated fully into the connector.
  • Threaded filter screen fitting is tight and not leaking around threads.
  • Rubber gasket is in position under the lip of the reservoir receptacle and the receptacle is locked onto the reservoir.
  • Buffer valve is functioning correctly.
  • Reservoir vent tube end is elevated above the level of the reservoir vent fitting.
  • Reservoir is not cracked.
  • Threads of the valve are mated correctly with the buffer bottle threads. A replacement valve might be necessary.
  • Reservoir or filter screen fitting threads are not damaged.
Reservoir is not filling from buffer bottle or software believes it is not filling
  • Reservoir plastic rod is place in the reservoir receptacle.
    Note: The valve on the bottle does not operate without this rod in position.
  • Reservoir level sensor is functioning.
  • Buffer bottle is not empty.
Air in buffer in instrument
  • Buffer supply fitting is tight on the fluidics manifold.
Clean or washed portion of system check has high RLUs
  • The reservoir is not contaminated. If necessary, rinse the buffer reservoir.
    Note: Refer to the Rinsing Fluids Tray Reservoir procedure.
Substrate Air in substrate in instrument
  • The white fittings on the substrate bottle are tight.
    Note: The new design has CnS fittings with a lock to prevent fittings from loosening. Removing the fitting lock can damage the fittings. If necessary, the lock can be removed to inspect the fittings.
  • Draw tube in substrate bottle reaches to bottom of bottle.
  • Bulkhead fitting for substrate line is seated and tight.
Loss of substrate
  • The substrate bottle is not empty and the substrate air intake filter is not clogged.
Substrate leak at manifold block on left side of instrument
  • New Design (tan CnS fittings)
    • Tighten the CnS fitting into manifold until a click is heard. If the fitting continues to leak, replace the substrate cap assembly.
  • Old Design (white fittings)
    • Tighten the white fitting into manifold until it is finger-tight. If the fitting continues to leak, replace the substrate cap assembly.
Waste Fluid Waste bottle overfills

OR

Waste bottle full error in event log, but waste bottle is not full.

  • Both the sensor connector and main harness connector are not corroded.
  • The switch under the bottle tray is functioning.
Waste Air System has outliers in system checks or assays. For standard waste bottle connections:
  • The waste air filter is not wet and there is no accumulation of fluid in the waste air bottle.
  • None of the waste air lines are kinked.
For systems routed to a waste drain:
  • The waste drain line does not route above the level of the waste fluid out fitting.
  • A kink or other flow limitation does not restrict the waste drain line.
