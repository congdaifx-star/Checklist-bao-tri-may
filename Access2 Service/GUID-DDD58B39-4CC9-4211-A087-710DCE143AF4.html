Hardware Verification Following Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF)/As Reported Safety

FSEs are to perform hardware verification after a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) is filed. This may be waived if the customer admits to poor sample quality or operator error as the root cause of the erroneous result. This may also be waived under certain circumstances if the customer is willing to run hardware verification including a system check and precision run. If applicable, document in the Oracle service request why service was waived. If this is not the case, the system hardware must be verified by field service as soon as possible.

Background

When a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) is filed, a field service request categorizes as "As Reported Safety" (ARS) in ServiceMax or "Type 1 CF" in Oracle may be generated by Call Center, and field service needs to assess and verify the system hardware with this procedure. As part of the CF investigation, field service should make reasonable effort to identify root cause during instrument inspection, repair and verification. Document the root cause of a Safety Complaint Level 1 (Type 1 CF) at the lowest level component reasonable to claim, preferably at the individual part number level, or if not possible at the module level (e.g. aspirate probes, mixer pulley, peri pump tubing). If multiple parts are replaced proactively, do not document them as root cause unless there is clear evidence of malfunction. If the root cause is determined to be operator error, then document that in the service request (e.g. wash buffer supply tubing kinked after operator repositioned fluids tray assembly, operator did not seat aspirate probe properly). If no fault is found, or the problem could not be reproduced, the documentation should reflect this conclusion.

In some scenarios, the CF event cause may be apparent and easily resolved over the phone, but the customer may refuse to perform necessary verification steps and request service. CRTS is an acronym used by Call Center meaning "customer refuses trouble shoot" over the phone. In these instances, field service is requested to go on site, or attempt again to troubleshoot with the customer over the phone. Any troubleshooting tasks and resolutions via phone contacts must also be documented in ServiceMax or Oracle. If there is a delay in the FSE eta, the customer may be more willing to assume some of the actions.

Customer Verification

If the erroneous result is from a single sample (e.g. a Troponin or BhCG flier) and the customer repeats the sample two times and the repeats are consistent and believable, the customer may verify the system to further eliminate hardware as source of error.

For example, an initial Troponin result is 0.10 ng/mL. Repeat results of 0.01 and 0.00 ng/mL. Low level QC is acceptable and no other assay performance issues are identified.

Verification consists of a system check, passing QC, and a 15 rep precision run (of the analyte causing the CF, near the dose level of the sample). Call Center will document this verification and attach data in the Oracle service request.

In other cases, the CF event needs to be investigated by field service and instrument hardware evaluated. Continue to Root Cause Assessment section.

Root Cause Assessment

When performing the initial instrument assessment, it is important to have a clear understanding of the failure, the assay characteristics, and then which diagnostic test to run. Apply logical troubleshooting and critical thinking. Use the information in the documents General Assay Troubleshooting and Assay Characteristics and Troubleshooting Sorter in the Applications database to decide assessment actions. Ask for assistance if you are unsure how to proceed. Consider the following:

  • Is the CF event for one assay or multiple assays?
  • Is the CF event based on one sample or multiple samples?
  • Review the event log messages near the time of the result.
  • Is the problem high RLUs or low RLUs? Are the patient results false positive, false negative or general imprecision?
  • What are the assay specific characteristics, use the assay sorter tool (Apps database)
    • sandwich or competitive
    • 1 step or 2 step
    • sample volume pickup
    • dilution assay

Verification Tests

  • Optional: System Check , if verification performed by customer or prior to on site service as requested by Call Center.
  • Mandatory: Tests: HSFoamA2 and HSNoFmA2 using HS system check solution, field service only.
  • As needed: Other diagnostic or verification tests as required: DilTest, carryover, level sense, volume checks, incubator belt calibration, etc.
  • Mandatory: 15 rep precision run of an appropriate level control for the analyte generating the CF, customer or field service. Evaluate results against the performance goals outlined in the “Defining Performance Goals” document in the Access Systems Applications Manual.

Small Volume Sample/Dilution Assays

If the result generating the CF was from an assay with a small volume sample pickup, a dilution assay (Dil-BhCG, Dil-Ferritin, or Dil-AFP) or from an assay that makes an automatic dilution (Myoglobin, Cortisol, AFP, Rubella IgG, etc.) special consideration needs to be taken:

Dilution Assay Result - False High Dilution Assay Result - False Low
  • DilTest procedure to verify dilution pipetting hardware
  • Use vessel exercisers and observe
  • Dilution factor set properly (Applications database)
  • 50 rep precision run to verify reagent aspiration
  • DilTest procedure to verify dilution pipetting hardware
  • Ultrasonics Level Sense Test - Wet/Dry
  • Check transducer voltage
  • Dilution factor set properly (Applications database)

Maintenance and Sample Handling

Other important aspects of system verification is to review maintenance and the customer's sample handling procedures. When a Safety Complaint or CF is filed against an Access product, it is not always a problem with the hardware or reagents, but perhaps other factors. Do not always assume issue with our products. FSEs should review the following:

  • PM schedule - if the instrument is due for PM or due soon, perform PM after initial instrument check for root cause determination.
  • Customer maintenance - verify the customer is cleaning (using clean brushes per the procedure) and replacing aspirate probes appropriately.
  • Sample handling - review the lab's sample tube handling, ensure centrifugation is according to tube manufacturer's recommendations. Reference the AccuTnI Support Portfolio >> Preanalytical Factors >> Centrifugation section in the Applications database.

Documentation

Attach all data to the service request in ServiceMax, Oracle or to the CF, or e-mail the documentation to cfcomplaints@beckman.com. Documentation to include:

  • A copy of the service request.
  • A copy of the event log .
  • Archdata file - select Diagnostics >> Copy to Disk. Enter appropriate start date. Select OK.
  • HS system check data, preferably the graphed results.
  • Precision data, if applicable.
