Troubleshooting Reagent Carousel Thermistor Failure

Perform these steps to troubleshoot a possible failure of the Reagent Carousel Thermistor.

The Reagent Carousel thermistor can fail showing one of three symptoms.

  • Temperature error indicating a temperature slightly higher than the actual temperature. - Partially shorted or otherwise defective thermistor.
  • Temperature error indicating a temperature of +50 to +60 degrees Centigrade - Thermistor shorted.
  • Temperature error indicating a temperature of -20 to -27 degrees Centigrade. - Thermistor open. Open circuit. Possible connector unplugged.
  1. View the Reagent temperature and duty cycle displayed by the User Interface.
    • If the temperature is slightly high out of range with a duty cycle of 1.00, then confirm the thermistor accuracy using a temperature probe. This may indicate a partially open thermistor.
    • A slightly high out of range temperature (confirmed to be accurate) with a duty cycle of 1.00 may be caused by high room temperature, a weak Peltier, failing fans, or dust obstruction of the air intakes or heat sink.
    • A short (+50C) or open (-20C) reading, with duty cycle of 0.00 indicates a thermistor hardware error.
  2. To troubleshoot a hardware error temporarily connect a replacement thermistor to connector J15 (sensor in) on the Reagent Carousel Interface PCB.
  3. If the displayed temperature changes from a short (+50C) or open (-20C) to a reading in the +25 degree range (ambient temperature), then the Reagent Carousel Thermistor is defective. Proceed to the document "Replacing the Reagent Carousel Thermistor". Replace the thermistor using only a sealed reagent carousel thermistor part number 973023.
    Note: The 14 inch universal thermistor can be used temporarily but will fail prematurely after installation due to not being sealed against condensing moisture.
  4. If the temporarily connected thermistor does not affect the displayed temperature, then continue troubleshooting by reseating J16 (sensor out) on the Reagent Carousel Interface PCB and J19 (analog inputs) on the Backplane PCB.
  5. The Reagent Temperature on the Backplane connects to J19 pins 4 and 5 and then to the I/O PCB pins at J17 pins B3 and C3.
  6. Also try cleaning dust out of the card cage and reseating the I/O PCB.
